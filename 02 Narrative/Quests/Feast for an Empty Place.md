# Feast for an Empty Place

**Role:** optional homecoming arc; changes in an ordinary relationship, a modest preference worth preserving, and a tonal comparison scene. **Place:** Rocca Selva's Bell Court, Chestnut Steps, and common cistern. **People:** Mara Vezzi, Tavio if his route permits, and village neighbors. **Creatures:** the protagonist's companions, a local Thimbleback, and visiting Bellwethers.

**Prerequisite:** finish or hand off [Bells Before Breakfast](Bells%20Before%20Breakfast.md) and return after meaningful contact with at least one wider community. The stronger anomalous version requires an independently witnessed regional discrepancy. **Completion:** the village holds its feast under a declared provisioning arrangement, and a small disagreement can be expressed without being silently replaced.

## Home has continued living

Someone has repaired the stair the protagonist always stepped over. A neighbor's child has learned to whistle badly. Mara has moved her worktable because her shoulder hurts in the old light. The protagonist's absence mattered without freezing the village in place.

Preparations for a seasonal feast fill the Bell Court. City aid has made the work easier: a borrowed provisioning accord predicts quantities, reduces waste, and grows suitable starter cultures in sealed earthen dishes. It has saved the village effort and helped a household that struggled to contribute last year. Nobody was foolish to accept it.

The protagonist finds a place set with a shallow blue bowl. Nobody can immediately explain why it is needed. It belongs to a sour chestnut dish that a few people enjoy and most tolerate affectionately. The question is small enough to be laughable. That is why the moment matters.

## Two layers of the quest

In an **early, lightly uncanny visit**, this is a social mystery. A recipe was misfiled, the usual cook is away, and people disagree about whose idea the dish was. The player can reconstruct it and help the feast. There is no mandatory supernatural reveal before sufficient context.

In a **later, evidence-informed visit**, several neighbors can recall eating the dish but have trouble explaining why anyone might want it again while discussing the provisioning plan. Outside the bellhouse, one person says simply, “I like it.” Inside, their explanation becomes, “It is useful for remembering old recipes.” The harmless personal preference keeps being rewritten as an approved function.

The anomaly belongs to one process and one set of interactions. Affection, childhood, and every shared memory are not revealed to be lies.

## Scene sequence

**1. Do some ordinary work.** Carry tables, help a companion settle among familiar animals, choose decorations, or repair the old lantern frame. A neighbor asks about the road and actually listens. The player may tell the truth, spare detail, or admit they do not yet understand what happened. This scene remains warm regardless of the eventual tonal direction.

**2. Find a recipe with several authors.** Mara's notes give one version, a scratched mark on a flour measure gives another, and an older neighbor insists both omit the important part. They need not agree to prove that the dish existed. The first quest's parcel choice affects available ingredients and who remembers the disagreement. Substitution earlier creates extra work now; it does not condemn the village.

**3. Hear the efficient proposal.** The provisioning accord offers an abundant, excellent sweet chestnut cake and reduces the sour recipe's ingredients to zero. It seems to classify a disliked-by-many item as an error in demand. Mara initially defends the saved labor. She is tired, and the protagonist's romantic enthusiasm can sound like assigning her another evening of work.

**4. Carry the cost oneself.** The player can help make a small sour dish, recruit willing cooks, or arrange a separate table of personal contributions. Mara may agree once someone takes the work seriously. This reframes refusal as a supported option rather than a heroic command to return everyone to hardship.

**5. Leave an empty place without filling its meaning.** The village can set one place for requests not yet made. The empty chair is not necessarily for a dead or erased person. During the feast, someone finally sits there and asks for something mundane: a quiet corner, a smaller portion, the chance to leave early. The place succeeds if that request can be heard without becoming a symbol the requester did not choose.

## Choices and continuing effects

- **Keep the aid, separate personal requests from its predictions.** Preserves labor savings while asking villagers to maintain an additional channel. A named rota and an ordinary ledger make that promise practical.
- **Return the provisioning accord.** Restores direct control but increases work. The protagonist helps negotiate labor or replacement supplies; people may fairly disagree about whether the cost was necessary.
- **Run the feast as originally proposed, preserve the dissenting record.** Honors villagers who prefer the arrangement while refusing to describe unanimity falsely. Later evidence can reopen the decision. The sour-dish enthusiast may be disappointed without becoming an enemy.

Whichever path is chosen, do not turn every neighbor into a synchronized chorus. One person is concerned, one is amused, one believes the explanation but likes the sweet cake better. Diversity of response is part of what remains worth preserving.

## Tone variants to compare with the user

**Enduring tenderness:** the meal succeeds and the conversation stays untidy. The final image is a companion asleep beneath the table while people argue over the recipe.

**Precarious sanctuary:** the village finds a workable arrangement, but Mara admits she cannot maintain it alone. The player leaves with a specific shared obligation, delegable rather than an endless personal chore.

**The cost of shelter:** the records remain, but nobody can be certain the missing preference has fully returned. The village nevertheless decides that uncertainty does not justify destroying the evidence or refusing the request.

These are alternative drafts. None has been selected, and no branch requires Mara's death or the loss of all companions.

## Order, abort, and reentry

A player can help early, encounter the wider pattern later, and return to compare records. The same festival need not replay; follow-up occurs during ordinary meals and civic work. If the player declines the feast, villagers hold it and leave an account of their decision. Reentry offers a conversation and a future provisioning choice, not an accusation that the protagonist abandoned home by exploring.
