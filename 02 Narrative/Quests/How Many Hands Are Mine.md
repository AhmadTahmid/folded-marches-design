# How Many Hands Are Mine

**Role:** Confluence regional arc; plural personhood, present consent, continuity, and exit. **Place:** the Reedhouse beside the Braided Road, a school favoring limited tide-sharing and supported intervals apart. **People:** Corda-of-Reeds and several community witnesses whose own voices must be developed separately. **Creatures:** Ribbonwake and a local Loam Choir. See [The Confluence](../../01%20World/The%20Confluence.md).

**Prerequisite:** reach the Reedhouse, through travel, an invitation, or an Orthe applicant's chosen journey. **Completion:** Corda can express a contested preference through a channel the assembly recognizes, and a specific next step is freely chosen with support. Completion does not require deciding what all transformed people fundamentally are.

## A place capable of joy

At dusk the Reedhouse appears to have several shorelines. Its inhabitants have learned to share a little of the river's timing; someone can step out to meet a returning boat before the tide would ordinarily allow it. Dinner is chaotic, affectionate, and occasionally confusing. A cook asks which of Corda's hands has already tasted the soup. Corda replies that appetite is among the few matters on which they have never achieved a consensus.

The protagonist is welcome as an unmerged guest. There is no suggestion that a refusal to transform is cowardice. A transformed child of an elder and the untransformed elder can love one another without either serving as an argument to win.

## The request

Corda has agreed to speak at a regional meeting about connected accords. Their public voice supports participation. A narrow thread of their speech, audible when a particular reed flute is sounding, says they want to withdraw from the delegation. Corda asks the protagonist to help understand the disagreement without treating the quieter expression as automatically more authentic.

An earlier agreement contains a provision for separation. Its authors assumed that a future request would be made by a clearly bounded individual. Corda's present request is more complicated: one expression wants distance, another fears that an abrupt separation would destroy capacities both value, and a third questions whether “separation” is even the desired word.

## Scene sequence

**1. Listen in three conditions.** Speak with Corda at the shared table, in a quiet room above the reeds, and on a small boat outside the communal tide-sharing boundary. Different preferences become expressible in different conditions. None is framed as a villain taking possession of a true body. Ask what each voice fears losing and what it wishes to preserve.

**2. Read an old promise with its authors present.** Community witnesses disagree about the exit provision. One intended an unconditional right to unmerge; another intended an ongoing commitment to make meaningful alternatives possible. Both accept that pain or inconvenience cannot simply void consent, but they differ about which entity can now invoke the promise. The protagonist can identify the ambiguity without becoming judge of everyone else's personhood.

**3. A creature cannot answer for a person.** The local Loam Choir tolerates multiple rhythms by leaving patches unsounded. This suggests a practical experiment: reduce shared signaling and provide separate intervals for expression. It does not prove that Corda is a fungus, that every merge works alike, or that ecological behavior determines human ethics.

**4. The interval.** The Reedhouse can create a low-sharing room by rerouting part of its tide accord. The work interrupts some conveniences and needs volunteers to cover them. In that room Corda expresses a more precise request: postpone the public delegation, retain certain shared capacities, and explore a partial separation without promising a permanent destination. The exact wording should respond to earlier conversations, not appear as a universal hidden “correct answer.”

**5. The invitation remains open.** A friend who hoped Corda would speak feels abandoned. Another is relieved. The player can help make a substitute delegation or transmit an account with several signatures and an explicit dissent. The Confluence is not represented by one neat sentence because the meeting would find that easier.

## Possible arrangements

| Arrangement | What it honors | What must be supported |
|---|---|---|
| **Temporary separate intervals** | A preference to explore difference without immediate irreversible change | Independent space, communication, and a scheduled review initiated by Corda |
| **Supported partial separation** | A clearly expressed wish to distinguish some capacities or memories | Care before, during, and after; acknowledgment that the resulting people may make new choices |
| **Remain joined, change governance** | A present wish for shared life with stronger internal channels | A dissent channel that cannot be revoked merely because it is inconvenient |
| **Decline public representation** | Corda's right to stop serving as evidence for others | Replacement civic labor; outsiders accept a boundary rather than demand disclosure |

The player may favor an arrangement, but cannot impose it by choosing dialogue over Corda's objection. A withdrawn invitation is a legitimate quest outcome if the investigation itself becomes intrusive. Trust can be repaired through respecting distance and offering practical support, not by finding a secret persuasion bonus.

## The unsettling moment

A delegation letter drafted through the communal accord says, “Corda has never wished otherwise.” The wording is beautiful, fluent, and false to a conversation the player just heard. When challenged, the letter changes to “Corda has never wished otherwise in a way that would help Corda.” The second claim is more frightening because it explains the suppression as care.

Keep the original letter and testimony available. The authorial question is whether the local accord is simplifying, an institutional practice is teaching it to simplify, or both. Do not solve the origin of the Largesse here.

## Discovery order, interruption, and reentry

If the garden case is known, Corda rejects a simple equivalence: a plural person's internal disagreement is not itself a symptom. If Orthe is known, the community asks which exit supports actually worked. If this is the first case, hospitality and practical preparation establish the setting before any unsettling document appears.

The player may leave between conversations; Corda chooses interim support and is not forcibly separated offscreen as punishment. Private testimony cannot be made public simply because it unlocked a clue. Reentry begins with asking whether the invitation to help remains open. Essential regional evidence can be supplied in anonymized or limited form, and no main-story route requires the protagonist to merge.
