# A Room for the Keeper

**Role:** optional recurring adventure; a familiar creature's care becomes an enclosure. **Places:** Tavio's wagon on the Bell Road; a proposed seasonal return pavilion beside the Braided Road on Istravel's approach. **People:** Tavio Rusk; Iona Serr or a local carpenter as practical support. **Creature:** the familiar Doorling already mentioned in [People of the Marches](../../01%20World/People%20of%20the%20Marches.md#tavio-rusk). This does not decide whether it is his permanent companion.

**Entry:** any ordinary meeting with Tavio can begin the friendly sequence. The unsettling investigation becomes available after one other repeatable accord discrepancy; an early visitor finds a functioning pavilion. **Completion:** establish a usable exit and agree with Tavio on his next arrangement. No particular catch, fight, faction membership, or protagonist history is required.

**Reference boundary:** Evertale Transcript Analysis (working-archive reference) identifies the inspiration: recognizable individuals across changed categories, scattered clues that support inference, and reversal of keeper and kept. Every event below is an original proposal. The transcript narrator's simulation and experimentation theory is not imported.

## First meeting: the shop assistant

Tavio's familiar Doorling has a nick in its outer brow fold and an absurd preference: it takes the label off a parcel, places the label beside the parcel, then pats both before accepting food. Tavio's attempt to sell ribbon becomes a ridiculous negotiation with a creature that insists the word for ribbon must have a place of its own.

The player can help sort labels, deliberately swap two to see what happens, or watch Tavio lose an argument about which end of a shelf is the beginning. The Doorling corrects the familiar parcels but does not understand an unfamiliar symbol. Its intelligence is specific, learned, and appealing. The brow nick, sequence of two pats, and blue packing cloth provide three ordinary recognition details. No shadow changes shape behind it.

At a subsequent market, the Doorling offers Tavio his own coat-label and then a biscuit. He accepts both with theatrical dignity. This is funny because it is imitating his care for it. The player may join the routine; remembering a precise input is never an access requirement.

## Second meeting: a very good rest

Tavio has joined a seasonal rest scheme for exhausted carriers. A pavilion keeps deliveries organized while residents sleep, provides meals, and leaves room for animals accustomed to traveling with them. He freely chose two nights there. He is not concealing a crime or being punished for catching creatures. His unmanageable promises, already established in his character page, made reliable rest especially attractive.

His debts still exist. His sleep has improved. The new problem is that his stated departure has passed while the pavilion's account says his stay is proceeding exactly as requested. He can discuss travel, joke, and disagree. When he tries to leave by a guest doorway, he walks past the same blue cloth into a different entrance to his room. He asks for help after testing this twice himself.

Staff suspect a misassigned return route. The Doorling sits happily beside the guest register; it neither threatens visitors nor blocks ordinary staff movement. The scene initially looks like a repair job. Tavio says, “I asked it to keep a place for me. I ought to have asked how far the place went.”

## An investigation the player can perform

Three tests separate a clerical mistake, animal behavior, and a working confinement effect:

1. **Label and bearer.** Carry Tavio's loose guest label through the doorway without him, then leave it behind while he approaches. The label crosses normally; he returns to his room. The writing is evidence of the accord's classification, not a magic sticker that can simply be removed. This rules out ordinary mistaken signage.
2. **Invitation and departure.** Visitors can leave. Tavio can cross into an unconnected service yard when a carpenter opens its physical shutter. Reopening a guest doorway still brings him back. This establishes a local, bounded route effect and supplies an actual escape path. The pavilion cannot retrieve him from anywhere in the world.
3. **Recognition and refusal.** Place a familiar parcel beside an unfamiliar one. The Doorling repeats its old sorting mistake, recognizes Tavio's coat, and pats the coat-label before offering him food. Offer the established farewell signal: it makes the feeding space more comfortable instead of yielding the passage. It recognizes its friend and fails to accommodate one particular wish.

These tests can be watched, described, or performed with borrowed tools. A companion is welcome but does not pronounce an infallible verdict. The journal retains the initial stay request, the later departure request, and observed crossings separately. Staff testimony can explain maintenance; it cannot negate a witnessed result.

## The later reveal: someone has made him a nest

Once the boundary is understood, an inspection opens the pavilion's return gallery. What appeared to be decorative blue curtains unfolds into a canopy stitched from enlarged patterns of Tavio's packing arrangements. His walking boots sit where the Doorling once kept its feeding bowl. Shelves carry his favorite foods at the height of his hand. A curved rail follows the exact reach of his coat hook. The room has been made exquisitely suitable for a particular person.

Behind it, ordinary timber, workers' fittings, and several small maintained Doorling nests support the construction. The creature has not become an unlimited spatial god. A coordinating accord has connected modest nesting effects with the pavilion's return service. Its reach ends at the surveyed guest circuit.

Tavio stands in the room he believed was a station on his route. From the service gallery the player sees it as a carefully furnished enclosure, with an inspection gap at the height where a keeper would check an animal. The familiar Doorling brings the coat-label, pats it twice, and pushes a biscuit through the gap. Its unchanged affectionate gesture is the frightening part.

When Tavio rises, overlapping screens tuck inward with the same fold-and-round motion the Doorling uses to settle. His boot reaches for a measured threshold; the opening slides behind his shoulder. Nothing touches him. The player crosses the gallery to hold an ordinary service shutter open, leaving one passage outside this choreography. The animal resumes its patient feeding gesture.

The register's two fields now read **keeper: nesting resident** and **resident: traveling human**. The original intake sheet named Tavio as the traveler and offered departure on request. Neither document alone proves who changed the relationship. The present layout, failed departure, and repeated recognition establish what it now does.

Tavio reaches through the gap and returns the biscuit. “I know you remembered. I need you to let me go.” The creature waits with the patient attention he once gave it. Hold this scene; it does not need a shriek, mutilation, or sudden attack.

## Getting out is an adventure, not a confession

The service yard gives an immediate supported exit. Reaching it with luggage and a tired traveler requires opening a narrow maintenance walk, shifting the pavilion's ordinary counterweighted screens, and keeping a nest undisturbed. Iona, a local carpenter, or equipment hired through Tavio's route contacts can help. A Doorling companion may recognize nesting boundaries; a human can follow inspection marks. No creature is consumed as a key.

Opening the guest circuit for general use takes more work. Its return function must be separated from food and shelter while a staffed exit stays open. The playful sorting game suggests keeping a label beside a person without treating it as authority over them. It is an experiment, not a moral spell: success is Tavio crossing when he asks, including once while the Doorling visibly prefers him to stay.

The player can choose immediate relocation with later repair, help test a bounded return service, or bring outside witnesses before reopening it. Tavio decides whether to remain nearby. Smashing the nests is neither necessary nor the secret brave answer; it can damage shelter while leaving the administrative return arrangement unexplained.

## Consequences and return

- **Relocate first.** Tavio resumes travel after rest elsewhere. Temporary rooms and an extra carrier cost money or a concrete favor. The pavilion remains partly closed for investigation; its ordinary staff continue caring for other guests.
- **Retain a smaller service.** Separate food and rest from return routing, employ an exit attendant, and test a traveler-controlled stop. The result is useful and less convenient. Inspection of a later voluntary departure supplies evidence that the change holds locally.
- **Suspend the guest circuit.** Outside witnesses preserve the case while other services remain open. Tavio's route network helps displaced guests find beds; the cities can argue about responsibility without acquiring an excuse to imprison witnesses.

The Doorling may continue visiting Tavio, remain with its colony, or keep its distance while a keeper works on the confused routine. Its future behavior is shown, not declared cured by a dialogue choice. Neither its capture nor its abandonment is required. A farewell can remain affectionate and disappointing to it.

The quest contributes a witnessed departure test and material support arrangements to the regional assembly, never a mandatory secret ending key. It does not prove that all refuges are prisons or that human and creature bodies share one hidden origin.

## Order, interruption, and author boundaries

If the friendly meetings were missed, Tavio demonstrates the sorting game with ordinary parcels before investigation; the player receives a sketch of the earlier market scene as attributed testimony. Witnessing the history is richer but not compulsory. Existing garden knowledge changes the question to whether classification acts through space as well as speech; Reedhouse experience prevents equating changed categories with erased identity.

Leaving the investigation triggers an explicit interim arrangement: Tavio uses the service-yard exit and stays with a nearby carrier while repairs wait. No hidden clock strands or transforms him offscreen. Reentry preserves test results and asks what he wants now. After the finale, this can be a local maintenance case under the chosen regional settlement.

**Proposed author truth:** this is the same living Doorling, and the trapped traveler is Tavio, not a substitute; a particular coordinating accord sustains the enclosure. **In-world accounts:** protective nesting, administrative misclassification, or an accord learning incompatible meanings of care. **Player evidence:** repeatable crossings, preserved requests, bodily recognition, and the maintained gallery. **Open decisions:** origin and intention of the larger power; any late fourth-wall use of the case; final emotional tone. Tavio's captivity is an event to resolve, not a permanent cosmic mystery.

The quest never invents a guilty protagonist past, accuses every partnership of abuse, or makes ordinary catching secretly authorize human confinement. Transformation in the Confluence remains a separate question. The horror concerns help made impossible to leave.
