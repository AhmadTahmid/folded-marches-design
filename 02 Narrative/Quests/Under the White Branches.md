# Under the White Branches

**Role:** optional field adventure; Vela's first substantial appearance; discovery and practical teamwork. **Place:** the [White Orchard](../../01%20World/Atlas.md#the-white-orchard), reached from the Bell Road circuit. **People:** Vela Cotrin, optionally Pell. **Creatures:** Glassgrazers, with several possible companion helpers. **Prerequisite:** ordinary regional access; no specific recruit or combat win. **Completion:** observe the changing herd path, make a usable route record, and settle how the joint work is credited or kept separate.

## A promising expedition

Mineral branches arch over shallow pools. Water trembles in their forks when a six-legged animal rubs against a stone. The place resembles a winter orchard until the player notices soft green growth under the pale structures.

Vela is standing in an awkward pool with an excellent waterproof case held over her head. She has kept the expensive instrument dry and soaked every other possession. She asks for a hand, then immediately explains what she measured. Help is an introduction, not a debt that makes her subordinate. A player who arrives from the upper path can meet her after she has climbed out herself.

She wants to chart where a usable walking route crosses the herd's feeding circuit. Her sponsor values the survey; she also wants to write an account with her own observations in it. She invites the protagonist to compare approaches. Her equipment and travel allowance make some problems easy. Her patience and ability to distinguish fresh mineral deposits are real skills.

## Three observations, several approaches

**1. Follow the animals from a distance.** Wet browsed leaves, fresh six-foot impressions, and a newly rubbed stone show a turn missing from the old visitor path. Vela can teach the player which deposits are recent. A familiar Glassgrazer may draw attention to a rubbing place but cannot certify water safety by instinct.

**2. Reach the lower pool.** Walk a longer dry terrace, borrow the survey team's line for a marked descent, or use a locally available suitable climbing partner. Equipment is supplied for the job; no crafting or rare mount is required. A Bellwether may aid an established path call, while a Thimbleback can draw attention to a disturbed drainage edge. Human observation remains sufficient.

**3. Watch the crossing change.** When the keeper opens a scheduled, ordinary irrigation gate, the herd moves to newly wet forage. A return route that was clear now runs through the animals' chosen line. The gate timing is announced when the observation begins, not triggered by how many side activities the player completed. This is ecology, not an impossible transformation.

One adult stamps and lowers its body when approached too closely. The player can back away and take the high path, remain still until the herd has passed, or undertake a clearly signaled defensive encounter while withdrawing to the marked exit. Exhausting the animal is not a recruitment prize. Vela dislikes unnecessary injury, but a frightened retreat does not permanently destroy the relationship. Later dialogue acknowledges the actual approach.

## What each person notices

Vela's measurements identify a stable dry ledge. The protagonist can point out that its narrow turn is poor for a loaded carrier. Neither observation cancels the other. Together they can mark a light-foot route and a longer freight route. Working separately preserves both records with their limits.

The player may have found the crossing before meeting her. In that version she asks to see the evidence, contributes an additional measurement, and does not reenact discovering it first. Completing the commission never requires pretending to be ignorant.

## A small disagreement worth remembering

Her draft report initially places the protagonist under “field assistance.” He can accept that description if it fits the work, ask for specific observations to be attributed, propose joint authorship, or keep an independent sheet. Vela's first defensive answer is that she brought the instrument and arranged the survey. The player can show what each actually contributed.

The proposal gives her room to revise the record. If she corrects it, the later published sheet visibly includes the agreed credit. If they part with separate accounts, neither account disappears; a later meeting begins with unfinished irritation rather than instant enmity. No option invents an observation the player never made or awards sole authorship for merely being present.

The payment or agreed field stipend is unaffected by refusing joint authorship. The personal consequence concerns recognition and collaboration. Her ambition is something the protagonist can understand, even when it makes her difficult.

## Rewards, leaving, and return

The player gains a useful route record, ecological knowledge, an agreed stipend where applicable, and a relationship with someone who may challenge their version of events. A distinctively banded Glassgrazer can be observed at a later known feeding interval; tracking signs provide a dependable return opportunity under [Rare Encounters](../../03%20Game%20Design/Rare%20Encounters.md). Bonding is a separate readable invitation, not a random trophy drop or a stronger-stat prize.

Before committing to the gate observation the player can leave freely. During it, the visible retreat route remains available; returning offers another scheduled observation. Vela continues her work if the player declines, and her later introduction refers to her own report. No critical evidence or later city access depends on completing this optional story.

**Closing image:** Vela dries a sock on the case meant to display her sponsor's emblem while the protagonist reads their corrected route caption aloud. A Glassgrazer begins an extremely satisfying scrape at exactly the wrong moment.

**Information record:** the herd's route and gate conditions are observed facts; whether Vela is careless about recognition is an interpretation supported by what she actually does next. The survey contains no cosmic revelation. Exact encounter statistics, reward amounts, and the eventual publication scene remain proposals.
