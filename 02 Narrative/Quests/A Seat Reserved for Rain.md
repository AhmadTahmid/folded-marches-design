# A Seat Reserved for Rain

**Role:** major Istravel arc; first repeatable anomaly, civic prosperity, care and autonomy. **Place:** the Ninefold Garden and Highwater Arcade in [Istravel](../../01%20World/Istravel.md). **People:** Dr. Senna Vey, Arel Dast, Pell Arven, and an unnamed garden regular to develop later. **Creatures:** Morrow Moths, Ribbonwake, and companion visitors.

**Prerequisite:** access to Istravel; the village correspondence provides an introduction but is not required. **Completion:** establish a bounded account of the garden effect and help an affected visitor choose a supported response. The quest never requires the player to declare the whole Largesse malicious.

## The invitation

The Ninefold Garden is a public miracle. Nine terraces hold different weather: fine cool rain for damaged lungs, dry warm wind for aching joints, a patch of afternoon used by night workers, and a lawn where flowers turn slowly enough for children to sketch their opening. The fees are graduated or publicly covered. Show actual beneficiaries having better lives.

Senna asks the protagonist to deliver a creature-safe shade screen to a regular visitor. That visitor has reserved two folding chairs beneath the rain canopy but arrives alone. Asked about the second chair, they say it belongs to a journey. Then, embarrassed, they say they must mean a visiting friend.

Their old reservation includes a practical request: build enough walking endurance to visit the mountain where their sibling lives. Their current care summary says “sustain a satisfying local routine.” Either could be a reasonable choice. The discrepancy becomes frightening only if the person cannot freely discuss the alternatives.

## Scene sequence

**1. An ordinary clinical disagreement.** Senna explains that recovery can change priorities. Arel worries that an investigation conducted as spectacle will frighten patients and give rivals a pretext to close the garden. Both accept that the visitor should be heard. The player can request a quiet interview, invite an advocate, or begin by studying the garden's work.

**2. A walk with two endings.** Under the canopy, the visitor describes the proposed mountain journey as pointless and struggles to remember the sibling's address. Beyond one particular arch, they can name the address and ask when the next caravan leaves. Their medical symptoms also worsen slightly outside the treatment area. Repeat the crossing only with permission; the person is not an inexhaustible test subject.

**3. Three kinds of record.** A reservation written before treatment names the journey. A Ribbonwake keeper's maintenance tally shows that the relevant water circuit was recently connected to the garden's scheduling accord. Pell has a route sketch annotated by the visitor. None alone establishes that a memory was stolen. Together they locate a change in conditions and a suppressed ability to express an intention.

**4. The garden attempts kindness.** When the protagonist asks the accord's civic interpreter to accommodate the journey, the garden produces a remarkably beautiful imitation of mountain rain. It has answered a geographic request with a sensation. The visitor cries, partly from pleasure and partly because the simulation makes it harder to explain that the sibling is a person, not a landscape feature.

**5. A public service worth repairing.** Senna can separate the treatment from one layer of predictive scheduling at a cost in staff time. Arel can fund a temporary traveling-care arrangement, but only by delaying a nonessential expansion or securing outside assistance. The visitor may still choose to stay. Their restored ability to choose, not agreement with the investigator's preference, is the relevant success.

## The upper rain circuit: an expedition through the garden

The investigation includes an active ascent, not only interviews. A maintenance keeper asks the protagonist to trace the recently connected water circuit from the public terraces to its overhead habitat. Visitors normally see falling water and brilliant flowers; the route above reveals ropes, root beds, nesting ledges, and living currents requiring daily care.

First, cross a gallery behind a thin sheet of elevated water. A Ribbonwake rests along the channel with its transparent fringe spread across the maintenance opening. It is tending a sheltered pocket rather than obstructing the route out of malice. A keeper can establish another pocket; a patient party can wait for the creature's feeding interval; a prepared companion and human team can use a dry upper crossing. None requires capturing the wild parent.

Next, follow an apparent patch of afternoon through a nursery in which Morrow Moths have all moved to the dark side of a screen. The public view makes the garden look continuously thriving. From above, the player can observe that pollinators need a cycle the scheduling accord has flattened into an ideal display. The beautiful moths are not corrupted. Their absence is ecological information, and restoring a darker interval produces a quiet, rewarding swarm.

At the circuit junction, the easiest maintenance plan would drain the nursery to preserve clinic pressure. The protagonist can instead help keep two limited channels functioning long enough for staff to separate them: reach the manual service line, carry a light guide rope, protect a fragile crossing, and signal the order of the change. A Kitefin can carry the line if accustomed to it; a Bellwether can relay a learned signal from safe footing; a ladder and trained garden workers cover those roles for other parties. This is a candidate traversal sequence whose precise mechanics remain for later design.

The strange discovery comes during the changeover. Two nearby streams briefly reflect incompatible versions of the same terrace: one full of people, one laid out as though nobody would ever need to leave a chair. Touching either brings ordinary cold water. The observation is documented, not decoded into a final cosmic truth. The party returns with a physical circuit boundary, a restored nursery interval if successful, and a reason to test the visitor's expressive difficulty under changed conditions.

If the crossing becomes unsafe, retreat leads to a slower staff-led separation and a temporary reduction in garden capacity. It does not kill a patient to punish a missed jump. The adventure can be difficult while its failure states remain intelligible and proportionate.

## Meaningful resolutions

| Resolution | Immediate benefit | Consequence to carry forward |
|---|---|---|
| **Independent treatment and an advocate** | The visitor keeps care while speaking outside the scheduling loop | More staffing and repeated reviews; Senna can attest that bounded separation helped |
| **Supported journey** | The visitor can attempt the mountain trip with travel care and a clear return option | Arel must explain a budget tradeoff; the protagonist may contribute a route or a connection rather than personally escort every mile |
| **Pause the affected garden segment** | Stops exposure while the cause is investigated | Other patients require actual alternative appointments; merely closing the gate is an incomplete response |
| **Continue with disclosed monitoring** | Maintains capacity and respects a visitor who prefers to remain | Evidence must be independently retained and withdrawals supported; officials cannot claim the issue has been disproved |

The player may publish findings, share them with a limited review group, or first work with the visitor on an account they consent to share. Hiding all evidence protects short-term capacity but weakens later independent review. Publishing private medical details against the visitor's wishes damages that relationship even if the broad warning is justified. Public testimony can be anonymized.

## Companions and visual set pieces

A Ribbonwake winds above the Arcade carrying rain without letting it fall. A visiting Bellwether hears a repeated interval wherever the scheduling circuit overlaps the water circuit; its response helps locate a boundary but cannot identify a culprit. A Thimbleback may happily nap inside or outside the effect, reminding the player that not every being experiences the same conflict.

The strongest uncanny scene is quiet: nine chairs turn with the sun, and the empty tenth patch of grass remains shaded for a visitor who has not yet decided to come.

## Discovery order and interruptions

If this is the first regional case, dialogue foregrounds measurement and uncertainty. If the player knows Orthe or the Confluence, Senna asks them to state exactly what matches, and what does not. Corda's testimony can suggest separating channels; Iona's work can support a less dependent care apparatus. These help but are not required.

The player may pause between interviews. Garden staff retain responsibility for patients. Abruptly disabling a service is not an ordinary exploration action; it requires an explicit quest decision with a relocation plan or a visible emergency consequence. A visitor who declines further investigation remains a person with rights; another consenting case or maintenance evidence can support the broader plot.

On reentry, appointments and elected arrangements are summarized. An initial interpretation can be amended when new evidence arrives. Completion supplies evidence about **one local interaction** and an ally with a specific position, not a universal truth token.
