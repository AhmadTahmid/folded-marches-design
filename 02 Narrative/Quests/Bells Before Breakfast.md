# Bells Before Breakfast

**Role:** opening arc; local belonging, creature partnership, first road choice. **Place:** [Rocca Selva](../../01%20World/Rocca%20Selva.md), the Chestnut Steps, and the first reach of the Bell Road. **People:** Mara Vezzi, Tavio Rusk, and the protagonist’s [household](../Family%20at%20Home.md). **Creatures:** Thimbleback, Bellwether, and Kitefin; see [Bestiary](../../01%20World/Bestiary.md).

**Prerequisite:** none beyond a selected or provisionally neutral protagonist identity. **Completion:** deliver or responsibly hand off the village's first three obligations and choose where to carry its correspondence. No starter species, combat victory, or faction pledge is mandatory in this narrative proposal.

## The day as it should feel

Morning reaches the terraces before the village. A Thimbleback has slept in the warm bread basket and emerged powdered in flour, leaving a narrow tunnel through the breakfast rolls. Festival ribbons climb the alley from washing line to washing line. Above the roofs, a school of Kitefin follows a cloud so low that children try to touch its trailing rain with broom handles.

Mara is polishing a bell that is not due to ring. “People notice a dirty bell when it is silent,” she says. She offers the protagonist a paid temporary road stewardship: carry correspondence and small goods to the season's first exchanges. The protagonist already supports his mother and three younger siblings. He wants better earnings for them and a larger life himself; wages and a reliable reference can make the first absence practical. An optional packing conversation from [Personal Story](../Personal%20Story.md) reveals what they hope to take with them.

Three errands make the village legible. Bring a cistern measurement to the communal water table; recover festival cloth carried into a chestnut tree; help Tavio settle an agitated animal before its keeper departs. Each can be approached through patience, physical work, a suitable companion, or help requested from a neighbor. These are different social experiences, not locked creature checks.

## Scene sequence

**Before departure — an agreed absence.** After discussing paid work, he reviews the terms and household arrangements with his mother, makes room for siblings’ requests, and decides what he wants to admit about his own excitement. [Personal Story](../Personal%20Story.md) and [Family at Home](../Family%20at%20Home.md) supply scene options. This can occur before the fair journey or after it if he first goes only for the day. Exploring the village does not consume the family’s support.

**1. A gift with instructions.** Mara gives the protagonist a small bell-shaped case for important notes. Its clapper has been wrapped because sounding it near pasture during the morning count confuses the herds. The case is ordinary craft, not a unique artifact. She also gives advice that changes with the protagonist option: a defined character gets a familiar private rebuke; a custom character is asked what they usually forget when they travel.

**2. The animal that will not perform.** Tavio has brought an inexperienced Bellwether to meet a prospective keeper. It will cross a plank when unobserved but freezes when the crowd claps. The player can clear the spectators, lead a wider path, investigate its response to the nearby bells, or persuade the buyer to postpone. A Thimbleback already present in the village offers a different first partnership: it follows someone who helps restore its disturbed nesting material. A passing Kitefin can be observed and assisted without becoming an immediate catch. Partnership is an invitation with behavioral evidence, not ownership awarded for pressing the right button.

**3. A road above weather.** The fair lies on a saddle where merchants set their stalls on both sides of a slow-moving cloudbank. Rope weights descend into white air; small creatures ferry paper streamers between carts. A suspended civic waterway is visible far below, a bright line leading toward Istravel. Orthe's stone road appears and disappears through the opposite terraces. The player sees two futures before being asked what either believes.

**4. The displaced parcel.** One basket has been unloaded at the wrong stall. Its owner remembers sending six parcels, while the protagonist's tally records seven. The seventh holds sour chestnut flour intended for an unfashionable village dish. It is not a cursed object. A traveling carrier may have combined two consignments; the local weighing accord may group “equivalent” staples. Record the mismatch without forcing a sinister interpretation.

**5. A choice of first destination.** Both cities will accept the correspondence. Tavio explains their services through experience rather than a lecture: one repaired a keeper's damaged hearing quickly; the other helped a family challenge a binding contract. A public notice offers seasonal field work through either city's expedition offices and independent carriers. The protagonist can ask about pay, lodgings, and creature arrangements, then head toward either city, accompany a short survey, or return home to prepare. The road posting stays open; employment opportunities do not vanish on a hidden timer. [First Paid Journey](../First%20Paid%20Journey.md) develops the optional survey follow-on, including a trip home to agree its terms before departure.

## Choices with continuing consequences

- **Keep the Bellwether meeting public or make space for it.** A quiet meeting may produce a more confident partnership and a disappointed audience; completing the demonstration may help Tavio's immediate trade but reveal the animal's need for better handling. The keeper is not permanently lost because the player needed a second attempt. The player can revisit and repair the situation.
- **Protect the sour flour as a distinct request or accept the substitution.** Protecting it preserves a minor person's preference and consumes carrying space or a favor. Accepting another staple satisfies the delivery's broad purpose. The village receives usable food either way; later [Feast for an Empty Place](Feast%20for%20an%20Empty%20Place.md) can refer to this concrete choice without making it a cosmic morality test.
- **Promise an early return, decline to promise, or appoint a substitute.** Mara remembers the promise. There is no secret real-time deadline. A later return scene acknowledges whether the player sent word or made alternative arrangements.

## First anomaly and restraint

As the fair closes, the wrapped bell seems to answer a distant Bellwether. The sound could have come from another stall. The protagonist can inspect the wrapping, ask a witness, or let the moment pass. Nobody ominously predicts that the player will regret leaving home. The day ends with good food, a new road, and the possibility of companionship.

## Discovery order, interruption, and reentry

This is the common starting arc, but errands can be done in any order. Leaving early creates a handoff scene in which Mara or a neighbor covers the outstanding obligation; the player later owes specific work, not an invisible reputation penalty. A rejected partnership remains revisitable if the creature's circumstances permit; an animal that leaves with another keeper can recur as a relationship, rather than respawning as an identical prize.

There is no catastrophic abort. On reentry, completed acts stay completed, villagers refer to the protagonist's actual choice, and essential correspondence can be recovered from the Bell Court. If the player returns after learning about accords, the parcel record offers supporting texture but cannot by itself prove a world-changing theory.
