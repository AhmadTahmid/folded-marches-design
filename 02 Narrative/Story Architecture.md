# Story architecture

**Active authored draft:** [Campaign v0.1](Campaign%20Draft%20v0.1.md) supplies the coherent twelve-beat route and three playable endings. Its local Keeper mechanism governs that draft; broader alternatives below remain reference.

An adventure about a young provider leaving home to improve his family's prospects, making room for his own ambitions, and learning to share responsibility when that life becomes frightening.

This is a proposed narrative spine, not a fixed script or approved ending. [User Brief](../00%20Foundation/Design%20Status%20v0.1.md) owns the commitments. The player's identity and the amount of surviving comfort remain open in [Protagonist Options](Protagonist%20Options.md) and [Horror and Fourth Wall](Horror%20and%20Fourth%20Wall.md).

## What the player thinks they are setting out to do

The season's first fair is approaching. [Rocca Selva](../01%20World/Rocca%20Selva.md) needs someone to travel the reopened roads, deliver village goods, register water and pasture agreements, and find out why returning traders disagree about the length of the journey. These are practical responsibilities with room for delight: earn expedition money, meet extraordinary animals, bring back a useful gift, see the famous gardens, perhaps decide where to belong.

The protagonist takes a **paid temporary road stewardship** because it can fund departure and provide references for better work. At eighteen or nineteen, he already supports his mother and three younger siblings. The family agrees a practical first absence; he wants to send more home and experience a larger life himself. [Personal Story](Personal%20Story.md) develops the arc, [Family at Home](Family%20at%20Home.md) the household, and [First Paid Journey](First%20Paid%20Journey.md) a concrete commission. The posting grants introductions and obligations, not legal command over cities or unique cosmic access. Responsibility for strangers extends existing care through particular commitments. The father's absence remains explicitly unresolved.

The mountain is a home with aspirations and arguments. Some neighbors want better access to city medicine; others resent maintenance obligations hidden inside generous aid. The protagonist begins outside the great camps without being raised outside politics.

## The underlying pressure

The new [observer/sanctuary comparison](The%20Observer%20and%20the%20Sanctuary.md) offers a candidate way to embody that pressure: a being uses observed attachments to offer the player an enclosure, while the protagonist and residents retain wishes of their own. Its scenes are alternatives, not a selected fifth movement or the answer to every mystery. [Companion Quests and Camp Conversations](Companion%20Quests%20and%20Camp%20Conversations.md) strengthens the ordinary adventure that must precede this reversal; [Foreshadowing and Revelation Ledger](Foreshadowing%20and%20Revelation%20Ledger.md) records setups and evidence without fixing a final cosmology.

A proposal to connect several major accords promises stable water, safer travel, and resilient public care across the Folded Marches. This would address real shortages and failures. Existing local incidents, however, suggest that some accords reconcile competing demands by making certain alternatives harder to perceive or express.

This is the story's strongest provisional danger: **help may become inseparable from an imposed account of what its recipients need**. Neither deliberate malice nor a singular thinking source is established. The same evidence may fit an alien misunderstanding, an emergent ecology, or human institutional incentives feeding an adaptive power. [Mysteries and Revelations](Mysteries%20and%20Revelations.md) keeps these interpretations distinct.

## Four movements, unlocked by discovery

| Movement | Foreground experience | Narrative change | Evidence needed to advance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I. A world worth crossing** | Mountain fair, creature encounters, beautiful routes, useful work, the pleasure of competence | The player's small commitments open a wider region | Complete or deliberately hand off the opening stewardship; choose a first destination |
| **II. The shape of a gift** | Rival cities offer attractive futures; local problems have reasonable competing explanations | Two independent cases suggest that useful accords can suppress distinctions | Witness one discrepancy and encounter a second independent account; either city can come first |
| **III. What refusal costs** | Expeditions, revisits, difficult hearings, deeper relationships | The issue becomes practical: people cannot meaningfully refuse help they cannot survive without | Establish an affected person's expressed preference, a material dependency, and a credible means of checking a claim |
| **IV. A world allowed to disagree** | A cross-community assembly and an expedition to a shared junction | The player helps make a durable arrangement, with identifiable benefits and burdens | Assemble a viable coalition or pursue an explicitly narrower local settlement |

Movement is epistemic rather than a mandatory clock. A player can spend a long time exploring, collecting companions, and helping neighbors. Horror does not secretly advance because they enjoyed side content. Late travel remains beautiful, and some quests remain funny or entirely ordinary.

The personal arc runs through these movements: agree departure and support, establish better-paid work and a life of his own, endure a loss of security and confidence, then keep a bounded promise with help. Movement III needs a direct ordeal and a played aftermath, not only increasingly difficult policy questions. [Personal Story](Personal%20Story.md#personal-arc-through-the-four-movements) owns those beats; [Horror and Fourth Wall](Horror%20and%20Fourth%20Wall.md#an-ordeal-the-protagonist-carries-out) proposes the bodily experience. Neither secretly makes a late tone or protagonist identity decision.

## Flagship expeditions inside the story

Every movement needs a physical adventure whose outcome gives the later conversation weight. These are proposed scene packages, with alternate routes and borrowed equipment so a particular collected species never becomes an undisclosed story requirement.

| Movement | Physical objective and landscape | Creature encounter and discovery | Playable climax to design later |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | Carry the village's goods across a mountain saddle occupied by a slow cloud; recover windblown festival cloth | A Kitefin's cloth theft reveals a displaced roost; a Bellwether responds to confusing festival tones | Reopen a crossing through observation, repair, and a first cooperative action; reach the cloud-side fair |
| **II** | Trace the Ninefold Garden's suspended rain circuit from terraces into an overhead nursery | Ribbonwake and Morrow Moths reveal that different parts of the apparently unified garden need incompatible conditions | Keep a nursery and public treatment supplied while separating one circuit; return with an actual boundary the witnesses can test |
| **III** | Carry three independent accounts across the junction's welcoming shortcut and survey an exit it repeatedly fails to display | A companion's ordinary preferences and wild animals' movements reveal a route outside the shortcut's assumptions | Preserve a physical retreat route while the party takes the less accommodating path; deliver people and unmerged evidence to the meeting |
| **IV** | Enter the destabilized junction, reach stranded delegates, and return through hanging water and impossible depth | Wild Ribbonwake, material-consuming Undoers, a displaced Vergecat, and companions with bounded abilities | Rescue and partition under pressure; emerge with enough independent lives and records to make a real settlement |

Movement III's preparation journey and Movement IV's descent are detailed in [A Table That Keeps Its Empty Chair](Quests/A%20Table%20That%20Keeps%20Its%20Empty%20Chair.md). The [White Road Moving](Quests/White%20Road%20Moving.md) expedition adds a separate large-scale wilderness adventure that can occur earlier or deepen later ecological choices. Political consequences follow the player's exploration and action; the story must not become a chain of hearings in place of the monster RPG.

## The opening should earn affection

[First Region](First%20Region.md) now gives this movement a hub and three outward loops, two additional adventures, a first-companion exemplar, and staged introductions to recurring travelers. [Cast Arcs and Relationships](Cast%20Arcs%20and%20Relationships.md) follows shared work into friendship, rivalry, damaged trust, and later cooperation. The cast changes through their own choices as well as the protagonist's involvement; an optional early meeting is never the only route to a comprehensible later rescue.

[Bells Before Breakfast](Quests/Bells%20Before%20Breakfast.md) introduces an inhabited place before an existential problem. The first remarkable creature might be asleep in a bread basket; the first spectacular vista might be a living ribbon of airborne rays threading a mountain cloud. The player learns that companionship can be cooperative, inconvenient, and rewarding.

Do not place a screaming face behind the first cute animal. One bell sounds where no bell should be, or a delivery tally contains an unexplained correction. The protagonist has a good day anyway. Subsequent evidence should make a player remember the detail, not feel punished for having enjoyed the scene.

## The middle remains genuinely open

Three regional stories establish different dimensions of dependence:

- [A Seat Reserved for Rain](Quests/A%20Seat%20Reserved%20for%20Rain.md): an Istravel public garden provides care and quietly makes certain desires difficult to articulate.
- [The Weight of a Gate](Quests/The%20Weight%20of%20a%20Gate.md): Orthe's safety rules protect many people while trapping some at the boundary of the permitted life.
- [How Many Hands Are Mine](Quests/How%20Many%20Hands%20Are%20Mine.md): a Confluence community must distinguish continuity, present consent, and the right to separate.

None proves its host faction wholly right or wrong. Each offers allies who disagree with one another, and each can be visited first. The second and third visits acknowledge existing knowledge instead of replaying a naive investigator's surprise. A skeptical city official may accept a physical observation while rejecting its interpretation.

The optional [White Road Moving](Quests/White%20Road%20Moving.md) adds a nonhuman perspective: human convenience is not the only interest an accord can erase. [Feast for an Empty Place](Quests/Feast%20for%20an%20Empty%20Place.md) returns the issue to ordinary affection without making the village a secret cosmic center.

[A Room for the Keeper](Quests/A%20Room%20for%20the%20Keeper.md) adds an optional recurring thread across these movements: a playful Doorling greeting becomes evidence when a rest pavilion begins keeping Tavio as its resident. Its bounded enclosure supplies a physical expression of the danger, with a usable escape and recognizable personal stakes. The first encounter can occur during ordinary travel; the confinement case follows another tested discrepancy. It supplements the regional argument without making completion a finale prerequisite or imposing the reference narrator's theory (working-archive reference) on this world's origin.

## What an antagonist can be

Opposition can come from an administrator who suppresses a worrying result to keep a clinic open, a beneficiary who knowingly accepts a change, a restorationist willing to deny others' treatment, or an accord that cannot represent an unresolved request. No character needs to confess that prosperity was always a fraud.

Each principal person should have something they will do, something they will refuse, and a cost they can bear only with help. Arel may defend public integration while demanding independent witnesses. Neris may authorize a carefully bounded exception while refusing an unverifiable universal promise. Corda may support connection while rejecting collective consent presumed on behalf of silent members. See [People of the Marches](../01%20World/People%20of%20the%20Marches.md).

The late opposing phenomenon can offer sincere, terrifying help: a junction at which every request is satisfied because nobody arrives wanting mutually incompatible things. Its beauty and practical success should remain observable. Its failure is the missing capacity to want otherwise.

## Ending logic: arrangements people can actually live under

[A Table That Keeps Its Empty Chair](Quests/A%20Table%20That%20Keeps%20Its%20Empty%20Chair.md) resolves the current regional crisis. It does not reveal the final origin of the universe. Outcomes depend on demonstrated commitments, available care and transport, independent testimony, wildlife provision, and whether affected people can withdraw. These are specific world facts, not a goodness score.

| Possible settlement | What it makes possible | Unsettled burden |
|---|---|---|
| **The Open Compact** | Linked public goods with multiple witnesses, local vetoes, and funded exits | Slower decisions and costly maintenance; a veto can protect a community or obstruct another's care |
| **The Great Provision** | Broad integration and dramatic improvements in access, with whatever independent safeguards the coalition secured | Concentrated dependence; the epilogue tests whether promised dissent remains expressible |
| **The Separate Hearths** | Smaller reversible accords and stronger local control | Uneven capacity; prosperous districts must choose whether to support poorer ones without absorbing them |
| **The Living Treaty** | Voluntary embodied negotiators and plural forms of personhood mediate between unlike needs | The work changes its participants; succession, fatigue, and the rights of those who will not merge remain real |
| **The Long Refusal** | Retire the proposed shared accord while preserving existing services where possible | Requires replacement logistics; a refusal unsupported by material preparation becomes an emergency, not a triumphant purity ending |

These are components and possible emphases, not five exclusive colored buttons. One watershed may remain separate while two districts integrate and a Confluence community serves as a voluntary intermediary. The epilogue follows named people and companions through that arrangement: who can leave, who receives treatment, which road remains passable, what the protagonist can truthfully promise a neighbor.

No proposal requires the compulsory death of every companion, revelation that all friendship was counterfeit, or destruction of the world to achieve a legitimate conclusion. A harsher tone can leave fewer secure places without invalidating what the player loved. Tonal selection remains open.

## Authoring commitments

- Critical revelations have more than one route. An optional quest may deepen or personalize knowledge but cannot be the sole undocumented key to a comprehensible ending.
- Missed investigations produce a narrower coalition or weaker claim, with explicit opportunities to reconsider. They do not silently convert the protagonist into an ally of a cosmic evil.
- The player may finish without explaining the Largesse. They must be able to explain the agreement they helped make and who bears its costs.
- Do not write a universal sequel catastrophe that cancels every outcome. Remaining uncertainty should support continuing life and future stories.
- A large, intricate design on paper is not a promise that every branch will be implemented. [Game Direction](../03%20Game%20Design/Game%20Direction.md) owns later scope decisions.
