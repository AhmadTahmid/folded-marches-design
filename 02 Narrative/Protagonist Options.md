# Protagonist options

**Decision deliberately open.** The user requested both a defined protagonist and a customizable, village-rooted protagonist before choosing. Neither option is the selected canon. Both originate in [Rocca Selva](../01%20World/Rocca%20Selva.md), begin outside the major ideological camps, and inherit ordinary social ties rather than a destiny.

## Shared anchor

[Everyday Joy, Trade and Appearance](../03%20Game%20Design/Everyday%20Joy%20Trade%20and%20Appearance.md) compares preset outfit changes compatible with either identity option. Clothing expression does not choose a customizable biography. [Resonance, Fusion and Last Resorts](../03%20Game%20Design/Resonance%20Fusion%20and%20Last%20Resorts.md) compares learned attention, widespread innate variation and an acquired perceptual channel without selecting unique destiny or explaining the father. The [observer proposal](The%20Observer%20and%20the%20Sanctuary.md) preserves his own wishes and reactions even if the player is separately perceived.

The shared protagonist is a young man of about eighteen or nineteen who already supports his mother and three younger siblings. He leaves for their material wellbeing and a larger life of his own. The user established this family direction on 2026-09-26 and explicitly left the reason for his father's absence open. Nineteen is the working age. Both identity options keep these anchors.

Paid road work offers a route toward better earnings. He knows familiar paths and household needs but is regionally inexperienced. [Personal Story](Personal%20Story.md) develops his arc and [Family at Home](Family%20at%20Home.md) proposes individual relatives. Mara remains a mentor or neighbor separate from his mother. The father's absence is an unfilled author choice, not a promised search.

## Option A: a defined person

**Revised provisional sketch:** nineteen, name unchosen, with paid experience helping local keepers and maintaining routes. He has become reliable before having much opportunity to be adventurous. He remembers household expenses, bargains firmly for others, and feels awkward admitting what he wants to buy himself. A careless joke can hurt a sibling before he notices that he sounds like the adults who once dismissed him. These habits are proposals, not a claim that every young provider behaves alike. The earlier older apprentice sketch is superseded by the user's age direction.

He draws tiny impossible additions in accurate maps: a staircase for a sheep, a window in a boulder, a balcony overlooking last summer. His siblings dispute which pictures are good. His fear is becoming someone whose life can be described entirely as useful functions. This gives the threat personal resonance without making his psychology the cause of the Largesse.

The player chooses how he acts on these contradictions. Family support and personal ambition are shared premises; later experiences can change where he wants to settle. Dialogue cannot replace the entire biography. Name, appearance, voice, and exact temperament remain proposals.

**Scene sample:** his mother asks whether he wants the posting. He straightens three already straight parcels. “It pays more.” She replies, “I asked if you wanted it.” The player can answer, “Yes, even if we didn't need the money,” “I want to see if I'm good at it,” or “I want us to stop worrying about next winter.” Each expresses a different part of the motive without denying the other parts.

**Strengths:** precise relationships, authored humor, a strong performance identity, and memories that horror can revisit without inventing a life for the player.

**Costs:** players inherit attitudes they may not share; an authored feeling must remain distinguishable from the player's own feelings.

## Option B: a customizable person with a real home

The player chooses name, presentation details, and a small number of grounded past roles within the established age and household premise. The precise editable options remain open. Customization does not imply an optional family or a blank past; character creation establishes connections rather than predicting morality.

Choose one primary apprenticeship as a proposed starting lens:

| Background | Existing competence | A relationship to write into the opening |
|---|---|---|
| Terrace and cistern keeper | Reading water, stone, communal schedules | Mara trusts their care but thinks they avoid asking for help |
| Pasture companion keeper | Creature behavior and seasonal travel | Tavio remembers an earlier animal-handling disagreement |
| Festival craft apprentice | Repair, costume, performance, listening | Neighbors expect the protagonist to carry a public role they may not want |
| Path and weather recorder | Routes, correspondence, field observation | Pell once borrowed an accurate notebook and returned an inaccurate map |

These are local entry points, not exclusive classes or guaranteed knowledge of supernatural truth. All starting routes can reach the same story. Improving family prospects while wanting more personally is the shared premise; the player chooses how to express it and may decline other interpretations of their feelings. No background resolves the father’s absence or dictates secret guilt.

Offer a few light relationship selections in play: “Mara taught me,” “I worked beside her,” or “Our households share the winter chores.” Avoid an extensive pre-adventure questionnaire. A later scene can ask what they miss about home; the player can name a sound, a person, a routine, or nothing yet. That answer becomes an in-game authored memory with visible provenance.

**Scene sample:** Mara asks what the protagonist hopes to find. “Better pay for all of us,” “Work I can choose,” “Things to tell them about,” and a reserved “A start” all fit. Mara's reply references a chosen shared history, not a secret personality assessment. The player is not penalized for remaining reserved or for expressing affection for home while leaving it.

**Strengths:** ownership and varied roleplay; a natural connection to creature collecting; the contrast between self-description and an accord's imposed summary can become personal.

**Costs:** more conditional dialogue and relationship maintenance; less opportunity for a tightly authored interior voice; the game must remember only what the player actually supplied or enacted. Blank identity cannot become an excuse for blank relationships.

## The meaningful tradeoff

| Narrative task | Defined protagonist | Custom village-rooted protagonist |
|---|---|---|
| Remembering home | Detailed shared memories can be authored early | A few selected memories need alternative versions and explicit context |
| Facing a changed recollection | The player can distinguish the protagonist's established recollection from a new claim | The game must preserve the player's chosen claim rather than guessing it |
| Relationship friction | Friends may credibly challenge known habits | Friends should challenge observed actions or selected history |
| Fourth-wall address | Can distinguish player from the authored person sharply | Must distinguish player from avatar without implying access to real identity |
| Long-term production | Fewer identity variants, potentially richer performance | More dialogue and presentation variants; modular authoring is essential |

## What we can draft before deciding

Every current quest uses **the protagonist** and relies on the shared anchor. Dialogue marked as an identity-specific sample stays illustrative. The same opening can later be drafted twice, in short scenes, to compare emotional texture before making a decision.

Do not silently combine both options into a compromise where customization is cosmetic but the game repeatedly asserts unwanted private motives. A hybrid can work, but its fixed biography and editable identity must be plainly stated. This page's recommendation is to compare the two opening scenes with the user; it does not select an approach.
