# Mysteries and revelations

**v0.1 author truth:** [Campaign](Campaign%20Draft%20v0.1.md) identifies the local Keeper and observer as one entity with bounded perception. Global origins remain unresolved; local rescues and exits receive definite answers.

Mystery needs authorial bookkeeping even where the final explanation remains open. This page separates proposed observations from interpretations. All entries are drafts; **“observed” means intended to be verifiable inside the proposed fiction**, not approved canon or a claim about an implemented game.

## The evidence contract

An observation can be incomplete without being worthless. The player should be able to preserve a contradiction, compare witnesses, and act under uncertainty. A later revelation may change the meaning of a scene; it should not routinely announce that every previous act of investigation was fabricated.

Three layers belong in the fictional journal: **field record** of what the protagonist encountered, **accounts** attributed to speakers, and **interpretations** the player may endorse or leave unresolved. A claim such as “the garden took away her wish to travel” belongs in interpretation until evidence supports more than a changed conversation. Original records remain accessible even when an accord offers a revised account. See [Horror and Fourth Wall](Horror%20and%20Fourth%20Wall.md).

## What can become firm without selecting a cosmology

**September proposed extension:** [The Observer and the Sanctuary](The%20Observer%20and%20the%20Sanctuary.md) develops M07 through accurate references to in-game interface use, the traveler's reaction and independent boundary tests. Its sanctuary expansions can develop M09's care/custody question, but do not change the local Tavio/Doorling quest's established draft facts. [Foreshadowing and Revelation Ledger](Foreshadowing%20and%20Revelation%20Ledger.md) records the candidate clues. An annotator, keeper, following figure and pursuer remain potentially separate; no single explanation is inferred from thematic resemblance.

The story can establish that particular accords respond to need, that observers and records sometimes change their behavior, and that local conditions can suppress a distinction useful to a person. It can establish that some remedies work in some places. It need not establish a god, a hidden AI, a simulation, a global mind, or a species-wide intention.

Avoid the premature line “the Largesse wants to enslave humanity.” A person may fear or advocate that interpretation, but the author must distinguish it from the measured result: after a particular intervention, certain people cease to express an intention that they previously expressed, and an independent condition can restore the expression.

Likewise, a beautiful city is not evidence that everyone secretly consented; a disturbing transformation is not evidence that nobody did.

## Revelation routes and redundancy

**Core chain:** discover a discrepancy → establish a repeatable difference → hear from an affected person → test a bounded response → confront regional scale. No single collectible carries the only explanation.

- The discrepancy can originate in Istravel, Orthe, the Reedhouse, or a survey with Pell.
- A repeatable difference can be demonstrated by walking the garden boundary, comparing the Threshold Court's service conditions, or providing Corda a separate channel of speech.
- The testimony stage cannot be satisfied by collecting documents about an absent person alone. Affected people need scenes in which their stated wishes can complicate the investigator's theory.
- A player who reaches the assembly without a full investigation receives a clear summary, can ask for witnesses, and can pursue limited local safeguards. They do not need to pretend to possess certainty they never earned.
- Optional ecological and homecoming quests add different interests and practical resources. Their absence changes representation and specificity, not whether the main plot makes sense.
- The optional pavilion case adds continuity evidence: an individual can remain recognizable while a system changes its assigned role. Its independent exit rules out unlimited retrieval; its intact requests rule out consent inferred merely from accepting earlier help.

## Discovery-order variants

If **Istravel comes first**, the player may initially think this is a technical failure of one ambitious city. Orthe then shows that withholding access can also narrow a life. If **Orthe comes first**, the garden initially looks like proof that broader provision is kinder, then complicates that relief. If **the Confluence comes first**, plural testimony gives the player vocabulary for disagreement before they see institutions erase it.

If the player already knows a concept, a quest offers a more demanding question. “Can this happen?” becomes “Can it happen without anyone intentionally causing it?” and then “Who must bear the cost of preventing it?” Dialogue should acknowledge what the player witnessed, not merely possession of an abstract clue flag.

## Deliberate ambiguity versus unfinished writing

For every ambiguous event, later drafts should record at least two explanations that fit the evidence and at least one explanation the evidence rules out. If nothing could falsify anything, investigation becomes decoration.

For the final origin, this pass deliberately leaves several compatible possibilities. For whether a particular official forged a certificate, whether an exit route exists, and whether a companion was actually harmed, later scenes need definite answers. Those are accountable human events; calling them cosmic ambiguity would dodge the player's investment.

Unresolved production questions, such as how many journal variants can be authored, belong in design notes. They must not be disguised as mysteries in the fiction.
