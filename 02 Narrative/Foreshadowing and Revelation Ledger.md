The user values the satisfying retrospect of Berserk and Attack on Titan. These are taste references, not a researched scene analysis or permission to copy twists. Here foreshadowing means **a detail first encountered for one coherent reason becomes newly meaningful after evidence**. It does not mean every pleasant moment was false or every minor character secretly controls the world.

All entries below are candidates. The table organizes existing drafts and the September expansion; it does not schedule every reveal or choose a cosmology. [Mysteries and Revelations](Mysteries%20and%20Revelations.md) owns the broad questions; individual quests own local author truth.

## Give the first appearance an ordinary job

The protagonist should have a reason to notice a detail before it becomes a clue: a tool works, a gesture solves an awkward crossing, a song accompanies an evening, a route sketch gets them paid. Repeat it in a changed but still understandable context. Only then ask a new question.

Distinguish a **setup**, a **reminder**, **independent evidence** and a **payoff**. Four identical mysterious noises do not make four clues. A skeptic must have a fair way to test at least one explanation. Missable material can enrich recognition without becoming the only route to understanding.

## Required evidence for stronger claims

To claim **the same individual persists**, use more than matching color or a label: a known habit, an identifiable object, continuity of a witnessed route or a response only that relationship established. Do not insert an unnoticed replacement solely to make a twist impossible to question.

To claim **someone cannot refuse**, show a prior stated aim, a present attempt, a local constraint and an independent channel or boundary comparison. A contented expression is neither proof of freedom nor proof of coercion by itself.

To claim **a being knows the player as distinct from the avatar**, reference an actual interface act inaccessible to the character, show that distinction in a scene and allow a bounded test. Remembering a previous timeline alone can remain ordinary fantasy.

To claim **a device and sanctuary are connected**, compare material structure, release behavior and a separate traceable record. An ominous shared emblem is an invitation to investigate, not enough to establish a cosmic origin.

## Redundancy without exposition walls

For a plot-critical idea, provide at least two routes: an observed action plus testimony, or an object comparison plus a later demonstration. A player who missed an optional joke still understands the climactic choice; the player who noticed it receives richer recognition. A short stable recap records observations, not a forced interpretation.

Do not introduce four new named mysteries during the first battle. The early opening needs an understandable goal and a creature the player wants to approach. One quiet discrepancy can stay in the journal until the player has more context. The horror's intensity does not secretly increase with real-world hours or optional exploration.

## The following figure

The attachment proposes a figure seen at the edge of perception or arriving where the player has been. Compare a traveler on overlapping routes, an observer's embodied messenger and an animal exploiting sightlines. Give a trace—an object displaced, a witness with a different account or a repeated but testable route. A figure that always vanishes with no possible investigation will soon feel like a decorative scare.

Use a few authored appearances, not permanent screen clutter or random flashes. Whether it belongs to the sanctuary/annotator remains open. A reduced-visual-effects account can describe the same visible occurrence without making hidden objects easier or harder to investigate.

## Revisions and eventual payoff review

Before moving a reveal, check the owning scenes, earlier knowledge, absent companions, outfit changes and optional relationship variants. Record which clue becomes misleading by accident. A clue that can no longer pay off should be revised, retired openly or given a valid mundane resolution; do not rescue it with another unplanned conspiracy.

Later ask a reader to retell what they noticed before explaining the intended twist. Surprising but unsupported is different from surprising and retrospectively fair. No table establishes emotional success without the scenes themselves.
