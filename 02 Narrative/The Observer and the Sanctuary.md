# The observer and the sanctuary

**v0.1 selection:** [Campaign](Campaign%20Draft%20v0.1.md) makes keeper, annotator and observer one local entity. Its copied hometown contains no substituted real family. Earlier alternatives below remain reference, not simultaneous v0.1 truths.

**Proposed central reversal:** you came to discover the world's creatures; one of them has discovered you. The user-supplied notes offer a stronger story mechanism than a sequence of glitches: an intelligence studies the distinction between the young traveler and the presence making choices, then uses real attachments to construct an enclosure.

This page develops that possibility, not a selected final plot. [Cosmic Possibilities](Cosmic%20Possibilities.md) retains its three origins. A sanctuary keeper, field-guide annotator and remote observer might be one being, related beings or independent failures. Combining them would need a deliberate decision. None is automatically the whole Largesse, the cause of the Opening or a literal human controlling another computer.

## Keep the young man's life real

The protagonist has his own history, body, relationships and stated wishes. He already supports his mother and three siblings; his father remains unexplained. Discovering the player does not reveal that he was empty, artificial or predestined. His family remains materially affected by what happens. Voluntary transhuman people are not secretly villains or failed versions of him.

Ordinary companionship is also real. Refuges, growth and recruitment should not all become cruelty revealed after the player used mandatory mechanics. The threat comes from a particular system or being refusing departure, confusing attention with consent or imposing a preferred account of a person's needs.

## Two structures to compare

| Structure | Story centre | Strength | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The field guide studies its reader** | Observation gradually becomes reciprocal; an uncertain intelligence learns how to approach the observer | Connects discovery and collecting directly to fourth-wall horror | Too many reactive entries can sound like a personality quiz or omniscient taunt |
| **The sanctuary makes room for everyone** | A sincerely helpful refuge expands into preservation without exit | Gives inactive companions a lived place and turns care into physical adventure | Makes party storage feel guilty unless normal use and the keeper's misconduct are clearly separated |

They can intersect later: an interface may be one of a keeper's senses. But start by writing one complete chain rather than declaring every journal, companion and city part of the same conspiracy. The current pavilion case in [A Room for the Keeper](Quests/A%20Room%20for%20the%20Keeper.md) stays intact; this page does not secretly enlarge that quest into the final explanation.

## The observation chain

| Stage | Scene and player action | New warranted inference | What is still unknown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary record | Observe a creature's habitat, habit and response. Compare an imperfect field note with what happens | Records can be wrong and corrected through action | Whether another author exists |
| Wrong addressee | A companion looks toward a viewpoint while the traveler stands elsewhere | Its attention is not following the usual target | Creature habit, staging coincidence or outside observer? |
| Unsolicited entry | A separate entry appears in a clearly identifiable unfamiliar hand | Someone is classifying a second participant | The writer's identity, senses and purpose |
| Tested distinction | The traveler cannot describe an interface selection that the writer accurately references | The phenomenon distinguishes an in-world act from a player selection | Whether it can influence either independently |
| An attractive approach | An invitation incorporates observed preferences and offers real help | The writer has learned how to draw attention | Kindness, curiosity, recruitment or containment? |
| A refused exit | A supposedly ideal visit prevents someone's expressed departure | Care has become confinement in this local arrangement | Ultimate motive and whether a broader system shares it |

Original notes remain available beside new annotations. A written claim about an observer is not proof on its own; the player needs a bounded test or witnessed consequence. Most ordinary records stay ordinary.

## Scene study one: the sleeper and the reader

**Prerequisite:** several ordinary camp visits have established the companion's familiar call and the protagonist sleeping while a player can inspect the journal. The selected companion is whichever individual has sufficient relationship scenes; Clatter is an exemplar, not a compulsory starter.

After an enjoyable evening, the field guide adds a small line under an unidentified entry: “The traveler sleeps. The observer does not.” The line is an attributed anomalous annotation, not a rewrite of the original notes. The player can leave it, compare it with the camp or ask about it in the morning.

Close the book. The young man is visibly asleep. The companion is awake and oriented toward the established player viewpoint. It performs its ordinary greeting, then settles. No sudden face, fake error or compulsory scream follows. In a reduced-direct-address presentation, a stable description records that its attention does not follow the sleeper.

**Player evidence:** sleeping avatar, known gesture, new attributed sentence. **Possible ordinary account:** an animal reacting to a sound near the viewpoint. **Open author choice:** whether this is the first actual contact or only a misleading observation. A later scene must do more than repeat the ambiguity.

## Scene study two: the observatory asks the wrong person

**Prerequisite:** the player has investigated at least two bounded observer anomalies or receives a readable introduction from a witness. An abandoned observatory is an optional proposed site; its place on the map is not selected. The strange following figure may be its messenger, but that connection remains open.

A figure asks the traveler a question. He answers. The figure interrupts: “Not you.” He looks behind himself. “You cannot turn far enough.” Then it describes a simple event the player actually performed in the current scene: opening the same sketch twice, inspecting the unopened door, or switching the displayed companion. Avoid invented statements about the player's life or habits.

The traveler asks, plainly, “Who are you talking to?” He is alarmed; give him a voice and an action. He moves his hand away from the figure's instrument and asks to leave. The player can leave with him, ask one further bounded question or agree to a clearly described test. No invisible consent follows from continuing dialogue.

One optional message asks, “When the choices appear, choose the second if you can hear me.” This can demonstrate an interface channel, but the second response must say what it does and allow refusal. A malicious being requesting a harmful choice is a later, telegraphed deception; button order alone cannot constitute a permanent sacrifice.

**Working author truth within this version:** the figure can perceive a specific interface event and distinguishes two agents. **Beliefs:** it calls the player an observer; the traveler may initially call it a second voice. **Evidence:** the accurate local description, his limited knowledge and a repeatable test. **Open:** its sensory mechanism and how the player's control relates to the world. No predestined host is required.

## Scene study three: a home with no tomorrow

**Prerequisite:** the player has several sincere homecomings and knows independent wishes: a relative learning a trade, a friend wanting to travel, a companion enjoying an inconvenient path. The settlement remains genuinely inhabited outside this scene. Its exact late-game tone is still open.

An intelligence offers a visit arranged around the player's actual in-game choices. The house is warm, friends are present and a favorite local performance is happening again. The first pleasures are real within this local proposal. The contradiction emerges when someone who wanted to leave can no longer explain the wish, tomorrow's carriage never arrives, and two neighbors cannot refer to an old disagreement even though their written note survives.

The entity says, “I have removed the reasons you leave.” This is its account of care, not the game's moral. The protagonist objects: “She wanted to go.” A happy expression cannot settle the question when the scene has evidence that expression is being constrained.

The playable task is to secure an independent route and help inhabitants express a current choice beyond the local boundary. Offer shelter outside, support for someone who freely wants to remain and an opportunity to revisit after release. Do not require destroying every comfort to prove a correct philosophy. The entity may learn a boundary, bargain incompletely or refuse and become an active opponent; none is a selected ending.

**Evidence:** retained earlier plans, interrupted attempts to name them, a boundary comparison and a route that restores expression. **Author truth for this candidate:** this particular arrangement obstructs departure. **Open:** whether the town is copied, displaced or locally enclosed. Each version carries different rescue obligations; do not write a final escape until selecting it.

## The sanctuary as an ordinary place before the failure

Inactive companions have habitats and recognizable routines; the player can visit or use a concise remote view. A short roster change does not require walking a large park every time. Animals play with each other, sometimes choose privacy and remember an earlier activity. No mandatory food-delivery circuit or decay during real-world absence makes storage convenient only on paper.

A patient keeper can arrange habitat needs without knowing everything. Introduce one honest limit—two animals need separate feeding areas, or a migrant returns seasonally. That prevents later expansion from appearing to be an unexplained unlimited service from its first scene.

The local horror begins with a habitat the player did not request. It contains a house and a resident who remembers an ordinary outside street. Later the refuge contains part of that street, and independent travelers report its outside disappearance. A keeper claiming “they were lonely” is a belief to investigate; loneliness does not authorize confinement.

The physical spectacle can remain beautiful: sunny lawns beside impossibly deep chambers, neighborhoods curated as enrichment, a nursery around something immense. If nothing can die inside, that is a keeper's claim until investigated. It must not be silently applied to every refuge or used to establish immortality worldwide.

Selection lights could be perceived as changes in the sanctuary sky. A resident says, “It brightens around us before you ask.” The interface becomes an observed channel; tactical commands and actual selection still work. Eventually the keeper asks where the choosing presence lives and offers to build it a habitat. The fiction concerns what it may do to inhabitants to preserve the observer's presence, not actual imprisonment of the person at the keyboard.

## Warehouse abduction: preserve the shock, compare the consequence

The attachment proposes arms taking a following creature from a warehouse and irreversible death that survives a reload. The strong visual beat is the violent interruption of a familiar walk. Its permanent punishment and unavoidable anti-reload taunt are unselected and conflict with the user's current recovery comparison.

**Rescue variant:** earlier traces establish that the warehouse draws living things into its service compartments. Entering the marked capture boundary is a consequential choice. The arms suddenly seize a companion, whose familiar call continues inside. The player can cut the tether, retreat with the others or enter a short rescue route. A creature able to evade the grasp may escape; absence of that species does not make rescue impossible.

If taken, the individual's identity persists. A borrowed helper and physical evidence support a finite recovery adventure. After release, let it regain normal play and develop a small new preference; permanent stat damage is not the automatic prize. This produces fear and subsequent care without replacing the partner with a new collectible.

**Near-miss variant:** the grasp takes a pack or tears a token free while the animal escapes. It preserves shock with lower consequence and lets the first event teach the boundary. **Harsher variant:** a clearly signposted late irreversible loss is retained for future comparison only, not written into this pass's default.

A reload restores the selected save. If a later design adds an explicit narrative echo, it can acknowledge supported story recurrence without overruling restored state or accusing the player of cheating. Swapping companions is a valid preparation choice; authoring an unavoidable death that ignores it is a different scene, not adaptive intelligence.

## Speaking, refusing and being heard

A formerly nonverbal companion speaking could be startling precisely because ordinary communication was established. Compare an actually acquired speech channel, another being borrowing the call, and an interface translating an unknown message. Evidence should distinguish them later; do not make every species secretly articulate.

A climactic companion refusal can express earned agency: it recognizes a repeated containment gesture and warns before the player's committed action. The interface offers an alternative, retains the cost and lets the player understand what changed. Repeated random disobedience would weaken this one event. A relationship scene, a rehearsal and an earlier harmless refusal must prepare it.

The protagonist asking the player not to enter a building also fits. His reason must be concrete and a valid alternative must exist. Agreeing to stop is an active choice with a scene, not merely failure to click the next plot button. If proceeding remains available, author his reaction and subsequent trust honestly; controlling him does not make him emotionally absent.

## Reframe the harsher storage and interface sparks

Universal storage compression agony, degrading evolution and corrupted breeding would make the central collection loop a moral trap. Prefer a **particular coercive apparatus** with observable defects, alternatives and rescued individuals. Voluntary growth, ordinary eggs and healthy sanctuaries can continue elsewhere. “Kindling, one source in three expressions” stays an ontology alternative; it is not silently merged with the Largesse.

An entity can damage a drawn representation of an HP bar or appear over a menu, while a stable accessible panel preserves the real value and commands. No undisclosed targeting inversion, false damage preview or running clock while paused is required. Actual exit, recovery, settings and accessibility remain dependable. Process names, desktop messages and external-file puzzles in the source are not commissioned features.
