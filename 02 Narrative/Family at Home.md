# Family at home

**Established direction:** he is eighteen or nineteen, lives with his mother and three younger siblings, has already supported them, and leaves to improve their material wellbeing while aching for a larger life. The user explicitly chose to leave the reason for his father's absence open. **Draft below:** nineteen as a working age, names, sibling ages and genders, occupations, scenes, and financial arrangements. ## A house people actually live in

The household occupies a modest home near the Chestnut Steps in [Rocca Selva](../01%20World/Rocca%20Selva.md). An upstairs shutter rattles; the good table doubles as a workbench; someone always moves his boots. Warmth and meals are usually dependable. Privacy, spare money, and control over next winter are less so.

His wages have been their main cash support. He carries goods, helps with route maintenance, and assists creature keepers. His mother adds repair income and keeps the household functioning; neither contribution would easily replace the other. He has supported his family, but has not single-handedly raised three children.

The absent father is not automatically dead, missing, cruel, or connected to the Largesse. Until the author chooses a history, leave that biography unfilled. Do not plant a suspicious portrait or an unexplained inheritance as if an ordinary open question were a promised mystery.

## Ada — his mother

**Proposed name and role:** Ada repairs clothes, pack harnesses, and weather coverings. She knows which travelers pay promptly and which beautifully dressed customers must be charged in advance. Her workbench supports a small business, not a decorative domestic hobby.

She wants steadier orders and eventually a covered workroom where she can leave a repair laid out overnight. She also likes noisy company, cards played badly, and hearing strangers describe places she has never visited. She is capable of envy as well as pride when her son prepares to go.

Her concern is concrete: an attractive wage means little if he pays for his bed, damages, and equipment from it. She asks to see the terms. She neither forbids departure nor pretends that the household will feel no loss. They negotiate as a mother and her nearly grown son, with uneven experience and affection, rather than as a dependent woman and her replacement husband.

**Voice cue:** “You can help us. You cannot promise that nothing will ever happen to us.”

## Lina — fourteen

**Proposed eldest younger sibling; she/her.** Lina has a quick eye for lettering and enjoys making elaborate signs for ordinary village business. She wants training in a lowland print shop, though she currently describes this as merely looking at possibilities.

She resents being volunteered for his old chores. Her affection comes through rivalry: she redraws his route sketch more clearly, then demands credit. She can be proud of his departure while asking why the eldest boy gets an adventure and the next child gets the laundry.

Their first useful argument ends with tasks divided among adults and siblings, not his telling her to be sensible. Her later letters include work of her own. She may win a small commission before he succeeds at his first major one.

## Tobin — ten

**Proposed middle sibling; he/him.** Tobin is absorbed by creatures but impatient with the unglamorous parts of their care. He can recognize a Kitefin's direction change at a distance and still forget to clean a water dish. He wants his brother to see something extraordinary and describe it accurately.

His request is a drawing of an unfamiliar creature, including its feet. He is certain adults leave the feet out when they are inventing a story. A letter can later correct the protagonist's confident claim about a familiar species: Tobin watched it himself.

He needs reassurance about contact, not a promise that his brother will always return immediately. He also has friends whose games sometimes matter more than listening to another travel story.

## Nella — six

**Proposed youngest sibling; she/her.** Nella invents competitions with rules that favor whoever is losing. She wants to ring one of the small practice bells without an adult holding it, and dislikes being asked for a farewell performance when she is busy.

She borrows his scarf for a game, then refuses the suggestion that keeping it proves she is brave about his leaving. She would rather keep the scarf because she likes it. Their bond lives in small habits: an intentionally terrible song, a chair they both claim, the way she corrects his reading voice.

When he returns, she may have changed the song. Affection can survive his missing part of her life.

## Why better wages still matter in an abundant world

The [Largesse's prosperity](../03%20Game%20Design/Prosperity%20and%20Dependency.md) is real, but uneven. Public warmth does not buy imported boots, pay for apprenticeship travel, replace a roof, or compensate Ada when she must stop paid work to care for someone. Their household uses shared services and exchanges help with neighbors; it has little financial reserve.

The goal is therefore a margin of choice: repairs before a leak becomes serious, training without a predatory obligation, fewer shifts accepted because saying no is impossible. Departure offers a credible improvement. Home remains a place where people live, not a punishment meter the player must keep filled.

## A departure they negotiate

The [current opening](Opening%20Day%20Script.md) begins with work-seeking, not an accepted posting. He takes lunch and the good shirt to the fair, where Tavio can point him to Pell's directly approachable notice. Ada later reviews the actual terms at her market worktable or the home fallback; this is one conversation, not a permission quest or required second home visit. Her optional table story leaves that review accessible.

Pell's finite survey pays **36 coins**: 12 advance split 8 home / 4 purse, then 24 split 8 home / 16 purse. Food, necessary tools and emergency lodging are covered. He starts with eight personal coins; household support is recorded by those receipts and is not charged again at supper. The optional orchard or wagon booking has its own stated split in [First Five Hours](First%20Five%20Hours.md). Unsafe findings earn the base wage; repairs, recruitment and Mara's practice are not conditions. Existing work covers the first absence. Mara remains a separate community contact.

They choose a known carrier for letters and agree to review the arrangement after the first completed placement. This is an authored story milestone, not a hidden countdown. Taking a side route or searching through several in-game nights cannot make the children hungry.

Later commissions may visibly support a roof repair or Lina's training. Routine support is accounted for within agreed contract rewards; the player does not repeatedly purchase groceries, grind remittances, or trade essential battle preparation against family suffering. Special gifts can remain optional expressions of attention, without affection scores or cruel comparisons.

## People continue while he is away

Letters arrive at understandable journey milestones and preserve each person's voice. Ada gains or loses a customer. Lina experiments with a new design. Tobin admits a mistake. Nella dictates a story that another child disputes in the margin. Visits reveal ordinary changes as well as affection.

These events need not become errands. Family members can solve problems, refuse his advice, and have good days that he did not provide. His growth includes learning to support people without claiming authority over their lives, and admitting that he wanted the road for himself too.

No massacre, abduction, supernatural paternal reveal, or compulsory sacrifice is proposed as this family's narrative purpose. Their continuing, imperfect life gives both ambition and later horror something specific to touch. How much comfort survives remains an open author decision.
