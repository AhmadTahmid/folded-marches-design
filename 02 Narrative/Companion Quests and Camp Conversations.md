# Companion quests and conversations between missions

Human friends and creature partners need lives that are enjoyable to share. The user asks for personal quests, conversations between missions and many memorable characters with depth. This page develops the existing ensemble before adding another crowd. Names, scenes and outcomes remain draft; romance, permanent deaths and a final roster size are not selected.

Humans are travelers, colleagues, family and allies. Their presence does not add free turns beyond the five-creature combat limit. A creature's personal story can be primarily enacted behavior; it need not become human dialogue or a fetch list with an affection meter.

## A relationship should have several forms of attention

| Form | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Passing recognition | Makes earlier contact matter without interrupting travel | Vela recognizes the repaired pocket and asks whether the rain held |
| Shared pleasure | Gives a reason to like someone beyond their usefulness | Jori insists the worst hat is the only honest one for his act |
| Personal request | Invites participation in an ambition unrelated to the main mystery | Help Miret stage the ending she has argued about for years |
| Disagreement | Makes attachment compatible with different needs | A companion refuses the noisy shortcut; Vela declines a dangerous expedition |
| Played aftermath | Shows how a success or failure enters ordinary life | A friend helps undo a stiff buckle, then asks about dinner |
| Independent development | Prevents everyone waiting for the hero | A letter reports Vela's corrected publication; the player can see it at a later stall |

Use **want → shared action → complication → choice → return** as the personal-quest structure. A confession alone is not a quest. Every personal request should be understandable before the player knows the wider conspiracy.

## Vela: the page that leaves space

**Existing authority:** [Traveling Cast](../01%20World/Traveling%20Cast.md), the White Orchard attribution agreement and [Cast Arcs](Cast%20Arcs%20and%20Relationships.md). This proposal extends those rather than repeating an already repaired breach.

**Invitation:** Vela wants a small field-guide page ready for a public stall. An unusual animal habit needs one more observation, and her expensive lens is less helpful in rain than the traveler's practical route sense. If the player previously chose separate work, she asks for a new paid contribution with explicit credit rather than claiming an old shared project.

**Adventure:** follow a feeding route, compare tracks with a seed caught in cloth and choose a safe viewing position. A field companion, local guide or longer walk all work. The memorable moment is her admitting she cannot draw the feet from that angle and asking the protagonist to sketch badly but accurately. No battle is required solely to fill a page.

**Complication:** the printer offers a cleaner composition that omits the inconvenient observation or contribution. If earlier credit was corrected, this is a new sponsor pressure and she initially defends the agreement. If credit is unresolved, the actual earlier promise supplies the dispute. With no player work owed, Pell's separate contribution can be the issue; never fabricate theft to force an apology.

**Choice:** retain a less elegant accurate page, include an attributed uncertainty inset, publish separate sheets or withdraw from this printing. Vela decides for herself after hearing the case. The player can decline more work without losing access to the regional story.

**Return:** encounter the actual page, a smaller stall or an unsold proof with her corrections. She asks what the protagonist thought of the drawing before requesting another favor. Reward: a useful habitat lead, agreed pay and a relationship variant. This is not a universal map unlock or an ending key.

## Jori: a house that laughs

**Invitation:** his small traveling show has one stage, two colleagues and not enough rehearsal time. He wants the protagonist to attend a full run, not repair his entire life. Depending on past help, a paid stagehand shift may also be offered. Preserve the first commission's exclusive work window; this takes place at a separate authored visit.

**Adventure:** find an acoustically useful courtyard, hang a borrowed sheet and coax a resident creature into a voluntary comic entrance. It may do the wrong thing in a funny, harmless way. Alternate staging works without owning a specific species. The player can suggest a brisker gag or keep the creature's interruption.

**Complication:** Jori has promised a freight shift and a rehearsal. A clear schedule reveals the overlap before someone misses it. If his established missed-booking event already occurred, this is the opportunity to keep a smaller promise, not another mandatory relapse. He cannot use charm to make everyone absorb the cost.

**Choice:** shrink the show, pay a replacement where a worker agrees, move it to a smaller later audience or let the booking go with its stated consequence. The player can watch instead of volunteering. His colleagues get views and decisions; they are not props for his generosity.

**Return:** a performance actually happens, even if it is smaller. A crooked costume repair becomes a running joke. Reward: shared pleasure, a cosmetic or song invitation and honest work terms. A failed version is an awkward evening followed by another practical attempt, not death proving that comedy was secretly tragic.

## Miret: the ending she can finally hear

**Invitation:** Miret wants a neglected comic opera performed with the ending she believes is funniest. Her rehearsal needs a room, three callers and a wind passage whose timing can be tested. Her former expedition experience helps but does not secretly make the performance a training scheme for a future horror.

**Adventure:** visit two possible venues, listen to echoes, recover an ordinary loose page and watch apprentices imitate the weather. The environment becomes something to hear and use. Captions, visual pulse marks and Miret's demonstrations preserve the task without pitch recognition or mandatory microphone input.

**Complication:** a pupil disputes the confident interview she printed on their behalf. If that failure was already repaired, use the agreed original program and a new request for permission. Otherwise the page and pupil's own words establish what was changed. The scene concerns a real person, not a supernatural overwrite excusing her.

**Choice:** let the pupil speak awkwardly, publish an agreed correction, change the program or cancel that publicity. The performance can still succeed. Asking rather than presuming becomes a habit visible in a later expedition.

**Return:** the final comic beat lands—or lands badly and everyone can say so. Miret has a career beyond the protagonist. Her later rope signals are introduced through a separate clear rehearsal, with alternate trained helpers if she is absent. Reward: a recognizable musical motif, a lasting invitation and optional practical knowledge.

## Rovan: the work after the offer

**Invitation:** the retrieval captain offers a limited paid trial on a stranded load. Declining is valid. The job demonstrates why people respect him: lawful team coordination, attention to tired creatures and a plan with a retreat.

**Adventure:** survey the loading path, choose a safe sequence and carry a delicate object without demanding perfect combat. An optional keeper contest shows how he saves a resource for a decisive reply. Winning or losing affects feedback and recognition, not legal ownership of the route.

**Complication:** later, his established exclusive-access conflict blocks another crew. The ordinary invoice, removed grips and misuse of Pell's name make it tangible. Friendship does not turn his lie into cosmic influence.

**Choice:** accept a narrower job without endorsing the closure, expose the false claim, build a rival safe approach or negotiate restitution. An emergency rescue can require temporary cooperation while the dispute remains unresolved. Rovan can change or persist in opposition; a tragic backstory is not automatic forgiveness.

**Return:** visible access, compensation and crew choices show what actually changed. His praise can coexist with disagreement. A later last-resort move, if selected, should expose a material commitment he makes for his crew rather than reveal an unexplained godlike power.

## Creature personal quests

### Clatter: a path it chooses

This uses the existing [First Companion](First%20Companion.md), not a compulsory starter. After several ordinary walks, Clatter repeatedly avoids a short crowded plank but enjoys a wider rough path. Follow its choice and find a place to scratch the horn ruff. A keeper may explain the noise, but the player can also observe it directly.

Later, a familiar resting post is moved during legitimate repair. The quest is to find a suitable alternative with the animal, not blame the workers or restore everything exactly. Offer two locations; let its behavior decide. It can begin trusting a calmer approach without becoming universally fearless. The payoff is a changed voluntary route, a greeting and a humorous refusal when applause returns.

If the individual is absent, another companion gets an anatomy-appropriate story. Do not replay Clatter's horn need on a creature without horns. Later horror can repeat the learned boundary, but this quest is useful and sincere by itself.

### A Thimbleback: the wrong excellent button

The button collector remains an existing recruitment exemplar. It wants a particular ugly button, not the most expensive gift. Follow the exchange among camp animals, discover its misplaced favorite and offer a trade that leaves it free to decline. Player quest items never enter the game. The creature may keep both the bad button and a smooth stone nobody understands.

Reward a small cache, individual habit and camp beat. A player who leaves it wild can finish the activity and see it again. No friendship decay forces daily gifts, and the prize is not a compulsory rare crafting component.

### A Cragmantle: a useful weight

An individual recruited through the existing weather signs enjoys mineral ledges and rests differently on wet stone. A proposed personal excursion follows it to a sheltered old trough, where human maintenance has altered access. The player observes the actual preferred ledge, helps open a different approach or leaves the animal to visit independently.

This can enrich a mount relationship later, but does not claim the current prototype has riding or the complete combat kit. Species anatomy and behavior still come from the Bestiary. A carried load must be appropriate to its eventual movement brief, not invented merely to force a strength puzzle.

## Short conversations with concrete subjects

These are sample beats, not a final script. Each speaker line appears separately; stage actions are author notes rather than visible Narration boxes. Choose one event-linked exchange at a stop, with optional further conversation.

*After wet fieldwork; Vela wrings out the expensive lens cloth.*

**Vela:** “Your drawing got the feet right.”

**Protagonist:** “It looks like a broken chair.”

**Vela:** “Six legs. Accurate chair.”

*Jori folds a costume beside the fire.*

**Jori:** “You can watch tomorrow. That is the whole job.”

**Protagonist:** “Someone will need a hand.”

**Jori:** “Probably. I have hired two.”

*Rovan after a poor practice sequence.*

**Rovan:** “You spent the answer before I asked the question.”

**Protagonist:** “Again?”

**Rovan:** “If you want. Keep one thing back this time.”

*Miret puts a chair near the exit after a hard expedition.*

**Miret:** “Here, or outside?”

**Protagonist:** “Outside.”

**Miret:** “Then outside.”

Clatter can interrupt any suitable ordinary beat by demanding the scratch that was postponed. Do not turn every camp exchange into a prophecy, exposition or confession. Letters from home can describe new work and games as well as request support.

## Availability and continuity

Conversations trigger from authored events, not real-world absence. Missing an evening supplies a later equivalent where needed. The recap says what happened and who is currently reachable without revealing secret interpretations. Personal quests need an invitation, playable situation, choice, consequence and re-entry route; they can remain available without forever freezing their characters.

Relationship tracks record actual promises, disputed credit, agreed work, animal preferences and witnessed care. Do not label the most compliant person the highest-affection winner. A refusal can be healthy, and a reliable friend can still disagree.

## Expanding the cast without making introductions exhausting

Use three layers: a recurring core with several played returns, regional people with one substantial local story and a later footprint, and small memorable encounters that do not all demand quests. New characters enter through work, travel, music, rivalries and ordinary leisure. Record who they know besides the protagonist and what changes in their absence.

The user's Naruto reference asks for breadth and depth; it does not fix a cast count or require each person to possess a trump card, tragic secret or ancestry reveal. Grow groups where a scene gives them room. [Cast Arcs](Cast%20Arcs%20and%20Relationships.md) remains the relationship authority; this page adds adventure and evening studies rather than replacing existing biographies.
