# Opening day — the road-opening fair

This is the critical-path script for [First Five Hours](First%20Five%20Hours.md), from waking at home to the first paid report and the evening return. [Two Bookings Script](Two%20Bookings%20Script.md) supplies the intervening optional branch. The first-five-hours package supersedes the earlier opening's contradictory hiring/report locations. Campaign events after the opening remain owned by [Campaign Draft v0.1](Campaign%20Draft%20v0.1.md).

Each spoken paragraph is one advanceable dialogue beat with a named speaker. Staging is not narration dialogue. Allow advance, skip and a short objective recap. Moving away from an optional conversation closes it politely and preserves its useful entry point. Keep essential terms available from the job card, even after skipping the spoken version. The protagonist's displayed name is the player's chosen name; **You** below is an author label.

## F5-M01A — the shirt on the wrong chair

**Entry:** new game, Morning Arrival, inside Ada's house. **Purpose within the first two minutes:** understand that the protagonist wants paid work beyond the village, knows Tavio can introduce him, and has a family worth returning to. The father is neither mentioned nor implied by an empty chair or suspicious object.

Morning sun reaches the upper wall before the floor. The player's boots are beneath a chair bearing Lina's painted sign: **ORDERS TAKEN HERE**. Ada is fitting a new strap to an old harness. Nella uses the good shirt as the roof of a cushion house, one sleeve held up by a spoon. Through the open door: a wagon wheel, a shouted direction, then the unmistakable scrape of something heavy making the turn below.

**Nella:** “You're standing on the garden.”

**You:** “My boots are in the garden.”

**Nella:** “They've been there all winter.”

Let the player lift the shirt carefully or negotiate an exchange for the folded work coat. Both free it; neither awards virtue points. Nella keeps building after the exchange. Taking the bag and putting on boots grants movement immediately. Inspecting family objects is optional.

**Ada:** “Tavio's here. That's him shouting at the corner.”

**You:** “He said he'd bring work notices.”

**Ada:** “He brought six last year. Three were for a man who'd already left.”

**You:** “Then I'll catch him before he sells the paper.”

Ada holds up a rolled cloth lunch.

**Ada:** “Eat this before you buy anything that smells better.”

**You:** “That's a very narrow window.”

**Ada:** “If someone offers you road work, let me see the terms. I'll be at the market table.”

**You:** “I want a proper posting this time.”

**Ada:** “I know.”

Player can answer **“And money left after the journey.”** or **“I want to see the other side.”** Ada answers, respectively, **“Then ask who pays for the bed.”** or **“Then take the good shirt.”** These are attitudes, not mutually exclusive motives.

**Objective:** *Find paid road work. Tavio's arriving wagon is in Carrier Yard, below Bell Court.*

On the threshold Tobin runs alongside for a few steps with a drawing.

**Tobin:** “If you see something with more than four feet, count them.”

**You:** “I do know how to count.”

**Tobin:** “You said that last time.”

He returns to his own game rather than following as a tutorial assistant. A glimpse down the stair shows a many-footed visitor unfolding beside the wagon. This immediately makes his request interesting. Taking the longer accessible lane reaches the same reveal. Nella's chalk course and Lina's lettering remain available without forced interaction.

**Skip/re-entry:** the objective and map lead persist. Inspecting the bag says *Lunch packed. Eight coins of your own. Tavio brought work notices.* No household deadline begins. The player may explore freely before approaching the wagon.

## F5-M01B — a wagon too cheerful for its corner

**Entry:** first approach to Carrier Yard from any direction. **State:** no compulsory conversation or movement lock before the player sees the situation.

A cart wheel is pinned against a stone lip. Its load of tins makes a descending musical complaint each time Tavio tries to shift it. Pavo, the porter, is laughing too hard to finish his useful advice. Bera has stopped her hammer in the doorway. An owned Cragmantle works a hauling line from the broad yard, with room for all six feet; it has not somehow fitted through the residential stairs. Its keeper waits for the creature to test the ground before asking for the pull. The asset and keeper detail belong to [Opening Creatures and Commerce](../03%20Game%20Design/Opening%20Creatures%20and%20Commerce.md).

**Pavo:** “It went in.”

**Tavio:** “Yes. I was here for that part.”

**Bera:** “Take off the left basket.”

**Tavio:** “It's holding up the right basket.”

The player can help release the painted side basket, indicate the broad approach to Bera, or just watch the workers solve it. No mandatory strength check or inventory quest. The final pull unloads a shower of harmless ribbons, and Tavio catches one tin as if that was the only thing at risk.

**Tavio, if helped:** “There. A person who sees the whole catastrophe.”

**Tavio, if watched:** “An excellent arrival. People will remember the stock.”

**You:** “Did you bring the work notices?”

**Tavio:** “Better. I brought the person who wrote one.”

He points through the weighing porch to Pell's blue cloth, pinned beneath a mug and a stone.

**Tavio:** “Pell Arven. Surveying. They pay in actual money, which I find refreshing.”

**You:** “You paid me in hinges.”

**Tavio:** “And have you run out?”

**Objective refinement:** *Ask Pell about the survey. Look for the blue cloth beside the route board.* The map marks a person with a reason, not an unexplained exclamation point. If Pell was already met, replace the referral with **“You found them, then. Good. Ask about the bed.”** Do not replay the introduction.

Clatter's optional crossing is visible nearby, separate from the heavy cart route. The yard also has real stock to inspect, a visitor caring for the magnificent working creature, a parcels counter and two exits. Give the player several credible curiosities, not a corridor ending at Pell.

## F5-M01C — Clatter decides the audience is unnecessary

**Entry:** the player approaches the short plank while Clatter is refusing it. **Ownership:** RS-C01 in the creature page owns invitation state and decline/revisit behavior; this scene supplies the spoken introduction. Tavio's job referral does not require it.

Pavo claps once to encourage the Bellwether; three bystanders take this as an invitation to join. Clatter plants its feet and turns away from the plank. A scratch post is visible on the far side.

**Tavio:** “It crossed twice before everyone decided to help.”

Options are actions: **Ask for room**, **Open the wide side gate**, **Watch**, **Leave**. Asking the audience to step back clears the view. Opening the gate creates an ordinary alternative. Neither requires buying food or guessing a dialogue keyword. Watch lets Clatter edge away until the same opportunities remain clear.

**You, after asking for room:** “Give it somewhere to look that isn't us.”

Pavo moves aside. Clatter crosses, immediately scratches itself, then discovers the player is still there.

**Pavo:** “All that dignity. For an itch.”

Away from the little crowd, the Bellwether presses its shoulder twice and backs off. The player can offer a scratch or space. If the invitation becomes available:

**You:** “Is it yours?”

**Tavio:** “We travel together. It stays up here when I take the marsh road.”

**You:** “I'm starting with the Bell Road.”

**Tavio:** “Then take a short walk together. See what it thinks of your pace.”

The following introduction is a voluntary partnership, not Tavio rewarding a useful errand with ownership. He explains the travel refuge in one concrete demonstration and leaves a recognizable return contact. A declined invitation leaves Clatter comfortable at the yard, without a sorrow animation or pressure to reconsider. No partner is needed to accept Pell's job.

An optional sign and Tavio's short invitation point to **Mara's first match at the Pass House practice court, via Rope Walk**. Offer this immediately after the short companion walk, before the long local-story loop. A player who declined Clatter can borrow the practice partner. Use F5-B01 in [Opening Battle Encounters](../03%20Game%20Design/Opening%20Battle%20Encounters.md); the later cistern sign points to the same match, not a duplicate reward.

## F5-M02A — the job is small enough to understand

**Entry:** talk to Pell at the route board, directly or after Tavio. **Visible before dialogue:** a real route sketch, three empty observation boxes and a wagon-width gauge. No fictional words such as resonance or accord are required to understand the job.

Pell redraws a bend after looking past the player at a cart trying to turn. They move the cup before its wet ring reaches the drawing.

**Pell:** “Looking for the fair map?”

**You:** “Work. Tavio said you need someone on the road.”

If approached directly: **“Work. Is that your notice?”**

**Pell:** “A short survey. Cistern tap, upper marker, wind-cut bend. Then bring the sheet to the exchange.”

**You:** “Checking what?”

**Pell:** “Can travelers get water? Can they find the road in both directions? Is the bend safe to pass?”

They place three small wooden tokens on the corresponding marks. Each flashes its landmark on the optional route preview, then the map closes.

**Pell:** “Write what you find. Don't write what you think I want.”

**You:** “If the road's broken?”

**Pell:** “Tell me where. I still pay you.”

**You:** “How much?”

**Pell:** “Thirty-six. Twelve now, twenty-four when you report. Tools, lunch shelter and a bed if weather holds you up are covered.”

**You:** “And after this?”

**Pell:** “A signed field reference. Other surveyors know my name. Make sure they have a reason to remember yours.”

The job card shows **three observations → report to Pell at the Bell Road exchange → 36 total, including 12 advance**. It also says **Unsafe findings are paid. No repairs or battles required.** This is the core promise; neither a tooltip nor an optional lore page should be its only source.

Choices: **Review terms with Ada**, **I'll come back**, **Ask where the exchange is**. If Ada has already read the card through an earlier loop, offer **Accept** directly. Declining leaves the fair and job available; no one rebukes the player for shopping first.

**Directions:** **“Through the east gate, past the paired chestnuts. Both marked paths reach the red-roofed exchange. I'll go ahead after we agree.”** That answer and the route sketch remain available in the journal.

## F5-M02B — Ada reads the part people skip

Ada has moved her work to a small market stand supported by two stools, where potential customers can see the stitching but the largest cover still sags. If F5-S01 is complete, show its actual school-table or schoolroom arrangement instead. She remains callable at the open market-side door and steps to her ordinary terms-review spot. If the player approaches her at home before that move, use the same exchange there; set `survey_terms_reviewed` once. She does not teleport during a visible conversation.

**Ada:** “Read me the money.”

**You:** “Twelve now. Twenty-four when the report's accepted.”

**Ada:** “Accepted for being true, or accepted for being useful?”

**You:** “True. A closed road pays the same.”

She points to the bed provision.

**Ada:** “Good. Keep four from the advance. Eight here. Same eight at settlement; the rest is yours for the next road.”

**You:** “That gets the roof timber?”

**Ada:** “With my harness order. Yes.”

**You:** “Lina said you were charging more.”

**Ada:** “Lina made the price board. Everyone can read it now.”

Lina, if present, does not look up from her brush.

**Lina:** “That was rather the point.”

The player may ask **“What if I come back later?”** Ada answers **“Send a word with the exchange. We're covered for this posting.”** There is no hidden hunger system. **“I might buy something for myself.”** receives **“I should hope so.”** Neither answer changes household affection.

On return to Pell, show the exact advance split before confirming. Once accepted: home contribution +8, travel purse +4, contract active; one signed sheet and borrowed fastening kit added. Repeat dialogue cannot pay again. Pell leaves for the exchange by the broad gate path when out of conversation; an informational note at the vacated blue cloth says where to find them.

**Objective:** *Survey three places, then report at the red-roofed exchange. First: the public tap at Cistern Crown, above Bell Court.*

## F5-M03A — the village is part of the road

The route to Cistern Crown passes activity rather than collecting arbitrary quest stamps: a smith checking a cart rim, Dema opening jars of herbs, Lina arguing over legibility, a Thimbleback taking something worthless extremely seriously. Optional stories can begin here; accepting one does not replace the survey objective without the player's choice.

At the public tap, a cup catches a steady clear stream. Behind a separate fence, fallen leaves obstruct the wash-water outlet. Water intake and waste drainage are visibly different systems, as the town atlas requires.

**You:** “Tap's running. People can reach it from the ramp.”

Survey action **Record water access** records the actual observation immediately. Inspecting the leaf grate adds an optional maintenance note. Clearing it may make a local keeper pleased, but has no effect on eligibility for the wage and needs no crafted tool.

If the player keeps inspecting without recording, the sheet itself offers **Record what you observed**, rather than requiring a special third button hidden in the environment. The objective changes to *Follow the carrying incline to the east gate. Find the upper Bell Road marker.*

The cistern sign points along Rope Walk to Mara's **Pass House practice court, RS-08b**. If the player has not already tried the early invitation, offer the same F5-B01 fixture in [Opening Battle Encounters](../03%20Game%20Design/Opening%20Battle%20Encounters.md). Do not stage a match beside the protected water or ask the player to repeat a completed lesson.

**Mara:** “Want to try your partner against mine?”

**You:** “For the survey?”

**Mara:** “For the pleasure of winning. Or the practice of losing.”

She lends a partner if needed. Decline, defeat or surrender returns control beside the route with the survey intact. Win acknowledgment: **“You watched its feet. Good.”** If the player won by another plan, use **“All right. You've earned that look.”** Do not assert a strategy the player never used.

## F5-M03B — farther than a delivery

The east gate opens on a view of the carrying road below, village roofs behind, and the first mineral-white shelves ahead. A cart passes heading inward, carrying mismatched household chairs: one new resident's move, a reminder that some people choose to come here. The driver and walking route remain safely separate at the broad verge.

At the upper marker, the uphill face reads **14**. Its downhill face is partly hidden by a tall seasonal plant. Inspecting either establishes the marker; moving to the other face provides the useful second observation. No invisible pixel or mandatory guess.

**You:** “Fourteen.” Only after inspecting the hidden reverse face add **“Visible going down. Coming back, that plant is in the way.”** The reverse-visibility note is a separate observed fact, not granted for reading one face.

The player records it or can first fold the stalk aside with the durable kit. The optional correction earns a specific acknowledgment, never extra mandatory labor. A ribbon shows the long contour path; steps lead toward the shorter bend. Both reach the second marker and exchange.

Between them, Vela studies hoof marks beside hooked seeds. She is occupied before the player arrives.

**Vela:** “Brackenstride. Feeding here, then crossing toward the orchard.”

Options: **“Those tracks go the other way.”**, **“Can I look?”**, **Keep walking**. The questioning line prompts a moment of stubbornness, then investigation.

**Vela:** “The seeds are fresh.”

**You:** “So are the tracks.”

She follows the marks, returns with a seed-covered pack strap, and holds it between finger and thumb.

**Vela:** “A remarkably persuasive animal.”

**You:** “Are you changing the drawing?”

**Vela:** “The caption. The drawing was excellent.”

She offers a genuine creature lead: a family has crossed at the lower scrub path, recognizable by paired hoof scuffs and cropped shoots. The sighting is optional and gives no guaranteed mount. Her name becomes known through **“Vela. I'll be at the exchange if you find something less convincing.”** Missed introduction: the later orchard scene supplies her name and never assumes friendship.

## F5-M03C — something is loose, something is wrong

At the wind-cut bend a short plank on the footpath rocks against its fastening. The carrying contour remains intact. The second marker reads **14**, while Pell's sketch labels it **16**. A dropped scrap of old red marking thread is nearby; it establishes recent maintenance, not the cause of the numbering discrepancy.

Player approaches: **Secure the loose fastening** using the supplied kit; **Ask the passing road worker to hold it** and complete the same ordinary action together; **Take the longer contour** and record the footpath issue. None requires a companion or a minigame with repeated failure. A missed traversal returns to the lower verge, never a death pit. The creature reactions are personality cues, not exclusive solutions.

**You, reading marker:** “Another fourteen.”

Inspect the sheet. Its **16** remains legible.

**You:** “I'll keep both.”

**Record marker and condition** stores numeral 14, expected 16, and footing secured or bypassed. A player who does not compare the sketch still records the numeral; Pell later identifies the discrepancy without accusing them of missing a hidden test.

The journal now says *Three observations recorded. Deliver the report to Pell at the red-roofed exchange, downhill past the fork.* If one observation is missing, it names that landmark. The route marker never points the player back to Tavio or home by accident.

Optional first wild resistance belongs to the lookout spur beside Strap Shelter: **F5-B04 / RS-C10**, fully specified in [Opening Battle Encounters](../03%20Game%20Design/Opening%20Battle%20Encounters.md). An established Thimbleback warns the player away from its cache. Observe, give space, bypass, or explicitly challenge with a companion. The fixture defines its cards, HP, intentions, retreat and recovery. A player can reach the exchange and earn the full base wage without winning a fight; the separate Vergecat glimpse never launches this encounter.

## F5-M04A — a report someone uses

The exchange has a public trough, a long porch, a cart turning circle and an indoor rest bench. Asta, the steward, chalks **FOOTPATH** and **CART ROAD** on separate slates. Pell is visible beside the report table. If asked, Asta points rather than sending the player through a new introductory errand.

Pavo's ankle is already wrapped when first seen here. On the journey ahead he stepped off the unloading board onto a loose stone; Tavio stopped work and Asta arranged rest. No unseen creature attack or player-caused accident is implied. Optional inspection gets **Pavo: “The board was sound. The stone was ambitious.”** and **Asta: “He's staying off it. We have other hands.”** This establishes the later handling vacancy without starting a rescue quest or delaying the report.

**Asta:** “Pell's at the end. If it's bad news, put it where they can see it.”

**You:** “And good news?”

**Asta:** “Same place. Saves us all a walk.”

On presenting all three observations:

**Pell:** “Start with the water.”

**You:** “Running. Ramp's clear.”

Only if the optional grate note was recorded, add **“Leaves in the separate wash outlet.”** A skipped inspection is not narrated as evidence the player collected.

**Pell:** “Good. The bend?”

Player's recorded approach controls the line: **“Fastening's secure now.”** / **“Loose foot plank. I used the cart contour.”** Pell writes either **CHECK AFTER NEXT LOAD** or **FOOTPATH REPAIR; CART ROAD OPEN**. Asta moves a physical route token, giving the report an immediate visible use.

**You:** “Both markers say fourteen.”

**Pell:** “Both?”

They place the two observations side by side instead of erasing one.

**Pell:** “I'll check the replacement list. You were right to keep what you saw.”

If the uphill plant was noted: **“That's useful. Whoever cut it last was walking downhill.”** If not, omit; do not grade curiosity as failure.

Pell signs the reference with the protagonist's actual name, pauses to ask its spelling if necessary, then offers it to the player to read. This is a tangible reward, not a promise that the real game begins after more errands.

**Pell:** “Your first report. You can show that at either city's field office.”

**You:** “It's only one page.”

**Pell:** “It says you finished a job.”

Pay the remaining 24 once: +8 household reserve, +16 purse. The player now has 28 personal coins before purchases and optional earnings: starting 8 plus 20 from this contract. The whole base commission is complete, independent of further bookings. Travel supplies are returned or transferred to the general free-loan kit explicitly; no surprise deposit is deducted.

**New freedom card:** *You have your wage and reference. Explore the exchange, take another job, return to the fair, or plan your first city journey.*

## F5-M04B — other people have ambitions too

Three small invitations make the exchange a place rather than a quest desk. None starts automatically after payment.

**Jori's bench:** Jori rehearses a speech to stacked baskets while checking straps. A creature noses one basket over at the worst pause.

**Jori:** “Difficult audience. Excellent timing.”

**You:** “Is it a serious play?”

**Jori:** “At the moment, the baskets seem to think so.”

He invites the player to an evening rehearsal in Rocca, where carrying work has paid for one rented stage cloth. If later hired to help Tavio, the script acknowledges the job, not a magical simultaneous appearance elsewhere. Optional performance assistance belongs to the residents page.

**Rovan's short demonstration:** Rovan secures a leaning practice load with two companions, letting one hold while the other adjusts. He notices the player's signed sheet.

**Rovan:** “Pell let you keep the bad news?”

**You:** “Paid me for it.”

**Rovan:** “Good. Someone always wants the road open on paper.”

He offers **F5-B03, Rovan's two-partner challenge**, as specified in [Opening Battle Encounters](../03%20Game%20Design/Opening%20Battle%20Encounters.md), never a mandatory toll. The nearby washing-line flag exercise is the distinct F5-B02. Here he is competent and likable. His later wrongdoing will be a choice, not something a sinister camera move announces today.

**Pell and Tavio:** the actual Two Bookings invitations wait as visible tasks. Read their scripts before staging the choice; the porter is cared for, the job terms are explicit, and no imaginary emergency makes the less profitable option compulsory.

## F5-M06A — coming home to a different afternoon

**Entry:** after either booking's outcome, or the explicit choice **Return for the evening fair** after declining both. Set Booking Resolved with its selected result, then Evening Return at the town approach. Do not advance these phases merely because the player wandered for an hour. The old optional city-departure route remains available after the report; returning is the representative route, not a prison gate.

The broad gate smells of warm bread. The ribbons that fell from Tavio's wagon are now tied above the weighing porch. Bera's repaired cart has moved; the formerly blocked doorway is open. Dema has swapped packed herb bundles for empty jars and winter orders. A market child is asleep under the table where the argument was this morning.

At least three changes depend on actual optional work: Ada's shared table, the creature refuge or roost, the delivered parcel, the smith's chosen repair arrangement. Untouched stories have their own evening condition and another invitation; the town did not freeze while waiting for a protagonist. [Rocca Residents and Side Stories](Rocca%20Residents%20and%20Side%20Stories.md) owns those branches.

**Pavo, if wagon completed:** “There you are. The load arrived with fewer complaints than the merchant.”

**Pavo, if wagon withdrawn:** “Tavio hired the relay hands. They brought it the rest of the way.”

**Pavo, if orchard completed or withdrawn:** “Jori made the wagon. We heard the whole speech on the way.”

**Pavo, if neither:** “We managed. You should've seen Tavio negotiating for an extra pair of hands.”

Nobody punishes the player for earning better money or choosing a quieter afternoon. Existing commitment outcomes still matter: Tavio's forgone **future wholesale cloth order** and Pell's earned introduction reflect the actual branch and completion state. Tavio's currently purchasable retail stock stays available except for actual purchases, as the commerce ledger specifies.

## F5-M06B — an ordinary evening worth keeping

At home, Ada has cleared space on the table for supper. A repaired harness hangs ready for collection. Household support has been recorded through the contract; this scene acknowledges it without paying or subtracting the money again.

**Ada:** “Did they take the report?”

**You:** “Signed it.”

She dries her hands before taking the reference.

**Ada:** “Your name looks grown up in someone else's handwriting.”

**You:** “Don't show everyone.”

**Ada:** “Then don't leave it where I can reach it.”

Lina wants to know whether the exchange signs were any good. Tobin asks about feet. Nella asks whether the good shirt has acquired any useful holes. Their responses depend on sightings and optional story outcomes; no invented adventure is inserted because a player skipped a creature.

Player picks one account to share: **The creature**, **The work**, **The road**, or **Keep it brief**. The first uses an actual seen creature; the second mentions the completed report; the third names the chosen bend route; brief gets a welcome without interrogation. Full sibling reactions belong in the residents page.

**You:** “I'm going to the rehearsal.”

**Ada:** “In the shirt?”

**You:** “It was packed for evenings.”

The small public performance is playable participation or watching, with no mandatory rhythm proficiency. A companion may take a harmless cue; a simple prop does the same job for a player traveling alone. Finish with laughter, the awkward recovery of a missed cue, or a modest success. A friendly evening is not retrospectively revealed to have been an evil simulation.

## F5-M06C — a destination, not another instruction list

After supper or rehearsal, the player can visit the route board. Two visible route sketches lead to the places already glimpsed from home: Istravel's suspended gardens eastward, Orthe's broad stair courts through the western approach. A smaller notice offers another local day. Passage terms appear before selection; a free foot route remains possible if the player spent every optional coin.

**You:** “I used to know where every cart was going.”

If Ada is present: **“Now you can tell us where you went.”** If alone, let the view and the actual route choice conclude the scene without an invented companion speaker.

**Choose:** *Prepare for Istravel* / *Prepare for Orthe* / *Stay another day*. This records a plan, not an irreversible faction pledge. Confirming departure starts the next regional chapter. Reconsidering leaves both cities accessible; unfinished local invitations persist under their stated return rules.

The last objective of this package should be a destination the player wants: *Travel to the city you chose. Your reference will get you to its field office; the rest is yours to discover.* Home remains reachable. The first five hours have supplied a finished job, an optional commitment with consequences, several relationships and a reason to imagine more.
