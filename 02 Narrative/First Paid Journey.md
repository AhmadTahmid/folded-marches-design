# The first paid journey

The user established a young man of eighteen or nineteen who already helps support his mother and three younger siblings, and leaves to improve their material circumstances while aching for more himself. Nineteen is the working age here. The reason his father is absent remains open. This commission, dialogue, and its outcomes are proposals developing [Personal Story](Personal%20Story.md), after [Bells Before Breakfast](Quests/Bells%20Before%20Breakfast.md).

## Leaving with an arrangement

His bag is open on the kitchen table. He has packed the better shirt beneath his work clothes. His mother finds it while looking for room for a wrapped lunch.

“For surveying?”

“They might have evenings.”

“I hope they do.”

She folds it again without teasing him. He expected to have to defend wanting something handsome. Now he has to admit that he wants the journey itself, too. The player can say that plainly, describe the work first, or keep the conversation practical. The narration never decides that every restrained answer conceals shame.

They review an actual departure arrangement: his agreed wage advance, her continuing work, essential household expenses already covered through the first commission, and who can help with an unexpected repair. He has contributed to the plan; he has not appointed himself sole decision-maker. They agree where correspondence can reach him and when to review the arrangement after he reports back. Nothing here becomes a daily hunger meter.

One sibling asks for something from the lowlands. Another wants to know who will now help with a troublesome school task. These are different needs. He can promise something small, offer a workable substitute, or admit uncertainty. A specific promise can matter later without every affectionate exchange becoming a binding quest.

## Work he can actually accept

Before the packing scene, at the reopening fair, [Pell](../01%20World/People%20of%20the%20Marches.md#pell-arven) offers a finite route-condition survey along the first reach of the [Bell Road](../01%20World/Atlas.md). Check the public markers, note shelter and water access, and deliver a usable report to the next exchange. Acceptance of the report completes this small commission; returning home or joining a further expedition follows a separate decision. The protagonist takes the terms home to discuss before accepting the advance and packing. This page develops one optional follow-on posting, not a compulsory extra job or a replacement for Mara's stewardship.

Pell explains the base payment, covered travel necessities, and what makes a report acceptable. Reporting an unsafe section earns the same base wage as reporting a clear one. The advance is part of that wage, not a hidden debt tied to accepting future jobs. Refusing a dangerous approach does not erase completed work.

He asks about money before asking whether the route is beautiful.

“Good,” Pell says. “Ask both.”

This gives his existing competence a place to show. He notices a marker visible from the downhill approach but hidden to someone returning uphill. Pell adds his observation to the shared sheet with his name beside it. Recognition is a small pleasure he did not budget for.

## A road worth taking slowly

The road descends through wind gardens toward the first view of Istravel's elevated water. An optional side path reaches a sheltered overlook. Kitefins settle beneath a stone lip while moving clouds reveal successive terraces below. Their behavior gives the player a useful weather observation; it does not predict the weather infallibly.

Fresh hooked seeds caught on a low fence and paired hoof scuffs lead to a Brackenstride family feeding in sunlit scrub. Watching from the marked path reveals the adults' warning stamps before a youngster bounds across a gap. The journal records a place, conditions, and recognizable signs. This sighting can begin a later partnership investigation without awarding an immediate mount or requiring a catch. Missing the overlook loses neither wages nor the opportunity to learn the species elsewhere.

A keeper at the exchange offers an optional supervised crossing exercise. This example follows two friendly partners, Bellwether and Thimbleback; earlier companionship choices can substitute other partners or supported practice with the keeper. No compulsory starter or full five-creature roster is assumed.

The exercise's announced objective is to bring a cloth marker out of the practice enclosure. Bellwether holds the front while Thimbleback waits behind it. The defender clearly intends to Strike Bellwether for 6 damage after Armor. Guard's 8-point Barrier absorbs that blow; Thimbleback then spends its activation retrieving the marker, ending the exercise. The objective is reachable from its marked rear position. That interaction belongs to this encounter and replaces an ordinary action. It grants no extra protagonist turn. The pleasure is giving two partners different jobs and seeing the plan work. [Tactical Combat](../03%20Game%20Design/Tactical%20Combat.md) owns the underlying rules; numerical balance and alternate teams still need testing.

## An opportunity and an inconvenience

After the base report is accepted, Pell offers a separately paid extension: accompany a demonstration of lowland surveying equipment. It includes a modest bonus and an introduction to someone who hires field assistants. The protagonist wants both. The player can explore first; the next passage begins only when they choose to advance the departure scene.

[Tavio](../01%20World/People%20of%20the%20Marches.md#tavio-rusk) then explains that his porter has strained an ankle. The porter has shelter and care. Tavio needs help restowing the wagon and taking it to a nearer safe stopping place. Other help is available at a price he can pay, but it will consume the profit he hoped to make from this leg. He admits that plainly instead of pretending somebody will die without the protagonist.

Two commitments now occupy the same agreed work window:

- **Take Pell's extension.** Earn the bonus and introduction. Tavio pays for local help and reduces his planned stock purchase. He can be disappointed without declaring the protagonist selfish. Sending family money and pursuing a career are valid reasons to accept.
- **Help Tavio.** Preserve the base wage and reference, but forgo this bonus and demonstration. Tavio cannot match Pell's offer; he pays a smaller agreed handling fee and teaches a useful packing and route-planning lesson. Their relationship deepens without secretly providing a larger financial reward.

The exact terms are visible before commitment. No superior hidden answer lets him obtain both appointments, and neither choice endangers his family's immediate support. Later work remains available; this is a missed opportunity or a costly favor, not permanent career failure.

## Money, a letter, and another departure

At settlement, the agreed household contribution is arranged. The remaining earnings are his to consider: send more, keep a travel reserve, or buy something he wanted. The script can acknowledge generosity, prudence, or pleasure without ranking one as the only adult answer.

His first letter can be honest and affectionate: work completed, a choice made, something beautiful seen, an uncertainty he has not solved. Choosing privacy is allowed. A reassuring reply should respond to what he actually wrote rather than exposing supposedly hidden thoughts.

Returning to Rocca Selva reveals a household that kept living. His mother has completed work he never saw; a sibling has found another helper; somebody has moved his chair. He can feel pleased and briefly displaced at once. He brings support and a story home, then decides what work to seek next. Leaving again is part of their continuing relationship.
