# Two bookings — two different afternoons

This chapter expands the existing optional commission in [First Paid Journey](First%20Paid%20Journey.md) and campaign quest 3. It follows the fully paid survey in [Opening Day Script](Opening%20Day%20Script.md). The player can choose one booking or neither. Accepting a conversation is not committing the work window; the explicit departure confirmation is. Both routes eventually return to the same evening with different money, knowledge and acknowledgments.

**Package rules:** [First Five Hours](First%20Five%20Hours.md) owns payment and phase names. No hidden elapsed-time deadline, compulsory owned creature, family emergency or guaranteed superior third solution. Early failures preserve the base survey wage, collected observations and relationships. Additional wage is paid once on completing this booking; the player may retreat and resume before final withdrawal. Partial notes/cargo stay in their named safe state.

## F5-M05-CHOICE — read the two offers

Pell pins a narrow card beneath the signed survey. Across the yard Pavo sits with his ankle supported, rolling a loose strap into a practiced coil. Tavio has already fetched water and arranged his bed. Asta tells a departing carrier that the porter will not be walking the load down the next bend.

**Pell:** “There's another paid afternoon, if you want it. White Orchard. We're taking a survey instrument above the mineral canopy.”

**You:** “Above it how?”

**Pell:** “An inspection cradle and a footway. You can use the footway.”

**You:** “That wasn't the question.”

Pell smiles and shows the drawing.

**Pell:** “Eighteen. Transport and supper covered. You help establish a safe sight line; Vela takes the observations. I'll introduce you to the basin field office.”

Tavio waits until the offer is complete before approaching. Do not make him interrupt with a fabricated crisis.

**Tavio:** “I've got an uglier offer.”

**Pavo:** “Shorter speech, though.”

**Tavio:** “Pavo's staying here. I need the wagon restowed and taken to the lower relay. Ten coins. I'll hire Jori if you take the orchard.”

**You:** “Can you afford him?”

**Tavio:** “Yes. I won't be buying the cloth I came for.”

**Pavo:** “He'll survive a week with fewer colors.”

The comparison shows **Orchard: 18, of which 4 home / 14 purse; new field contact, canopy expedition** and **Wagon: 10, of which 2 home / 8 purse; practical freight work, time with Tavio**. **Neither: base job already complete, explore or return to fair.** Both use the same scheduled work window. A choice cannot be annulled by walking back and accepting the other after its reward.

Optional question **“Could I do both?”**:

**Pell:** “The demonstration needs the afternoon light. The wagon needs the relay crew before they leave. Not this time.”

**Tavio:** “You're choosing a job. You aren't choosing who gets to like you.”

**Confirm departure** records `booking=orchard` or `booking=wagon`, changes the other job's staffing, and begins its route. Before that confirmation, both offers can be inspected freely. Returning to the fair without a booking requires its own clear choice. The game never interprets closing a dialogue box as rejection.

## Orchard branch — a path above the trees

### F5-M05-P1 — Vela packed the wrong comfortable shoes

**Place:** Split-Branch Track into White Orchard. **Cast:** Pell, Vela and the protagonist. A field attendant is available for equipment demonstration but requires no new biography to follow the story. **Objective:** *Reach the Listening Shelf with the instrument case. Follow Vela's white trail ribbons; they mark the walking route, not a climbing requirement.*

The borrowed case is worn equipment, not a physics object that can be dropped irretrievably. Its handle has four repairs. Vela carries a beautiful padded lens bag that keeps hitting her knee.

**You:** “Yours looks expensive.”

**Vela:** “It is.”

**You:** “Does it stop doing that if you pay more?”

She changes shoulders.

**Vela:** “Apparently not.”

If previously met, a brief callback: **“Any more persuasive luggage?”** / **“I've forbidden the bags from contributing.”** Otherwise: **“Vela Cotrin. Pell says you kept both marker readings.”** No memory of an earlier conversation is invented.

The first orchard view is a shallow basin of pale mineral trunks and living scrub. A Glassgrazer moves through it, close enough to hear deposited fans brushing the branches. Several fans catch the sunlight without becoming an abstract laser puzzle. A narrow upper footway and the inspection cradle both reach the canopy station. Vela immediately wants the higher view; Pell checks the supports first.

### F5-M05-P2 — the clear path is someone else's breakfast

**Place:** White Orchard Listening Shelf. The expected approach crosses a temporary feeding route. The herd is not a hostile random encounter. Two adults are using familiar scratch-stones beside the loaded inspection line, and the noisy hanging instrument hood makes one keep backing into the path.

**Pell:** “We can't swing a case over those animals.”

**Vela:** “There was room when the route was marked.”

**You:** “There isn't now.”

**Pell:** “Then that's the useful thing we found.”

**Field objective:** establish one safe approach. Three observable details are sufficient: the hood taps the line in the wind; the animals settle when it is held still; the worn scratch-stone lies away from the main walking route. Each detail has a readable inspection description. No creature encyclopedia quiz.

**Approach A — quiet the rig.** Fold and fasten the empty hood, then ask the attendant to slide the unused inspection basket to its marked parking hook. The creatures finish rubbing and the marked approach clears. Two deliberate interactions; no repeatable resource grind. The instrument stays packed until the crossing is clear.

**Approach B — take the old footway.** Ask Pell for the longer route, open the existing pedestrian latch, and climb a series of broad mineral shelves with ordinary rails and resting places. A low gap can be crossed by the placed board; a mobility-friendly carrying path joins the same final station. The player sees the herd below instead of moving it. Any invitation involving herd access remains available from its own public encounter; choosing this route is not a permanent loss of the species.

**Approach C — optional keeper exercise after clearance.** A keeper can offer a supervised two-partner practice using the already authored flag exercise. It is a separate pleasure, not the method for forcing wildlife out of the way or a hidden wage condition. Borrowing a partner is free. Do not give a capture for winning.

If the player unsettles the animals by reopening the rattling hood, the local scene resets to its visible starting arrangement after they step to the observation rail. No injury, lost wage or hostile herd pursuit is invented. The invitation to a willing Glassgrazer belongs to RS-C08 in [Opening Creatures and Commerce](../03%20Game%20Design/Opening%20Creatures%20and%20Commerce.md); recruitment is never a prerequisite for the expedition.

### F5-M05-P3 — the sky under your feet

**Place:** Mineral Canopy Walk. **Objective:** *Set the survey vane at the station, then record the two sight lines.* The climb is the adventure's main physical pleasure: broad pale branches, wind moving the scrub below, a suspended platform that feels substantial beneath the travelers, a glimpse back to Rocca on its ridge. Movement may be ambitious; it does not require a new combat system.

At the station Pell demonstrates the vane using the nearer village bell tower. The player aligns the visible slit with the tower and seats a pin in the matching marked notch. The farther sight line points toward Istravel's hanging channels. If manual alignment is inaccessible or irritating, **Use the marked alignment** completes the same action. A reference sketch and clear endpoint make this an observation interaction, not guesswork against an invisible tolerance.

**Vela:** “There. Do you see the water?”

**You:** “That's water?”

**Vela:** “The gardens are underneath.”

**You:** “I'd have put them above it.”

**Vela:** “Most people do.”

Give the player control of the view. No dialogue advances until they return to the station or choose to continue. They may discover birds using the protected underside of a branch, or spot the trade road and name the route they just walked. Do not fill the view with compulsory map markers.

A gust turns the loose proof sheet against the outside of the railing. It is a recoverable interruption, not an instant fail. Vela catches the frame while the player chooses the inner catch-line or the lower service ledge. Both approaches use visible handholds; falling returns to the last broad platform with evidence retained. An owned suitable companion can give a contextual assist, but the catch-line is always present. Recovering the sheet reveals that the first distant sketch counted a reflection as a second channel.

**Vela:** “I drew two.”

**You:** “There are two.”

**Vela:** “One of them is in the water.”

She laughs, then corrects it herself. This echoes her earlier error without treating her as perpetually incompetent: the observation is good enough that she can recognize what went wrong. Pell's demonstration succeeds with one verified near line and one corrected distant line. A rushed alignment produces an explicit **Check this sight line** state and a chance to adjust it, never invented evidence of sabotage.

### F5-M05-P4 — credit written while everyone is present

At the sheltered table Vela writes the observations. The protagonist points out which line he took and where the animal route interrupted the planned approach.

**Vela:** “This bit's yours. How do you want it credited?”

Options: **Named field observation**, **Keep my name off it**, **Ask to see the wording first**. The wording is concrete: *Access and second sight line checked with [name].* Asking to see it leads to that same choice, not a penalty. No early dialogue automatically grants coauthorship of all her future work.

**You, named:** “That spelling. Pell already had to ask.”

**Vela:** “Then I'll get it right before this becomes expensive to correct.”

If anonymous: **“All right. I'll credit the observation without your name.”** Her later publication must respect the recorded choice. The household situation is not turned into a lesson about how privileged she is; future relationship writing can let them disagree more naturally.

Pell pays the additional 18 once and supplies the promised field-office introduction. The **new resource** is a named appointment opportunity at the Istravel basin field office, not an obligatory city order. The public footway and orchard return route are recorded; a willing creature encounter can be revisited even if recruitment was declined. Tavio's afternoon result is honest: he hired Jori, his wagon reached the relay, and he bought less cloth.

**Pell:** “Go and have an evening. You've done the work.”

**Vela:** “If Jori's rehearsing, save me a place where I can't be volunteered.”

**You:** “You could tell him you're working.”

**Vela:** “I've just been forbidden.”

Return via the exchange or the public trail. Confirming **Head back for the evening fair** begins the authored evening phase; further orchard exploration does not secretly consume the performance.

**Withdrawal:** before either verified sight line, leave the unpaid extension open for resumption or explicitly cancel it. After one line, retain it. If abandoning the booked demonstration, record `orchard_withdrawn`; Pell arranges another assistant, no extension wage or introduction is awarded, and the already earned survey reference remains valid. The competing wagon window is still unavailable. Its later public route can be explored normally.

## Wagon branch — the road has room for ridiculous things

### F5-M05-T1 — what stays in the wagon

**Place:** Three-Awning Exchange loading porch. **Objective:** *Restow three loads, then escort Tavio to the Lower Relay.* Pavo remains seated; asking him to work on the injury is not an option required for a good outcome.

**Pavo:** “Tools low. Jars in the sprung rack. Cloth wherever Tavio says it looks worst.”

**Tavio:** “People have to see what they're buying.”

**Pavo:** “The road isn't buying it.”

There are three visibly different cargo spaces: low central tool well, cushioned jar rack and dry cloth shelf. The three persistent load IDs are **tools** (a small hooped tool barrel), **jars** (a padded crate) and **cloth** (a dry wrapped bale). The barrel in the later recovery scene is this same tools load, not a fourth cargo object. The interface describes their needs directly; putting a load in an unsuitable place produces Pavo's plain warning before departure. This is a short tactile arranging scene, not a recurring weight simulation or a puzzle with one uncommunicated solution.

The optional decision is where to put Tavio's bright festival cloth. Tuck it inside for weather protection or tie a narrow display strip outside. Either is secure. The outer display produces a comic ribbon incident at the bend, with no extra wage or hidden punishment; the covered version shows Tavio conceding the point and later proudly presenting uncreased stock. This is a characterful difference worth seeing rather than a disguised correct answer.

**You:** “Why keep coming up here?”

**Tavio:** “People buy things.”

**You:** “Not enough for you to pay Jori without losing the cloth.”

He checks a knot before answering.

**Tavio:** “I carry the medicine on this run. The pretty things help pay for it.”

**You:** “And the hinges?”

**Tavio:** “Those are apparently a sound investment.”

Do not expose his entire later debt plot in the opening. The player learns that trade has material connections and that the man likes selling beautiful nonsense. Medical deliveries continue even if the player did not choose him.

### F5-M05-T2 — a load with its own idea of downhill

**Place:** the Lower Relay spur off the public cart road. It has an upper rest, a broad turning ledge and a lower catch apron, all visible from the first overlook. The safe walking contour connects them. Exact placement belongs to the [Detailed Atlas](../01%20World/Rocca%20Selva%20Detailed%20Atlas.md).

The wagon's wheel drops into a shallow worn rut at the turning ledge. A strapped barrel tilts but remains on its secondary line. Tavio braces the handle. The event stops advancing while the player inspects or chooses an action; animation communicates urgency without a hidden input timer. The public route remains an ordinary place, not a mandatory permadeath trap.

**Tavio:** “Don't pull it toward me. I'm at the bottom.”

**You:** “I noticed.”

**Tavio:** “You'd be astonished.”

Three named interaction targets explain the scene: **holding cleat** beside the player, **wheel chock** on the inside of the curve, **turning rope** on the upper stone post. The load needs two things before it can move safely: the wheel held, and tension transferred to the post.

**Approach A:** place the chock from the supplied kit, transfer the rope to the post, then release the hand line on Tavio's cue. The player gets to see the weight change and the wheel settle. No extra item purchase is needed.

**Approach B:** ask the relay attendant for the fixed hand winch, connect its visible hook, then operate it from the upper ledge while Tavio handles the chock. A short accessible ramp reaches the control. Help costs Tavio the already declared use fee within his operating costs, never a surprise deduction from the player's ten coins.

A companion may assist one ordinary step: a bracing creature holds while the player places the chock, or a nimble partner brings the nearby line. The safety of a mandatory route never depends on who joined. The effect is a memorable animation and some companionship, not a third creature-exclusive solution with strictly better pay.

An incorrect release moves the barrel into the lower catch apron. Nobody is hit. The short recovery loop follows the safe contour, reattaches the existing hauling line and returns it to the wagon. Tavio comments on the actual mishap; the agreed fee remains available after recovery. Walking away leaves the barrel visibly secured and the booking resumable. This failure is a new small situation, not replaying the entire journey.

### F5-M05-T3 — the village that buys one of everything

At the Lower Relay a few travelers meet the wagon: a family carrying lamp housings from Orthe, a woman buying medicine for someone she discusses with practical familiarity, and a child interested only in the bright cloth. They are passersby, not six new compulsory quest givers. A modest covered meal table overlooks the approach.

If the cloth strip was exposed, it has wrapped neatly around a roadside peg. The child declares this a much better way to advertise. Tavio pretends to consider a business expansion. If stowed, he unfolds a flawless strip with a flourish; Pavo's advice gets a grudging acknowledgment later. Neither variation changes the stock ledger or pays twice.

**Buyer:** “Do you have the small jar?”

**Tavio:** “Two. Same price as last time.”

**Buyer:** “That's not like you.”

**Tavio:** “I'm diversifying.”

The delivery is completed when the tools barrel reaches the relay's dry tool bay, the padded jars reach its shaded receiving bench and the cloth bale reaches Tavio's covered display rail, then he confirms the manifest. Each load has one location and one receipt. The player can inspect the different city marks on useful goods without needing a political lecture. The imported tools, local chestnuts and outgoing repaired harnesses make the route's two-way traffic visible.

Tavio pays ten once, divided two to the household contribution and eight to the purse. He makes a practical introduction to the relay attendants: they will recognize the player on a later visit, point out a dry shortcut and let them use the public covered rest. Do not secretly attach a superior gold reward or rare guaranteed creature to compensate for choosing the lower wage.

**Tavio:** “Ten, as promised. Less than the orchard.”

**You:** “I read both offers.”

**Tavio:** “Good. I don't want gratitude doing my accounts.”

The player can ask about one city through its goods: Orthe's repairable fittings or Istravel's protected medicine jars. Tavio gives one vivid personal detail and a useful destination, not a faction essay. **“In Istravel, sit under the water garden when the carriers change shift. Everyone looks up at once.”** / **“Orthe's workshops paint the things they've repaired. From above it looks like the city keeps changing its mind.”** These are observations and atmosphere, not additional gate conditions.

### F5-M05-T4 — come back with something besides work

On the return, an animal uses the broad roadside verge. If the RS-C02 Thimbleback is actually traveling with the player, it carries something entirely inappropriate in its familiar proud posture. Otherwise a distinct wild individual provides the behavior without false recognition; an animal resting at a refuge is not teleported into this scene. Tavio stops talking long enough for the player to watch.

**You:** “Do you ever get used to them?”

**Tavio:** “Some. Then one puts a spoon in your boot.”

At the exchange, Pell's demonstration has happened without the protagonist. Vela can show a distant sketch at the evening fair, but this is not the missed paid fieldwork, its wage, its credit or its employer introduction. The public White Orchard remains visitable later, including its species encounters and routes. Jori completed his separately booked work, as established; he may now return for the rehearsal.

If the player mentions the missed canopy, Tavio answers **“Go another day. The orchard wasn't hired for the afternoon.”** Let the small disappointment exist alongside a good day. Confirming the evening return rejoins F5-M06.

**Withdrawal:** the player can leave at a named safe rest and explicitly cancel. Tavio hires help from that point, with no handling fee awarded for unfinished work. Secured cargo stays secured; unsafe cargo is put on the catch apron before departure is available. No partial failure changes the completed base survey, family reserve or protagonist's first reference. The orchard appointment does not reopen inside the already spent work window.

## Declining both — an afternoon of your own

The choice is available after both offers are legible, without having to listen to both speeches. **“I'm keeping the afternoon.”** receives Pell's **“Then don't spend it standing here.”** and Tavio's **“Come and look at the cloth before I fail to buy it.”** Neither line removes later work.

Set `booking=none`; confirm either **Keep exploring** or **Return for the evening fair**. The former leaves the afternoon geography and optional stories open until the player deliberately advances. The latter resolves staffing as Pell with another assistant and Tavio with Jori. Their outcomes are told as events in other lives, not failures the player caused. Wages remain the thirty-six already earned, and the next city can be chosen normally.

## Continuity facts to carry forward

Only one booking can be committed for this window; only its completed reward pays. Survey completion and household support are independent facts. Vela's observed evidence and actual credit choice persist separately from whether the player likes her. Tavio reduces his future wholesale cloth order on orchard/none branches and after wagon withdrawal to cover hired help; the completed wagon branch preserves that order. This never removes existing retail stock. Pavo is cared for in every branch. Public roads and later creature introductions remain accessible after a missed booking. The evening script must acknowledge the actual branch and withdrawal, never silently narrate both adventures as completed.
