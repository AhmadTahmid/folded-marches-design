# Rocca residents and side stories

Companion to [The First Five Hours](First%20Five%20Hours.md). This page supplies people to interrupt, jobs worth considering and an evening that remembers the morning. It owns six optional local stories, **F5-S01–F5-S06**. [Family at Home](Family%20at%20Home.md), [People of the Marches](../01%20World/People%20of%20the%20Marches.md) and [Traveling Cast](../01%20World/Traveling%20Cast.md) retain the existing biographies. New people, dialogue, prices and outcomes remain authored draft.

The interiors belong to the [detailed Rocca atlas](../01%20World/Rocca%20Selva%20Detailed%20Atlas.md): RS-I01 home; I02 Ruskell's workroom; I03 charcoal loft and roof garden; I05 schoolroom; I06 tower gallery; I07 smith; I08 Neri's Gutter Lane shop and rear porch; I09 bakehouse; I10 warm rooms. Bell Court's market worktable is RS-04a; Rope Walk's cloth pegs are RS-07a. [Opening Creatures and Commerce](../03%20Game%20Design/Opening%20Creatures%20and%20Commerce.md) owns creatures, invitations and shops. These stories and creature ownership are unnecessary for Pell's survey, departure or later endings.

## How to meet a village

The first walk needs three conspicuous things: Lina's sign drying across a household shutter, a handsome ridiculous cart on Bera's blocks, and steam escaping around a creature asleep in the cool doorway. People elsewhere remain discoverable. Do not stop movement to deliver this cast directory. A name enters the journal after an introduction, a transaction or a conversation the player chooses to join.

Spoken lines below appear one at a time with their speaker. Directions describe visible acting, not a Narration speaker. Accepting a story records one clear next destination. Declining keeps the person available without repeated pleading. A phase change never eats a half-finished paid job: its props remain or a note directs the player to the new location. The five journey phases advance through the main chapter's explicit events, not elapsed minutes.

Only three stories pay the protagonist: S01 **4 coins**, S02 **4 coins**, S03 **4 coins**. These are stated labor shares, granted once on either completed approach; no automatic household split applies. Ada, Lina and other residents can earn their own money without adding it to the player's purse. S04–S06 pay no coins and confer no shop discount. The total optional cash here is twelve coins. Recruitment, purchases and fair food have their own owners; no creature interaction pays these sums again.

## People before the player arrives

**Ada** has accepted a large weather-cover repair that her home table cannot hold flat. She has already measured, quoted and bought thread. She wants the finished work seen, and later wants an evening when nobody asks her to mend what they are wearing. Her market table displays an excellent repaired strap beside a cheap one she refuses to endorse. She keeps the son's contract review short and practical; the main script owns those terms. Optional approach line: “If that's Pell's offer, put it by the weight. If it's another sleeve, I am charging.”

**Lina, fourteen**, is lettering Ollo's new sign for a separately agreed three-coin fee. The flourishes are beautiful; the spacing on BAKERY has gone wrong. She has hidden the first version beneath her bed and is still deciding whether to fix it or claim the unevenness is intentional. She wants adults to ask before committing her time. She is not appointed replacement parent when her brother leaves. Her eventual print-shop ambition belongs in a conversation earned by noticing the work, not her introductory speech.

**Tobin, ten**, has drawn two creature trails that intersect where no creature could fit. He wants to publish an account in which he is right, then quietly wants help correcting it. He takes his water-dish duty seriously only after the dish's owner drinks from Nella's play cup. He can be found with other children, not permanently waiting for travel stories.

**Nella, six**, has turned the landing's chalk squares into a contest called “You Cannot Stand There.” She changes its rule after a successful jump. Her private achievement is ringing a practice chime without help. She declines a second performance for her brother: “You were away for the first one. That was the one.” Later she offers to teach him the new song, badly.

**Mara** has counted the pass-house bedding, discovered two beautiful but useless decorative blankets among the emergency stock, and sent them back. She also wants a quiet seat from which to hear Miret's rehearsal. She is willing to borrow Ada's time before remembering Ada is working. Let her catch herself sometimes: “No. Finish yours. I'll find another pair of hands.” She remains a neighbor and bellkeeper, not the protagonist's mother or a universal quest authority.

**Miret** is trying to make three apprentices sound like weather without damaging their throats. One apprentice has an excellent thunder noise and keeps using it during love scenes. Miret wants a rehearsal space, a paid audience for the eventual comic opera and one person willing to tell her which joke does not work. Her patience with creatures is better than her patience with performers pretending a mistake was intentional.

**Bera Venn, she/her**, repairs ironwork and carts in Carrier Yard. Her apron has one bright blue patch she refuses to replace with sensible leather. She has copied a lowland carriage suspension onto the back of a coal bill and wants to prove she can build something graceful as well as durable. Ollo's commission is her chance; his changed expectations infuriate her. She charges for her skill and enjoys showing it off. Her shop can continue through an order slate while she accompanies a test roll.

**Ollo Fen, he/him**, owns the bakehouse on the warm-room side of Carrier Yard. He can identify a regular by the way they knock flour from a sleeve, but loses count when somebody compliments his bread. He wants a little mobile counter for picnics and evening trade, and would quite like Bera to attend its first supper. He has described these two ambitions as one simple cart order. He is generous with broken crusts and exact about the price of intact loaves.

**Sela Marr, they/them**, keeps the warm rooms. They move slowly through wet passages and speak quickly when someone invents a rule about their towels. They want both a pleasant public place and a private afternoon. Today a linen delivery has invaded the Embermoles' cool passage, while Ollo is campaigning for more warmth beside his dough. Sela is fond of him without regarding every baking need as a civic emergency. Their dry office contains a chair nobody is permitted to stand on.

**Neri Avo, she/her**, runs the Gutter Lane general shop and parcel room, whose rear porch opens onto the carrying incline. She can wrap a fragile cup while conducting an argument through the wall. She wants her stock counted before the fair, a tolerable profit and an evening in the violet boots she has privately ordered. She tells customers they cannot eat beautiful shoes, then buys beautiful shoes. Her impatience is comic; careless accusations can still hurt her friends.

**Dema Corr, she/her**, sells seasonal herbs from the western terraces. She arrives with earth under her nails and an expensive little mirror tied inside her herb basket. She wants to sell enough cuttings to stay another day, then enjoy the fair without being asked whether every plant cures something. She knows what grows on Ruskell's roof because she planted half of it. Their arguments about the roof's former beauty are a form of pleasure, until he refuses to look at what is beautiful now.

**Ruskell, he/him**, is an elderly Gutter Lane resident and enthusiastic chair repairer. He is unrelated to Tavio Rusk and mildly offended when asked to explain the similarity. He wants to open his old roof garden for an evening display of paintings made there years ago, including Ada's dreadful landscape. He has polished a chair nobody has bought. He likes young visitors who disagree with him and dislikes being praised merely for remembering things.

## F5-S01 — The longest table

**Invitation:** Morning Arrival onward, after seeing Ada's repair at home or RS-04a. Ada spreads a dark weather cover across two stools; the middle sags almost to the paving. Lina rescues her lettering from the wet edge. The visible problem is a work surface, not a suddenly impoverished household.

**Ada:** “I measured the cover. I failed to measure the village.”

**Lina:** “The school table opens twice.”

**Ada:** “Mara said the fair had it.”

**Lina:** “The fair has the room. It hasn't booked every object inside.”

**You — accept:** “I'll find out where you can lay it flat.”

**Ada:** “Four coins from the handling allowance. This is a job.”

**You — decline:** “I want to find my own work first.”

**Ada:** “Then go. I can price a large repair without making you carry it.”

Acceptance sets `s01_started`; journal: **“Ask Lina about the schoolroom table.”** Append her actual phase location: the household shutter before her sign delivery, Ollo's counter during delivery, otherwise the schoolroom/market work area where she keeps the hinge sketch. Her sign-board note gives the same direction if she has moved. Lina already knows the folding sections and the rehearsal arrangement. The player does not collect three permissions. Her claim is verified by the schoolroom's physical layout and Mara's one short conversation at Mara's actual phase location.

**Lina:** “They can rehearse at the windows. They don't need a table to pretend it's raining.”

**You:** “Will you show us?”

**Lina:** “After this letter. Ollo paid for the whole word.”

**You — respect her work:** “I'll carry it. Tell me which hinge.”

**Lina:** “The brass one. The other folds your fingers.”

**You — ask about the sign:** “Is BAKERY meant to climb uphill?”

**Lina:** “Yes.”

*She looks at it again.*

**Lina:** “No. Don't tell him.”

**Mara:** “I thought Ada was still on small repairs.”

**You:** “It stopped being small before the price did.”

**Mara:** “Good for her. Keep the rehearsal passage open.”

**Approach A: take the folded table to Ada.** Use the schoolroom's short service ramp to the adjacent Bell Court awning. At the awning's tight corner, choosing “set down and turn” changes the grip safely; a missed turn causes a comic stop, not broken furniture or a dexterity tax. Lina marks the two sections so they return together. Do not send this same-level carry down through Carrier Yard. Ada keeps her familiar market position and can speak to prospective customers.

**Progress:**

**Ada:** “You brought the hinges too. Luxury.”

**Lina:** “My mark goes underneath. Don't scrub it off.”

**Ada:** “It stays. Anyone who borrows it can find you.”

**Resolution A:**

**Ada:** “Flat at last. Here are your four.”

**You:** “That looks like more work than before.”

**Ada:** “Now you can see all of it.”

**Approach B: bring the work to the schoolroom.** Carry the rolled cover and Ada's prepared tool basket to the table already inside. Reserve the window-side strip; the apprentices keep their floor. Ada gets covered space, although fewer passing customers see the repair. Lina writes a market notice instead of minding the stall. Before contract acceptance, Ada answers at the open market-side door and steps to her worktable for the main terms review; no new search is required.

**Progress:**

**Miret, from the doorway:** “Is it waterproof?”

**Ada:** “When I'm finished.”

**Miret:** “Then we'll rehearse the wind first.”

**Resolution B:**

**Ada:** “Four coins. And I may ask about using this room again.”

**Lina:** “Ask about the rent. Don't offer all our evenings.”

**Ada:** “One afternoon. Mine.”

Both approaches set `s01_complete`, store `s01_place=market|schoolroom` and pay only if `s01_paid=false`. Ada's customer pays her independently after inspection; Lina retains her own sign fee. On evening return the cover hangs in clean folds, leaving actual room on the family table. Ada says, “I could leave the whole thing laid out. Tonight nobody eats supper on a buckle.” Lina shows her paid sign, with the corrected lettering. If the story was declined, Ada hires a neighbor and works at the schoolroom; she earns less margin, finishes anyway, and never sends a guilt letter. Accepted unfinished work can resume on the next local day at the same agreed fee.

## F5-S02 — A carriage for nobody's wedding

**Invitation:** see Bera's bright cart body on trestles at RS-I07. It has elegant metal pears along the rail, narrow drawers and no seat. Ollo stands beside it holding a length of white cloth. A passerby has already congratulated him.

**Bera:** “There is nowhere for a bride to sit.”

**Ollo:** “It's a bread counter.”

**Bera:** “Then stop putting a veil on it.”

**Ollo:** “It's shade.”

*Bera takes a step back. She clearly likes the cart, despite herself.*

She has quoted a functional frame and added the pears to demonstrate her work. Ollo now wants a grand unveiling and assumed her help with that was included. Her own ambition made the boundary unclear; the player is not asked to find which friend is secretly bad. She wants an independent set of hands for a proper test.

**Bera:** “Four coins to help me prove it rolls and stops. The wedding costs extra.”

**You — accept:** “Tell me where to hold it.”

**Bera:** “A promising first question.”

**You — decline:** “I'm already carrying enough today.”

**Bera:** “Then admire it without touching. That's free.”

Journal after `s02_started`: **“Choose Bera's public counter test or the quiet carrying-incline test.”** Both use supplied ballast and Bera's brake handle. No purchase, creature labor or rolling minigame is required. Confirming either test starts its authored scene; departure remains reversible before that confirmation.

**Approach A: public counter test in Carrier Yard.** Ollo lays out three trays of sample-shaped wooden blocks. The player checks drawer clearance with the awning open, chooses the broad porch route and holds the end while Bera sets the brake. One drawer collides with the supposedly ornamental pear. Bera removes that single ornament, turns it and discovers it makes an excellent handle.

**Progress:**

**You:** “This one bites.”

**Bera:** “That one was meant to be admired.”

**Ollo:** “Customers do both.”

**Bera:** “Then admire the improved version.”

Ollo finally addresses the unpriced event work: “I wanted people to see it. I should have asked how much of your day that meant.” Bera quotes her own modest fee, which he accepts outside the player's purse. She does not donate it because the protagonist delivered a stirring speech.

**Resolution A:**

**Bera:** “Four coins. Tell anyone who asks that it stopped.”

**You:** “Even if they ask about the pears?”

**Bera:** “Especially then.”

**Ollo, to Bera:** “Would you come to the first supper?”

**Bera:** “As the mechanic?”

**Ollo:** “As somebody I'd like at the table.”

**Bera:** “Put that on a separate invitation.”

**Approach B: quiet loaded test on the incline.** Bera loads sealed flour sacks already belonging to Ollo and gives the player a side grip. Stop at two painted rests, then release the brake only on her signal. The load shifts because Ollo packed all the heavy sacks in the handsome drawer. Repack by weight, not silhouette. The player can inspect the suspension drawing while Bera tightens the supplied retaining pin.

**Progress:**

**Ollo:** “I wanted the nice side facing out.”

**You:** “The hill wants the heavy side facing down.”

**Bera:** “The hill has seniority.”

**Resolution B:**

**Bera:** “Four coins. It handles properly now.”

**Ollo:** “Can we still show it tonight?”

**Bera:** “Yes. Without pretending I built it in my spare time.”

**Ollo:** “I was going to say you were a genius.”

**Bera:** “A paid genius.”

At evening, A produces a polished public opening: Nella asks whether the pears are edible. B produces a smaller first sale at the lower fair edge; Ollo proudly demonstrates the brake to a customer interested only in buns. In both, Bera accepts supper, sits down and immediately tells him to stop discussing wheel loads. This is a developing adult attachment, not an established engagement or a player romance flag.

Record `s02_test=public|incline`, `s02_complete`, `s02_paid`. The one-time four coins come from Bera's test budget. Without participation she recruits another worker and unveils a less thoroughly rehearsed cart; it remains safe, and one drawer sticks until she adjusts it. The story can be accepted after the fair as a delivery test, retaining both approaches and the same fee. No destroyed wedding or absent customer follows from wandering.

## F5-S03 — Neri's sensible purchase

**Invitation:** at RS-I08, Neri is comparing a parcel slate with a shoe-shaped empty wrapping. The unattached Doorling **RS-C04** has threaded a violet lace through several loose tags beneath the rear porch. It is a different individual from Tavio's familiar companion. The porch shelter and legal invitation belong to the creature page.

**Neri:** “One imported parcel. Marked received. Not here.”

**Dema:** “You put it away yourself.”

**Neri:** “I put everything away myself.”

**Dema:** “That narrows it down.”

Neri offers four coins to locate the missing package. Its contents are hers, not urgent medicine. The player knows that before accepting. She says “display samples” much too carefully; a passing customer need not hear the whole story.

**You — accept:** “Four coins. Tell me what the wrapping looked like.”

**Neri:** “Cream paper. Violet cord. Entirely ordinary.”

**You — decline:** “I'll leave you to search.”

**Neri:** “Fine. If I find it somewhere obvious, you heard nothing.”

Journal: **“Trace the violet cord on Neri's rear porch, or ask Dema where she saw the parcel.”** `s03_started` freezes no clocks. The package remains in a dry high cupboard behind an unlabelled cloth screen. The Doorling moved a loose lace and hanging label, not a heavy sealed box or a person's private correspondence. Neri herself moved the box while making its porch shelter less draughty.

**Approach A: follow the porch arrangement.** Inspect the violet loop, a tag caught on a low peg and a clear patch where a little screen recently stood. The Doorling tucks the lace inward whenever anyone leans into its refuge. Do not drag it out. Neri supplies an unused soft tie so the player can place the free end near the peg; the creature can leave the violet lace or ignore it. Its choice is not a capture roll or required timed interaction. The cupboard's matching torn tag is independently visible from the side passage.

**Progress:**

**You:** “It has your lace.”

**Neri:** “It has excellent taste.”

**You:** “And you moved this screen.”

**Neri:** “Because it was sitting in the draught.”

**You:** “The cupboard behind it?”

**Neri:** “If they're in there, I want my four coins back.”

**You:** “We haven't reached that part.”

**Resolution A:** Neri opens her own parcel. Violet dancing boots emerge. She tries to hold them below the counter; they are too bright to disappear.

**Neri:** “Durable soles.”

**You:** “Very sensible.”

**Neri:** “Here. Four coins for being briefly tolerable.”

**Approach B: reconstruct Neri's morning with Dema.** Dema saw Neri carry the parcel toward the sheltered cupboard while holding a bowl for the Doorling. Compare the cream paper scrap beside that bowl to the receipt; neither private letters nor the contents need opening. Dema asks the player to stop the accusation that she collected it with her herb delivery.

**Progress:**

**Dema:** “If I had ordered something violet, I wouldn't store it with the parsley.”

**You:** “She was making room at the porch.”

**Dema:** “Of course she was. She does a kindness and calls it stock control.”

**Resolution B:**

**Neri, to Dema:** “You didn't take it. I put it behind the screen.”

**Dema:** “I know. I was here for that part.”

**Neri:** “I shouldn't have accused you.”

**Dema:** “No. Now show me.”

*Neri raises one violet boot. Dema smiles without pretending to be surprised.*

**Neri, to you:** “Four coins. You found the parcel, not a village announcement.”

Both paths settle the mistake and preserve the porch. The player can later compliment the boots publicly or simply greet her; there is no blackmail reward or relationship penalty for not investigating her purchase. In the evening she dances with Sela, who remarks, “Those look difficult to clean.” Neri answers, “I'm not cleaning them tonight.” If the player knows only her shop voice, that answer reveals something new.

Set `s03_complete`, `s03_method=porch|witness`, `s03_paid`; then expose the **separate** RS-C04 keeper conversation, never automatic recruitment. If ignored, Neri finds the boots at closing and apologizes to Dema herself; no four coins are awarded. An accepted unfinished search retains its props and fee. Neri attends the fair in work shoes: “I can enjoy an evening in these.” She returns to the shop when the player resumes.

## F5-S04 — The best seat in the house

**Invitation:** walk through RS-I10 toward the bathing court. An adult Embermole **RS-C03** lies on the cool threshold with its forefeet precisely in the shadow. People step around it carrying towels. Ollo arrives with a covered bowl of dough and tries to negotiate six inches of warmer shelf.

**Sela:** “You're bringing work into the baths again.”

**Ollo:** “It's resting.”

**Sela:** “So was I.”

The cool passage has been narrowed by the delivered linen, while a new drying screen directs sunlight across the old resting tile. Nobody is starving or dying. Sela wants a comfortable arrangement that accommodates bathing, laundry and creature rest, without turning the whole public room into Ollo's bakery.

**Sela:** “Help me make room, if you like. No wages for this one. You can have the first quiet sit.”

**You — accept:** “Show me what can move.”

**Sela:** “The linen. The screen. Ollo.”

**You — decline:** “I'd rather use the baths than rearrange them.”

**Sela:** “An excellent ambition. The dry hook is yours.”

Journal after `s04_started`: **“Find a clear cool resting place without closing the warm rooms.”** Show the creature moving away from direct heat and toward a shaded stone before the player chooses. Inspection gives ordinary temperature and access descriptions, not translated creature speech. The player need not own it, command it or press it toward a location.

**Approach A: clear the original passage.** Sela carries the linen bundles when the player opens the wide storage screen and identifies an empty dry rack. Ollo tries to donate a flour shelf until Sela points out the flour already on it. Move one bench sideways along its existing guides; the shaded tile and two human seats become usable together.

**Progress:**

**Ollo:** “People like sitting near the warmth.”

**Sela:** “After they have sat in the warmth, some enjoy sitting somewhere else.”

**You:** “The creature agrees.”

**Sela:** “The creature hasn't bought an oven and made it a personality.”

**Resolution A:** the Embermole takes the recovered cool passage on its own. Sela places two cups on the moved bench, leaves one beside the player and sits without discussing work. Ollo delivers a broken crust from his own supper tray, then remembers to leave.

**Sela:** “Listen.”

**You:** “To what?”

**Sela:** “Exactly.”

**Approach B: give the drying court a shaded nook.** Keep the linen where it is temporarily, open the upper ventilation screen and angle the existing shade panel over an accessible stone recess. Sela checks that neither the washing route nor the Embermole passage is obstructed. The player lays the unused towel basket outside the recess as a visible boundary, not a trap. The adult can choose to investigate; completing the human arrangement never requires forcing it to settle.

**Progress:**

**You:** “The shadow reaches the stone now.”

**Sela:** “Good. Leave the opening wide.”

**Ollo:** “Could I put the dough beside it?”

**Sela:** “You could take the dough home.”

**Resolution B:** Sela tests the nook themselves, making Ollo wait for a verdict on something unrelated to bread.

**Sela:** “Cool stone. Warm air. No one asking for a towel.”

**You:** “I was going to ask.”

**Sela:** “Then I was happy for almost a whole sentence.”

Set `s04_complete`, `s04_nook=passage|court`. Reward is the accessible resting space and a return conversation, with no coins, free premium service or recruitment receipt. RS-C03's independent adult invitation may become available through its owning page; other Embermoles and the warm rooms do not lose their heat source. On evening return, Sela appears at the fair in an unfamiliar bright scarf. “Dry clothes,” they explain. “I own some.” The original invitation remains available through Evening Return: the warm-room attendant can call Sela from the nearby fair for the scene, without duplicating them. If never accepted, Sela reopens the passage themselves only when the player confirms a next-day or city-departure transition. That competent solution remains inspectable; the player can still offer the alternate arrangement on a later day. An accepted unfinished arrangement retains its completion step.

## F5-S05 — The borrowed shade

**Invitation:** Miret's apprentices have moved long festival cloth from RS-07a onto a sunny ledge. A Kitefin **RS-C05** keeps pulling a free end beneath the rail. From below, its broad surfaces flash brilliantly; on approach, its body withdraws into the damp shelter. A rehearsal page flaps against Miret's shoe.

**Miret:** “We have acquired a fourth performer.”

**You:** “Is it good?”

**Miret:** “It arrives on cue. I'm reconsidering the others.”

Her real problem is that the cloth must suggest a traveling storm for the fair scene. She is not furious with an animal for spoiling paperwork. She wants the effect to look marvellous, and is embarrassed that she has not noticed where her apprentices put the cloth.

**Miret:** “Would you look along the rail? Something is making that end more interesting than my direction.”

**You — accept:** “I'll see where it's trying to take it.”

**Miret:** “Thank you. None of us has been invited into its wings.”

**You — decline:** “I'll watch the rehearsal later.”

**Miret:** “Good. We are currently better from a distance.”

Journal: **“Follow the cloth along Rope Walk. Look at the wind, shade and nesting fibres.”** Three visible signs remain available together: a warm updraught lifting the exposed cloth, damp sheltered stone beneath the rail, and loose nesting fibres caught in an existing peg. An ordinary ribbon or drifting dust indicates the wind. No mount, attack, rare partner or hatchling removal is required.

**Approach A: retie the cloth.** Use existing pegs to lift the public cloth above the ledge while leaving its sheltered strip unoccupied. Miret holds the far end; the player picks the leeward peg after inspecting the ribbon. A wrong first peg makes the cloth slap her hat, then she points out the visible current. Retry is immediate and benign.

**Progress:**

**Miret, hat over one eye:** “A convincing storm.”

**You:** “Wrong direction?”

**Miret:** “For me personally, yes.”

**Resolution A:** the Kitefin settles beneath the rail while the higher cloth streams across the performers. Miret rewrites a stage direction in three words and the apprentices groan at her delighted new idea.

**Miret:** “Thunder. Then sneeze.”

**Apprentice:** “The storm sneezes?”

**Miret:** “The man under it. Try listening to the story.”

**Approach B: change the rehearsal's blocking.** Leave the sheltered cloth end in place and stage the storm from the open inland pegs. The player stands at the audience mark while the apprentices pull a shorter sweep across the background. Miret tests whether the interrupted view is readable, then uses the gap for a performer who persistently emerges in the wrong weather.

**Progress:**

**You:** “From here, it looks as if he escaped the rain.”

**Miret:** “He did. Unfortunately, that's where the hail starts.”

**Apprentice:** “Do I get an umbrella?”

**Miret:** “You get timing.”

**Resolution B:** the scene becomes smaller and funnier. The Kitefin makes one unplanned pass above the cloth; the apprentices keep going instead of stopping to applaud themselves.

**Miret, afterward:** “That. You let the interesting thing happen and remembered what you were doing.”

Both paths set `s05_complete`, `s05_staging=high_cloth|inland`. No coins or invitation: this roost individual remains unavailable for recruitment this visit. Miret gives the player a harmless yellow offcut if wanted; it is a scene prop for Nella's later game, not gear. In the evening the chosen staging appears in the actual short comic performance. If skipped, Miret retrieves the cloth and performs indoors; her career has not failed. The roost can still be found, and a later outdoor rehearsal can provide the unfinished story's two approaches.

## F5-S06 — The sea from here

**Invitation:** Ruskell stands outside RS-I02 holding a small painting at arm's length. It depicts something blue beneath several determined green triangles. Dema has come to collect empty pots and is laughing too hard to carry them. The charcoal loft's independent outside stair is openable with ordinary permission; this story never gates rooftop exploration.

**Ruskell:** “It's the sea.”

**Dema:** “It has chestnut trees in it.”

**Ruskell:** “Ada was sixteen.”

**Dema:** “So were the trees, apparently.”

He is preparing a tiny evening display of paintings once made on the roof. He wants people to see the garden as somewhere they enjoyed being, rather than praise him for having kept old wood dry. Dema wants room for this year's planters. She loves the old picture precisely because it is terrible and identifiable as Ada's.

**Ruskell:** “Help me choose how to hang them. I can offer a roof and an argument.”

**You — accept:** “I know what the argument looks like. Show me the roof.”
