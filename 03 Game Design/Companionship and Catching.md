# Companionship and catching

“Monster-catching” must retain the pleasures of discovery, anticipation, assembling a team, and getting to know a new companion. This proposal makes the capture interaction depend on the creature and its situation.

## A species is not a relationship

The bestiary describes a species. Each individual can have a temperament, learned habits, recognizable markings, and a history with the player. Two individuals need not require a large simulation to feel different: a handful of authored behavioral tags can affect greetings, preferred camp locations, and responses to particular tasks. Their feasibility is untested.

## The field accord — working concept

**September expansion:** the user rejects straight random-ball catching as the central gimmick (D-051). [Recruitment, Eggs and Binding Instruments](Recruitment%20Eggs%20and%20Binding%20Instruments.md) develops readable invitations, eggs and rare containment/coercive exceptions. The token and criteria below remain proposals; deterministic recruitment details, breeding and universal capture are not adopted. [Companion Quests and Camp Conversations](../02%20Narrative/Companion%20Quests%20and%20Camp%20Conversations.md) gives individual relationships played adventures. [The Observer and the Sanctuary](../02%20Narrative/The%20Observer%20and%20the%20Sanctuary.md) explores a specific refuge failure rather than declaring all party storage harmful.

A portable resting token holds a familiar pattern of shelter, scent, and return. It lets a willing creature retreat into a small maintained refuge and return near its partner. Its name and appearance remain open; early visual proposals are glazed pocket shrines, woven nest-rings, or folding copper lanterns. Do not select a spherical throwing device by default.

Rules proposed for this draft:

- The token does not create obedience or ownership.
- A creature can decline a task, leave an unsafe partnership, or need a larger refuge. These possibilities are telegraphed; normal affectionate play is not punished with random desertion.
- Refuge access has an ecological maintenance cost. Rest stops, village keepers, and sanctuaries matter.
- Some beings cannot enter a refuge because of size, migratory obligations, distributed anatomy, or incompatibility with the accord. “Uncatchable” does not necessarily mean hostile.
- Companions must remain conveniently available enough for ordinary play. Long maintenance chores would undermine the intended experience; tuning comes later.

## Different ways to meet a partner

| Encounter | Player action | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Curious visitor | Observe, offer a suitable invitation, give it space | It follows temporarily and may accept a bond |
| Proud territorial creature | Survive a readable challenge without destroying its habitat | Respect opens an invitation; victory alone is insufficient |
| Injured wild animal | Help it and release it safely | It may later approach; care does not purchase consent |
| Working creature with a community | Earn the keeper's and creature's trust | Shared or seasonal partnership; the community remains involved |
| Predator in conflict with people | Redirect prey, negotiate territory, or defend others | Often coexistence or exclusion, not recruitment |
| Vast or alien being | Learn a boundary, route, or communication pattern | A pact or safe passage, not a slot on a team |

Some straightforward encounters should take little time. Requiring a quest for every common creature would exhaust the collection loop. Elaborate recruitment belongs to select species or individuals.

## Three complete recruitment sketches

These demonstrate the intended pleasure of catching. Species biology stays in the [Bestiary](../01%20World/Bestiary.md); the individuals and sequences below are proposals.

### A quick playful recruit — the collector of bad buttons

**Anticipation:** a Thimbleback follows the protagonist's coat, trying to reach a large mismatched button. Its shell cups already contain several much worse buttons.

**Action:** the player can offer a spare, redirect its attention with a rolling nutshell, or invite their existing companion into a short passing game. Its turns are exaggerated and funny. There is no long quest prerequisite and no consumable wasted on a hidden probability.

**Bond opportunity:** after a successful exchange, it pats the offered resting token, puts its favorite button beside it, and waits. The interface clearly offers partnership or a friendly farewell. This is the catch moment: anticipation resolves into a recognizable voluntary arrival.

**Payoff:** it joins the camp scene immediately, noisily reorganizes a travel pouch, and can help uncover an optional cache beside the next trail. The creature's happiness has a specific expression. Declining leaves it available in its home area.

### A skillful challenge recruit — the keeper's unbeaten Bellwether

**Anticipation:** a keeper organizes supervised sparring on a padded hillside course. One independent young adult Bellwether has learned to use its horn resonance to interrupt opponents just before they commit. It repeatedly returns to the challenge and ignores partners who cannot hold its attention.

**Action:** the player predicts a feint, protects a companion, and answers the Bellwether's resonant charge with a well-timed team technique. In the selected turn-based direction, these are readable turns and preparations, not a reflex minigame. A strong performance can include a clean decisive victory; battle is an attractive valid activity in its own right.

**Bond opportunity:** the exhausted but delighted creature repeats the player's opening signal and approaches. The successful challenge directly unlocks the invitation. No unrelated errand is required to prove the player deserved to win.

**Payoff:** a distinctive cooperative technique becomes available for further practice; the keeper invites rematches with varied courses; the creature contributes its individual call to the party's departure. Losing produces advice and an ordinary retry after rest.

### A rare exploratory recruit — the guest of the folded loft

**Anticipation:** a Doorling with a silver brow fold leaves carefully sorted scraps on beams in a disused mountain wayhouse. Several sightings establish an individual preference for triangular pieces of blue cloth. The player can solve its nesting pattern from different approach routes.

**Action:** restore three shelter markers around its extended loft, respecting the colony's local geometry. A companion can carry a light marker, a nearby craftsperson can lend a long hook, or the player can climb a slower route. The puzzle depends on observation, not one mandatory creature.

**Bond opportunity:** the individual escorts the player through a small surprising interior and presents a scrap in the color of their travel kit. It may then accept a refuge invitation. The remaining colony stays in its habitat.

**Payoff:** the new partner reveals a previously overlooked ledge on the return journey and decorates its resting place. It does not confer universal lock bypass or infinite inventory. A player who wants to leave it wild can retain the discovered route and revisit it.

## An individual relationship across the story

[First Companion](../02%20Narrative/First%20Companion.md) develops Clatter, the inexperienced fair Bellwether, through household visits, useful fieldwork, and later returns. It is a different proposed individual from the unbeaten challenge recruit above. Neither species nor individual is a compulsory starter; other companions need their own habits and scenes.

## Celebration and collection

Each new partnership deserves a short introduction, a readable individual trait, a journal illustration, and an immediate chance to do something together. Repeated catches should have streamlined presentation. Optional field records can celebrate observing rare beings without requiring ownership of everything in the ecosystem.

[Rare Encounters](Rare%20Encounters.md) proposes discoverable conditions and dependable tracking alongside chance sightings. [Mounts and Field Abilities](Mounts%20and%20Field%20Abilities.md) gives partners useful roles beyond battle without forcing every traversal helper into the five-member combat team. [Progression and Talents](Progression%20and%20Talents.md) proposes catch-up growth so a newly befriended creature can join current adventures without hours of trivial battles.

## Affection, reliability, and disagreement

Avoid collapsing the relationship into one affection meter that solves every problem. A companion can love the protagonist and dislike confined spaces; trust them in battle and refuse work that harms its nesting ground. Store the design in a small set of readable preferences and commitments. Whether these become numeric variables is an implementation question.

Useful feedback: anticipatory animation, a short field-journal note, an optional companion interaction, or a clear task response. Failure to understand a creature should invite another attempt, not force unexplained permanent loss.

## Growth options to compare

1. **Life-stage transformation:** strong reveal and collecting appeal; risks treating earlier forms as disposable.
2. **Adaptation through relationships and habitat:** individuals develop different abilities; requires restrained complexity and legible outcomes.
3. **Stable forms with new practices:** a cherished design remains recognizable; progression comes through techniques and cooperation.

A mixed system could exist, but it should follow species ecology. No universal evolution rule is approved.

## The unsettling extension

Later, a refuge might begin anticipating a companion's wishes so perfectly that it no longer wants to leave. The horror is not that the friendship was fake. It is that a genuine need has been satisfied in a way that can sever the relationship. This is a possible story mechanism, not a universal affliction imposed on every partner.

The supplied Evertale commentary transcript (working-archive reference) suggests a second, related possibility: the recognizable care procedure is extended to the keeper. A larger local accord offers a person shelter and decides that the familiar animal should authorize departure. The disturbing transfer is care becoming custody. It does not prove that ordinary field tokens can capture humans, every creature was once a person, or normal partnership was secretly wrongdoing. A specific story must establish the local mechanism and show independent evidence; it cannot rewrite the whole catching system during a reveal. See the [quest index](../02%20Narrative/Quest%20Index.md) for the resulting optional investigation.

## Questions for future testing

Can an invitation feel as satisfying as a successful catch? Can refusal be clear without becoming tedious? Can players understand the distinction between a species record, a temporary visitor, and a bonded companion? Can a small amount of individualized behavior create lasting attachment?
