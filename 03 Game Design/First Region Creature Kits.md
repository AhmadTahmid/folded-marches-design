# First-region creature kits

These are five complete **proposed equipped kits**, using the player-side benchmark values from [Worked Battle](Worked%20Battle.md). They give the region's encounters a shared vocabulary and make an opposing five-creature team possible to examine. They are not five compulsory early recruits. The [First Paid Journey](../02%20Narrative/First%20Paid%20Journey.md) still begins with a small partnership; one to three companions and a few introduced techniques are sufficient for its early lessons. This full roster represents a later regional practice challenge or an author-side comparison.

Every technique, number, and extension of species behavior below remains draft. [Bestiary](../01%20World/Bestiary.md) owns observed ecology; a combat animation does not establish a new universal biological power. There are no talents equipped in this benchmark. [Progression and Talents](Progression%20and%20Talents.md) explores later modifications separately.

## Common card conventions

[Tactical Combat](Tactical%20Combat.md) owns alternating activations, formation, damage, and Focus. [Status and Counterplay](Status%20and%20Counterplay.md) owns all named effects here. No new status or reaction system is needed.

- Each creature equips the **four listed techniques**, plus unchanged **Strike, Guard, and Move**. All actions spend its one activation. There is no keeper turn, reserve replacement, or extra summoned body.
- All start with 3 Focus; standing creatures recover 1 at the start of rounds 2 onward, capped at 3. `1 / 2` means 1 Focus and cooldown 2: used in round 1, ready in round 3. Strike, Guard, and Move have zero Focus cost and no cooldown.
- `Physical` and `Accord` describe activation: Silence prevents Accord techniques. `Attack` is a separate explicit tag: Disarm prevents Strike and tagged techniques. Every damage-dealing technique in these five kits is tagged Attack; non-damaging hostile techniques identify that tag individually. This is not a rule that every future source of damage must be an Attack.
- `Impact` uses Armor; `Resonance` uses Ward. Delivery is independently `Melee` or `Reach`. Melee requires a front-row user and a legal enemy front target while any front defender stands. Reach works from either row and can address either row unless narrowed below.
- Targets must be standing. “Ally” includes the user. One enemy means one creature, never a position. All effects last **through the end of the application round**, except Stagger's resulting Resolve, which follows its established rule. Printed steps resolve in order; a zero-damage hit still applies its following status unless protection rejects it.
- Barrier values do not add together. Strip removes removable positive effects, including Steadfast and Barrier; it cannot remove Resolve. Basic and Strong Cleanse retain their full existing meanings. A cleanse with nothing to remove has no benefit; the preview says so.

## Benchmark bodies

| Creature | HP | Armor / Ward | Common Strike | Suggested starting position |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Thimbleback | 30 | 2 / 0 | Physical, Attack, Melee; 6 Impact | Front 1 |
| Bellwether | 32 | 0 / 0 | Physical, Attack, Melee; 8 Impact | Front 2 |
| Glassgrazer | 36 | 2 / 2 | Physical, Attack, Melee; 6 Impact | Front 3 |
| Embermole | 26 | 0 / 0 | Physical, Attack, Reach; 6 Impact | Rear 1 |
| Doorling | 24 | 0 / 0 | Physical, Attack, Reach; 5 Impact | Rear 2 |

Common Guard gives its user an 8-point removable Barrier through this round. Move exchanges rows with a willing ally or enters an empty slot; Root prevents participation, and the exchanged ally gains no activation. Suggested positions are an opening arrangement, not locked roles. Embermole's Strike remains the proposed small thrown stone from the original example; Doorling flicks a small carried scrap. Neither attack implies infinite ammunition or humanlike tool use throughout the species. Ordinary battle presentation assumes a modest prepared supply without a separate gathering chore.

## Thimbleback — the stubborn improvisor

The Thimbleback uses its body, stored objects, and digging habits to remain useful while partners prepare something elaborate. It can protect itself, attack behind the front, or obstruct an opponent; it cannot cleanse the team or interrupt an activation outright.

| Technique | Tags | Focus / cooldown | Exact steps and targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nut Pitch** | Physical, Attack, Reach | 1 / 1 | Deal 8 Impact to one enemy. No secondary effect. |
| **Cupguard** | Physical, Reach | 1 / 2 | Give self a 12-point Barrier through this round. Self only; it replaces a smaller remaining Barrier rather than adding to it. |
| **Burrow Grip** | Physical, Attack, Melee | 1 / 2 | Deal 6 Impact to one legal enemy, then attempt Root through this round. Does not move either creature. |
| **Clod Flick** | Physical, Attack, Reach | 1 / 2 | Attempt Disarm on one enemy through this round. No damage. Steadfast rejects the Disarm. |

**Body and behavior.** Nut Pitch uses cached hard nuts or a prepared practice equivalent. Cupguard folds its cups across exposed surfaces. Burrow Grip is a short low grapple using digging claws, not an instant tunnel under any terrain. Clod Flick throws a carried loose clod, distracting the motions needed for attacks. None creates a persistent ground hazard or consumes a crafting ingredient.

**Decision and counter.** A Thimbleback can Disarm an Accord attacker even though it cannot Silence it: the Attack tag is the relevant fact. But Clod Flick also has that tag, so a Disarmed Thimbleback cannot use it. Cupguard and Move remain options when legal. Root matters before a planned exchange; applying it after everyone has acted usually wastes its brief window. Reach pressure and Strip keep its self-protection from becoming an invulnerable wall.

## Bellwether — the opening-maker

Three techniques retain the names and values already demonstrated. Its fourth lets it prevent control instead of correcting it afterwards. It has the team's strongest free Melee Strike and no base Armor, making a spent activation on setup a real choice rather than an automatic opener.

| Technique | Tags | Focus / cooldown | Exact steps and targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Calling Note** | Accord, Reach | 1 / 2 | Attempt Exposed on one enemy through this round: Armor −2, minimum zero. No damage. |
| **Arresting Peal** | Accord, Reach | 2 / 3 | Attempt Stagger on one enemy with an unused activation this round. Steadfast, Resolve, or an existing Stagger makes it ineligible. No damage. |
| **Horn Run** | Physical, Attack, Melee | 1 / 2 | Deal 12 Impact to one legal enemy. No status, row change, or follow-up attack. |
| **Steady Chord** | Accord, Reach | 1 / 2 | Give one ally Steadfast through this round. It rejects new Silence, Disarm, Root, and Stagger; existing effects remain. |

**Body and behavior.** Herd calls become practiced combat signals. Calling Note disrupts an enemy's defensive bearing; Arresting Peal breaks a moment of commitment. Steady Chord gives a partner a stable signal to follow. These are proposed bounded accord techniques informed by horn resonance, not universal mind control or evidence that a Bellwether understands human language.

**Decision and counter.** Calling Note contributes nothing against Armor 0; it does not lower Ward. An enemy cleanser may erase it before the next attacker acts. Arresting Peal costs two Focus and returns slowly; cancelling one action is worthwhile only if the team uses that opportunity. Silence closes its three Accord options while Horn Run, Strike, Guard, and Move can remain legal. Exposed does not make those alternatives stronger against an already unarmored target.

## Glassgrazer — the anchor that can rescue a plan

Its larger body and mineral fans justify durable baseline defenses. Its most valuable rescue costs most of its starting Focus and occupies its entire turn. It therefore cannot rescue, shield, and deal damage all at once.

| Technique | Tags | Focus / cooldown | Exact steps and targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fan Shove** | Physical, Attack, Melee | 1 / 2 | Deal 10 Impact to one legal enemy. No forced movement. |
| **Shed Screen** | Physical, Reach | 2 / 2 | Give one ally a 10-point Barrier through this round. No cleanse or status protection. |
| **Settling Pulse** | Accord, Reach | 2 / 3 | Strong Cleanse one ally. Remove Basic effects and Stagger; removing Stagger grants that ally Resolve through the following round. No healing. |
| **Fan Ring** | Accord, Attack, Reach | 1 / 1 | Deal 8 Resonance to one enemy. No secondary effect. |

**Body and behavior.** Fan Shove uses the shoulders and the broad faces of its growths, avoiding a new crystal-blade anatomy. Shed Screen places a carried or recently shed plate across an ally's immediate danger line. Settling Pulse and Fan Ring propose a limited trained resonance through its mineral fans. Those two accord behaviors need narrative and animation review before being added to the species' ecology. Their battle effect does not mean that filtered water cures every ailment or that mineral deposits can erase a person's distress.

**Decision and counter.** Settling Pulse can recover a partner whose next action would be lost. It cannot recover an activation already spent, and a Glassgrazer that is itself Staggered cannot activate it. Silence can prevent this rescue; protection, a different action order, or accepting the lost turn are alternatives. Shed Screen costs more than Doorling's smaller screen, so the final two points matter only when they actually prevent damage. Ward, Strip, and well-timed Silence answer different parts of this kit.

## Embermole — directed heat with an exposed body

It threatens either row without first dismantling the front. Its lack of Armor and Ward means that leaving it exposed is a decision with consequences. Its two existing attacks remain distinct burst and repeatable options.

| Technique | Tags | Focus / cooldown | Exact steps and targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Furnace Breath** | Accord, Attack, Reach | 1 / 2 | Deal 12 Resonance to one enemy. No secondary effect. |
| **Warm Thread** | Accord, Attack, Reach | 1 / 1 | Deal 8 Resonance to one enemy. No secondary effect. |
| **Coal Cast** | Physical, Attack, Reach | 2 / 2 | Deal 6 Impact to one enemy, then apply Scorch: 4 Resonance at this round's end. Ward and Barrier mitigate the tick normally. |
| **Radiant Cover** | Accord, Reach | 1 / 2 | Give one ally an 8-point Barrier through this round. No cleanse, healing, or Steadfast. |

**Body and behavior.** The attack names describe brief shaped releases of stored warmth, using its existing heat-moving associations. Coal Cast throws a small warmed stone; it does not generate coal. Its Scorch is the lingering heat of that object, so the Physical action can still work while Silenced. Radiant Cover is a proposed short deflecting heat shimmer maintained by a field accord; it is not a wall of unlimited fire. Focus represents combat readiness, not a proof that the creature can supply an industry with perpetual energy. Suitable resting conditions still matter in the world.

**Decision and counter.** Coal Cast offers a costly Physical alternative when Silence prevents the cheaper Accord attacks. Its delayed damage gives an enemy time to cleanse or protect itself. On a Ward-2 target, its Scorch deals only 2 before Barrier, and its Impact portion separately faces Armor. Disarm prevents all three offensive techniques; Radiant Cover, Guard, and legal movement remain. Ordinary warmth, affection, and domestic use do not acquire a hidden horror penalty because the creature can fight.

## Doorling — remove the right obstruction

Its four slots combine the two player and two defender techniques already present in the original example. It is useful before control, after control, and against protection, but can perform only one of those jobs per round.

| Technique | Tags | Focus / cooldown | Exact steps and targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pick the Latch** | Physical, Reach | 1 / 2 | Strip one enemy. Remove its removable positive effects; deal no damage. |
| **Unknot** | Physical, Reach | 1 / 2 | Basic Cleanse one ally. Does not remove Stagger or grant Resolve. |
| **Borrowed Screen** | Physical, Reach | 1 / 2 | Give one ally an 8-point Barrier through this round. No status protection. |
| **Muffle** | Accord, Reach | 1 / 2 | Attempt Silence on one enemy through this round. No damage. |

**Body and behavior.** Physical techniques use nimble folded limbs, carried scraps, and access to the visible seams of temporary battle effects. Muffle proposes a brief, local sound-damping arrangement of those scraps. Its encounter-sized accord neither grows an entire nesting cavity on demand nor silences a distant building. These interpretations require visual review; they do not establish universal lock-opening, unlimited pocket space, or fourth-wall awareness. A physical cleanse resolves the listed combat restrictions, not every possible injury or illness in the setting.

**Decision and counter.** Silence cannot stop its three Physical support techniques. Stagger can deny the action; Unknot cannot remove that Stagger even if another Doorling is available. If it spends its activation stripping a screen, it cannot later cleanse an ally that round. Low HP and enemy Reach pressure punish exposing it without support. A player who watches the intent queue can decide whether removing eight protection now is worth holding no cleanser in reserve.

## What these kits let us examine

[Counterplay Battle](Counterplay%20Battle.md) uses identical benchmark teams to interrupt a planned attack, recover it at a cost, and punish an unnecessary attack into expiring protection. The original [Worked Battle](Worked%20Battle.md) remains a simpler resolved teaching example; its three defenders intentionally have different training values from these full benchmark bodies.

Later paper passes should examine a team without Doorling, a team without a Strong Cleanse, rear-positioned Melee specialists, and a modest level difference. Counters should create decisions without making one species compulsory. Twenty written techniques establish coverage and expose tradeoffs; they do not establish enjoyable pacing, successful balance, or production feasibility.
