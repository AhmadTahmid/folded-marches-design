# Combat learning sequence

Five encounters build from **one partner's choice to a coordinated objective**. Their pleasure should come from a creature, a place and something worth doing. There are no fixed hours, compulsory license or species checklist. Players may skip practice, take either city road and return later; keepers offer voluntary loans. Late arrivals receive explanations without pretending to have met someone before.

These proposals fit the existing [Opening Script](../02%20Narrative/Opening%20Script%20v0.1.md), [First Paid Journey](../02%20Narrative/First%20Paid%20Journey.md) and [Campaign](../02%20Narrative/Campaign%20Draft%20v0.1.md). They add no survey requirements, combine no exclusive bookings and replace no quest outcomes. [Tactical Combat](Tactical%20Combat.md), [Status and Counterplay](Status%20and%20Counterplay.md) and the [benchmark kits](First%20Region%20Creature%20Kits.md) own the rules. Each scene interaction spends its creature's activation, costs zero Focus and grants no protagonist turn. Fixtures remain draft.

## 1. The crooked pennant — choose what one partner does

At Mara's optional cistern practice corner, a red pennant has snagged around its pole. Her Bellwether looks at it, then squares its broad feet toward the player's partner. Mara leaves it crooked: “It likes having something to beat.” Clatter is an example; declining its invitation removes neither the match nor loan access.

The opponent lowers its crescent horns before **Horn Run**. UI: **“Charging at your partner: 12 Impact.”** The primary decision is whether to make damage progress or spend this activation stopping the hit. Win by exhausting the opponent; friendly exhaustion loses. Immediate menu surrender and full practice recovery are available, with retry or departure. Victory is never required for wages or travel.

**Fixture and beat:** use benchmark Bellwethers, both in front, except Mara's has **18 maximum/current HP**, an openly labelled training value. Its intended sequence is Horn Run in round 1, Strike in round 2, then Horn Run whenever ready; otherwise Strike. With an illegal attack it Guards. A round-1 friendly Strike deals 8; the enemy then deals 12. Alternatively, Arresting Peal spends 2 Focus, cancels the unused enemy activation and gives it Resolve through round 2. Peal is ready again in round 4, not round 2.

Repeated Strikes win this fixture in round 3 with 12 HP remaining. Peal, then Horn Run after the enemy's round-2 Strike, then Strike at the friendly opening of round 3 wins with 24 HP. Other choices can lose. Another species needs its own declared practice pairing. The invitation is to enjoy the contest and a partner's response; executing a signature move does not prove enjoyment.

## 2. The flag under the washing line — give partners different jobs

At the exchange, damp cloths cast moving stripes across the enclosure. A marker hangs behind a low rail; the keeper's Thimbleback plants itself underneath a spectacularly unsuitable towel. “Bring back the flag. The towel is its problem.” This expands the existing optional crossing exercise, not the survey's required route.

Two friendly partners face one defender. The single primary decision is **who keeps the defender occupied while the other retrieves**. The defender visibly covers the flag until it has spent or lost its activation that round, or is exhausted. Only then may a standing friendly in marked Rear 1 use **Retrieve flag**, ending the exercise immediately. The command preview states “Wait for the defender to commit”; this condition prevents an immediate first-command retrieval. Win that objective or exhaust the defender and then retrieve; lose if both partners exhaust. Surrender and recovery are immediate. Participation offers the little victory of a shared job, not a better survey wage.

The defender scrapes its claws toward the front partner. UI: **“Strike → Front 1 partner.”** Use a benchmark Thimbleback; its intended Strike targets the named front creature while legal, otherwise the lowest-numbered legal front target, then Guard if none exists.

**Beat:** friendly Bellwether in Front 1 Guards for an 8-point Barrier. The defender's 6 Impact against Armor 0 consumes 6 Barrier. Friendly Thimbleback in Rear 1 spends its activation retrieving; the exercise ends. Both retain 3 Focus. Alternatively, Bellwether can spend 2 Focus on Peal before the defender acts, letting the retriever work without taking that blow. Any legal front partner can use Guard; any standing rear partner can retrieve. A player can also accept survivable damage and save resources. Two partners cooperate without first needing a glossary of setup bonuses.

## 3. Rovan's second answer — a familiar plan meets a counter

On an optional return to the Bell Road exchange, Rovan lays two cups on a crate. His Glassgrazer carefully shelters one beneath a mineral fan while his Bellwether noses the other toward the edge. “You stopped the charge. Now I've brought someone who can help it.” This is the existing early keeper challenge, not his later closure or a compulsory appointment.

Two friendly partners face his front Bellwether and rear Glassgrazer. Win by exhausting both opponents; lose when both friendlies exhaust. Surrender, recovery and a later rematch remain available. The reason to accept is a recognizable rival answering a plan the player already enjoyed.

The primary decision is **when to disrupt a plan whose support can answer**. The Glassgrazer opens the enemy queue: Settling Pulse on a controlled Bellwether, otherwise Guard. Bellwether follows with Horn Run when ready, otherwise Strike at the named front target; an illegal attack becomes Guard. The fan turns toward its partner before the rescue. UI: **“Clears its partner's Stagger before the charge.”**

**Beat:** friendly Bellwether Peals the enemy Bellwether for 2 Focus. Enemy Glassgrazer spends 2 Focus on Settling Pulse, removing Stagger before its activation and granting Resolve through round 2. Friendly Thimbleback then uses Clod Flick for 1 Focus: Resolve rejects Stagger, **not Disarm**. Horn Run becomes illegal, so the enemy Guards. Peal and Pulse return in round 4; Clod Flick in round 3.

Two useful approaches are delaying partial control until the cleanser has acted, or dealing damage while protecting the threatened ally and waiting out the enemy's expiring Barrier. Reach attacks can pressure the rear rescuer. Resolve cannot be stripped; the player's own Peal cooldown is a separate restriction even after Resolve expires. No borrowed duplicate interrupter is required to understand either rule.

## 4. The sling in the white channel — rescue an action, or change the worker

This optional White Orchard incident follows a completed local observation, independently of accepting Pell's extension. A keeper has lowered a sling of survey cases into a mineral-lined channel. A territorial Bellwether blocks retrieval while water lifts the sling against white branches. Its keeper has moved people clear and requests help; the cargo contains replaceable instruments, not an undisclosed dying patient. “The sling is holding. We've got time to think.”

Three friendly slots let a newly acquired or voluntarily loaned third partner matter. The primary decision is **recover the planned worker's activation or assign the task differently**. Any standing friendly in marked Rear 1 may use **Lift sling** to win. At the end of round 3 the current carries the cases to a known lower landing: failure opens recovery work, not lost survey wages. All friendly exhaustion also ends the attempt. Any standing friendly may spend an activation on **Withdraw**, taking everyone to the bank; cargo recovery remains available.

Enemy initiative in round 1 is announced before entry. The front Bellwether fixes its horns on the marked worker: **“Peal → retriever. Enemy acts first.”** Thereafter it plans Horn Run at the lowest-numbered legal front target when ready, otherwise Strike, with Guard as the no-target fallback.

**Beat:** the enemy Peals friendly Thimbleback in Rear 1. Friendly Glassgrazer spends 2 Focus on Settling Pulse before Thimbleback activates. Resolve lasts through round 2; Pulse returns in round 4. With the sole enemy already acted, another partner can Guard and Thimbleback can Lift sling.

Without Strong Cleanse, let Stagger consume its activation; another front partner can Move into Rear 1 by exchanging with it, spending only the mover's activation. Stagger does not forbid that exchange. The new worker can lift at the friendly opening of round 2. Visible slack makes that slower plan credible. Recovery earns thanks and a look at the curious instruments, not automatic recruitment.

## 5. The freight lock's two hands — coordinate five toward passage

The eventual five-active scene develops the campaign's **Captain at the Closed Gate** battle. Beyond Rovan's chalkboard, stretchers wait beneath enormous counterweights. His safety reserve is visible equipment and an announced defensive plan. He tells his crew, “Keep the brakes. Let them spend themselves.” The player wants the freight route open, not a certificate for collecting five particular species.

The primary decision is **which activations serve passage and which keep workers functioning**. Five companions or agreed guest creatures occupy the ordinary five slots; humans add no turns. The surveyed bypass remains available. Any front creature can **Secure brake** on either named brake; two activations secure both. Any rear creature can then **Lift latch**, followed by **Turn wheel** on a separate activation. These four steps persist across rounds and win passage; no species key applies.

Rovan's fixture uses benchmark Doorling in rear, Bellwether and Glassgrazer in front. Round 1 announces Muffle on friendly Glassgrazer, Peal on friendly Embermole, then Fan Ring on friendly Thimbleback. His Bellwether's horns point toward the worker. UI: **“Will stop this worker. Rescue it or use another.”** Later queues announce legal attacks and targets: cooldown or invalid-target fallbacks are Strike against the lowest-numbered legal front creature, otherwise Guard.

**Beat:** use the kits' suggested friendly rows. Thimbleback secures one brake; enemy Doorling Muffles Glassgrazer for 1 Focus. Friendly Bellwether secures the second; enemy Bellwether Peals rear Embermole for 2. Friendly Doorling uses Unknot for 1 to cleanse Glassgrazer's Silence; enemy Glassgrazer's Fan Ring spends 1 and deals 8 to Thimbleback. Friendly Glassgrazer spends 2 on Settling Pulse; Embermole's recovered activation lifts the latch. All five contributed; turning the wheel still needs another round.

Alternatively, accept the lost activation and use the unthreatened rear partner across two rounds, or pressure the interrupter before operating. A smaller team can attempt this objective. All-friendly exhaustion loses; any standing creature may spend an activation withdrawing to camp, retaining campaign evidence but resetting this attempt. Bypass and retry remain. Victory restores access; Rovan's accountability follows the campaign, not automatic pardon.

## Keep the choices visible

For full kits, show all four equipped technique names and Strike/Guard/Move as compact commands from entry. Selection reveals result, cost, next usable round and legal targets; inspection remains free. Emphasize the current threat and one relevant preview without concealing other legal actions. Scene interactions occupy a clearly labelled contextual command, not a free extra turn. Loan cards disclose their actual kit before acceptance; a limited practice kit must be explicitly equipped, never secretly substituted for the player's learned moves.

These scenes establish authored situations and checkable beats, not balanced full encounters or evidence of fun. Later review must distinguish understanding the choice, liking the creatures and voluntarily wanting another encounter. See [Combat Design Review](Combat%20Design%20Review.md) for the separate action-economy comparison and open design judgments.
