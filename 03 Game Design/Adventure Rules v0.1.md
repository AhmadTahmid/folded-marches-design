# Adventure rules v0.1

This is the recommended playable contract for [design v0.1](../00%20Foundation/Game%20Design%20v0.1.md). These choices make one coherent paper game. They are not user approval or claims that a prototype already supports them. Numerical combat details remain owned by [Tactical Combat](Tactical%20Combat.md), [Status and Counterplay](Status%20and%20Counterplay.md) and the [first-region kits](First%20Region%20Creature%20Kits.md).

**Combat development note, 2026-09-28:** [Combat Design Review](Combat%20Design%20Review.md) adds creature identities, optional learning encounters and an action-allocation comparison. The existing individual-activation rules remain this edition's baseline; alternative cards and shared actions are not simultaneous requirements.

## Out in the world

Walk, run, jump, roll, interact and inspect. Movement should be enjoyable even before earning a reward: a quick run-up, a readable leap, a landing with weight and a roll that returns control promptly. Jumping crosses low gaps, stepping stones and marked ledges; rolling is a playful short evasive movement in exploration, not permission to pass through bodies, walls or locked routes. No compulsory stamina wait on safe roads. A contextual climb handles important ascents without requiring precision platforming everywhere.

The protagonist is physically present during exploration. Entering a creature battle changes to the stable turn-based encounter; he does not dodge a second real-time battle behind the command menu. Smooth movement and strategic combat serve different pleasures. A chase can use exploration movement, with pause and an assisted traversal option that retains its route choices.

Every significant route has a visible destination, a junction with a reason to choose, one distinctive interaction and a return improvement. The mountain handline, a flooded ferry court and moving mineral branches should ask different things. A monster ability can make a route delightful or reveal a bonus, but the main campaign also supplies a guide, loan tool or slower ordinary route. Field abilities do not consume combat technique slots.

The map begins with home and heard directions. Walking records connected paths and landmarks automatically; entering a place adds its confirmed shape. A bought sketch marks a lead, not an omniscient survey. Unknown interiors and wildlife conditions stay unknown. Discovered navigation can highlight the next known landmark; the journal states why the player wanted to go there. Late anomalous overlays never destroy the original record.

## Encounter rhythm

Wildlife does not equal an automatic battle. Watching, following, playing, avoiding and helping can all produce useful encounters. Put visible tension at the commitment boundary: a rival holding a crossing, a frightened adult guarding young, a weather front about to close a shelf. The player can prepare, take another route or enter knowing the objective.

A standard route should contain a small number of authored combat situations and many noncombat signs of life. Repeat fights belong in optional keeper grounds or rematches with altered tactics. A battle needs a purpose beyond its XP: learning, recruitment, a rival, protection, recovery or a changed route. A long expedition earns pressure through decisions and investment; its length alone is not content.

## Making a companion

Use three readable beats: **notice what this individual wants, do something interesting, receive its response**. The [roster](../01%20World/Creature%20Roster%20v0.1.md) owns species-specific examples. No repeated hidden capture roll. Some invitations take a minute; others finish a substantial adventure. An animal staying wild can still reward discovery, a route or a friendship that recurs.

The field guide records observations, attempted approaches and the next useful lead. It should say "returns to the warm stones after rain" rather than show a secret affection percentage. Once an invitation is available, the player's choice is explicit. A refusal names a visible circumstance or leaves a discoverable clue. It is not a demand to exhaust random dialogue.

An entrusted egg hatches after two stated expedition/rest milestones, with visible changes between them. Care can stay with a local keeper; no real-world timers, abandonment punishment or mandatory walking circles. It becomes a personality before it becomes a combat slot. This version includes entrusted/rescued eggs, not a breeding factory.

The rare **clasp** is emergency containment, not friendship. This edition recommends three authored opportunities to obtain or borrow one across the campaign, each with another way to solve its associated problem. A card declares compatible targets and release conditions. Binding a dangerous creature for evacuation does not put it obediently in the roster. Rovan's coercive modifications and the Keeper's enclosures can share a recognizable refusal-of-release pattern without making ordinary companionship fraudulent.

Companions rest in living refuges and can be swapped at safe stops. A concise roster view avoids a mandatory walk through a sanctuary for every change. The named individual, learned moves, appearance and relationship history persist. No real-time hunger, neglect decay or daily attendance debt. When an individual wants privacy, it is a scene or field preference, not a surprise ban on the battle team.

## From one partner to five

The opening introduces one partner and a short, optional practice. A later two-creature encounter makes coordination matter. Three, four and five active companions become useful as the player encounters richer objectives and obtains willing partners. There is no compulsory full roster before the first enjoyable battle.

A player who declines every early invitation can still explore. Before an essential creature encounter, a keeper offers a clearly described journey loan and a suitable rest/return arrangement. Borrowing permits progress without pretending an invitation occurred; later partnership remains a distinct choice.

The formation is **three front slots and two rear slots**. All chosen partners are active; there is no mandatory reserve-switch carousel. Select an unused friendly creature, perform one action, then the enemy resolves its next declared activation. Opening sides alternate by round. Each creature has four equipped techniques plus Strike, Guard and Move; early practice uses a clearly stated smaller loan kit. Ordinary combat pauses for the player's decisions.

Creatures start with 3 Focus and recover 1 at each new round from round two, capped at 3. Costs, legal targets, enemy intentions, status expiry and next-ready rounds are visible before commitment. Stagger stops one unused activation; Resolve prevents immediately repeating the lock. Boss phases obey the same meanings. A new body or trump card gets a tell, a visible rule and a viable response; it cannot secretly restore infinite health or invalidate a winning plan.

Teach one relation at a time: interrupt a charge; protect one ally while another works; strip a protection before applying control; move out of a position-targeted hazard; decide whether to spend the last defence now or preserve it for the escape. Exact damage belongs in the owning kit, not in five inconsistent quest documents.

## A cooperative spectacle worth earning

For v0.1, recommend **Duets**: brief joined forms whose silhouette visibly combines two companions for one compound action, then separates. They express a practiced relationship. They are not universal every-pair offspring, and neither individual is consumed or permanently replaced.

Both participants must be standing, unused this round, able to perform the action and each able to pay its printed Focus cost. Confirming the Duet consumes both activations; the compound effect resolves once and the enemy then takes its normal next activation. There is no bonus turn, sixth creature, pooled HP or hidden cleanse. Each partner retains damage, statuses, cooldowns and position. It is unavailable if either cannot legally act. A pair's Duet can be used once per encounter in this draft.

**Worked proposal: Bellwether + Cragmantle, "Stormgate."** A horned stone canopy briefly joins the two silhouettes. Tags: **Accord, Attack, Reach**. Either participant may occupy either row; neither changes position. Silence, Disarm or Stagger on either participant prevents this action; Root alone does not. Each spends 2 Focus. One standing enemy front target takes 16 Impact damage using ordinary mitigation; one chosen standing ally, including either participant, then gains an 8-point Barrier through the round. If the enemy front is empty, either rear creature is a legal target. Damage and Barrier are printed separate steps, and protection does not prevent status effects. The opportunity cost is two activations and four Focus; an enemy can bait the early use or Strip the protection later. These values are a paper comparison, not a replacement for either creature's ordinary kit.

Earn this Duet through a cooperative route scene with both individuals, or use a loan pair in that scene to learn what the system offers. Missing this exact pair cannot block the campaign. Start the design catalogue with a few authored Duets whose visual stories differ; add another only when it supplies a distinct pleasure or tactic. Persistent merged bodies remain a separately retained alternative in [Resonance, Fusion and Last Resorts](Resonance%20Fusion%20and%20Last%20Resorts.md).

## Growth, money and ordinary pleasure

Keep levels and visible growth. This edition uses a **provisional level ceiling of 30** to give the campaign an intelligible paper scale: home/first roads approximately 1–6, city adventures 6–14, difficult expeditions 14–23, final routes 23–30. These are encounter planning bands, not measured play hours or gates on curiosity. A threatening outlier announces itself through behavior and local knowledge.

Meaningful discoveries, completed work and consequential battles award growth; identical trivial repetitions are unnecessary. At safe rest, recruits can reach an earned regional training floor (illustrative floors 5, 12 and 21 after the first three movements). A borrowed high-level creature cannot raise that floor. Technique libraries preserve learned moves; four are equipped at a time. Three compact talent forks offer distinct roles, with free reassignment at safe rest and no healing/reset exploit.

Useful purchases include salves, ordinary travel gear, a night's lodging, ferry passage, an attributed local sketch, gifts someone actually likes and preset clothing. Basic field tools are durable. The first paid job must cover an ordinary rest and leave a meaningful remainder, not start a rent treadmill. Exact coin prices stay in a later economy sheet; the prototype's 18-coin fixture has no authority over this edition.

Family support uses deliberate milestones and conversations, not an ever-ticking debt meter. Paying for Ada's material order, Lina's lessons or a household repair changes a scene and their opportunities. A gift is not a substitute for a missed promise. The player may keep money for a personal luxury without being labelled selfish by a morality score.

Three small optional pleasures anchor this edition: arrange a companion's harmless stage cue with Jori; try an outfit against an actual regional occasion; follow a creature to its absurd little cache. No crafting, cooking or base-building progression is required. Meals and music can be scenes and atmosphere without becoming production chores.

## Failure that creates a next action

Ordinary defeat returns the party to a known safe stop with earned growth and recruited identities intact. The journal states what changed and suggests a practical adjustment. Optional practice costs no lasting resource. No permanent stat injury is the default reward for surviving a frightening scene.

An expedition commitment screen identifies its return point and what can be lost or separated. A retreat can abandon the current job's bonus or leave a route unresolved. A companion separation creates a marked, finite rescue opportunity with a loan helper if needed; it is not an invisible species replacement. Existing exploration elsewhere remains possible, and a rescue never requires the missing creature's own unique ability. The campaign owns any lasting world loss and its warning.

The Latchrunner sequence is a prepared pursuit: learn the terrain and countermeasure first, cross a clearly marked danger boundary, then make short route decisions under pressure. For v0.1 it uses turn-based escape stages with fast visible bursts between choices, as the campaign specifies; there is no hidden input deadline. Catching the party leads into its authored recovery branch, not automatic deletion of a beloved companion. A later real-time comparison can use assisted timing, but is not this edition's default. Permanent-loss alternatives remain in [Tension, Stakes and Recovery](Tension%20Stakes%20and%20Recovery.md), outside this draft's default.

## Horror without breaking the player's tools

The Keeper may address an inspected entry, a remembered choice or the attention behind the protagonist. It knows only authored in-game events. Its claims remain attributable and testable. It may alter a fictional page, a landmark or a safe-looking room; real pause, exit, save restoration, input and readable combat values stay dependable. Refusing a test can produce a different scene rather than a dead end.

Comfort before horror is not filler. Neither is comfort afterwards. A successful rescue needs an aftermath where the creature eats, plays or chooses an inconvenient familiar path again. Let the player feel what was recovered before presenting another crisis.
