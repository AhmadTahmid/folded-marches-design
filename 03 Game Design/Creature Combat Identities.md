# Creature combat identities

These companions should make the player notice different opportunities. These **draft refinements** develop the [Combat Design Review](Combat%20Design%20Review.md); its source assessments do not adopt the external brainstorms. [First Region Creature Kits](First%20Region%20Creature%20Kits.md) retains every existing number and card as the comparison baseline. [Bestiary](../01%20World/Bestiary.md) owns ecology; the gestures and kit alternatives below are proposals.

[Tactical Combat](Tactical%20Combat.md) and [Status and Counterplay](Status%20and%20Counterplay.md) still govern timing: one activation per creature, alternating sides, normally round-end expiry. An unused ally does not necessarily act immediately after setup; the next enemy acts between them. Nothing here adds a type chart, combo meter, required party composition or bonus activation.

## Bellwether — make the moment count

**Fantasy and clue.** The confident companion that finds a gap and expects someone to use it. Its existing herd calls help animals stay connected in fog; a proposed field vignette shows one awaiting an answering call before committing to a narrow path. Clatter's manner remains individual, not compulsory species behavior.

**Signature decision.** Strike now, open armor with Calling Note, or spend two Focus on Arresting Peal before a dangerous unused activation. Calling Note is worthwhile only when subsequent Impact attacks exploit it this round. With Armor 2, each such attack gains at most two damage; forfeiting Bellwether's strong Strike needs a reason beyond “setup is good.” Interrupting the next enemy can instead create a safe interval for another partner's ordinary action.

**Weakness and partnership.** Armor 0 defeats the Exposed plan; Silence blocks its three Accord techniques; Steadfast and Resolve answer Stagger. Doorling can strip Steadfast first, but the intervening enemy activation can disrupt that sequence. Bellwether can also attack alone: its identity does not require a designated finisher.

**Gesture.** It plants its broad feet, turns one horn toward the target and breaks the wind-up with a sharp head movement; the target visibly loses its committed stance. A brief “Action stopped” label carries the same information without sound.

## Thimbleback — spoil the tidy plan

**Fantasy and clue.** An absurdly serious improvisor that brought something useful, perhaps not what anyone requested. Existing cache sorting supports this personality. A proposed observation shows it bracing a slipping nut with a clod, then pocketing the clod too. Field collection need not become ammunition homework.

**Signature decision.** Watch what the enemy intends to do with its body. Clod Flick Disarms an Attack before it resolves; Burrow Grip Roots a creature before a planned row exchange. Against an enemy already settled in position, a brief Root achieves little. Nut Pitch can reach the enemy rear without repositioning, trading disruption for immediate progress.

**Weakness and partnership.** Its own Disarm blocks Clod Flick and both damaging techniques. Armor reduces its Impact offense; it cannot remove a partner's restriction. Bellwether's Exposed can make a thrown nut matter, while Thimbleback's interruption gives any vulnerable ally room to act. Neither interaction is an exclusive combination.

**Gesture.** For Clod Flick, it searches one cup, rejects a prized pebble, selects an unimpressive clod and snaps it into the attacker's working limb. Recovery includes checking that the treasured pebble is still there. The action remains one readable toss, with no extra hit hidden in the joke.

## Glassgrazer — bring a friend back into the plan

**Fantasy and clue.** A substantial, patient animal whose careful attention feels reassuring. Existing shed fans and favored rubbing stones give its protection physical weight. A proposed field moment lets the player see it patiently free a shed plate caught against a stone; trained rescue resonance remains a combat proposal, not proof that mineral filtration cures distress.

**Signature decision.** Retain its activation and two Focus for Settling Pulse, or use them now for damage or protection. Strong Cleanse can remove Stagger only before the ally's activation is consumed. It gives Resolve through the following round; it neither refunds a spent turn nor heals HP. Holding Glassgrazer too long risks losing the rescuer's own action before it can help.

**Weakness and partnership.** Silence or Stagger on Glassgrazer can break the rescue plan. Bellwether's proactive Steadfast offers insurance, at the cost of Bellwether's attack. A rescued Embermole may deliver pressure, but rescuing a Doorling that can clear another obstruction may matter more. The threatened plan, not a fixed damage ranking, should decide.

**Gesture.** Six feet settle in sequence, its soft flank remains visible between the fans, and one controlled ripple crosses the mineral growths. The ally uncurls or regains its stance. Avoid a generic healing burst: the visible outcome is restored freedom to act.

## Embermole — make delay expensive

**Fantasy and clue.** A contented creature that becomes alarmingly decisive when it opens its back plates. The established warmth of a favored brick suggests stored heat with material limits. A proposed observation shows it warming a cooler sleeping hollow, then abandoning the now-less-comfortable source.

**Signature decision.** Furnace Breath reaches a vulnerable target now; Coal Cast supplies a costly Physical attack and an end-round Scorch when Silence blocks Accord attacks. Delayed pressure gives the enemy a chance to cleanse or shield, but spending that response can divert it from another plan. Do not describe damage as guaranteed merely because the first impact landed.

**Weakness and partnership.** Ward reduces Resonance; Disarm blocks all three offensive baseline techniques; Reach pressure threatens its low defenses. Doorling can strip a Barrier before an attack, while any partner's damage can wear the Barrier down before Scorch ticks. Waiting for help risks letting the target act first.

**Gesture.** Plates lift from front to back; the body compresses before a focused release, then settles with a disgruntled whisker twitch. Coal Cast instead gets a distinct paw flick and a visible warm object at the target. Text shows the tick's damage and round; color alone never announces danger.

## Doorling — find what is holding things together

**Fantasy and clue.** A busy dismantler, pleased that a forbidding obstruction has a loose end. Existing nest scraps and attraction to seams ground the idea. A proposed encounter lets it tug the wrong scrap, inspect the result, and confidently choose another. This implies no menu awareness or universal lock-opening.

**Signature decision.** Pick the Latch removes an enemy's removable protection; Unknot removes an ally's Basic restrictions. Spend its action opening an attack now, or retain it until an ally needs freeing. Borrowed Screen remains a modest fallback when neither removal has value. Casting it over a larger Barrier wastes protection rather than stacking it.

**Weakness and partnership.** Low HP and enemy Reach punish careless exposure. Unknot cannot remove Stagger, and Strip cannot remove Resolve. Its Physical support works while Silenced, distinguishing it from Glassgrazer's stronger rescue. It can free a Silenced Bellwether, which may then protect another partner; enemy alternation still separates those actions.

**Gesture.** Doorling flattens, catches a visible seam in the temporary effect and backs out with an exaggerated tug. The obstruction comes apart; it briefly inspects the useless scrap. Cleansing removes the restraint motif, without implying that all injuries are detachable objects.

## Reduce repeated protection through controlled alternatives

Cupguard, Shed Screen, Radiant Cover and Borrowed Screen all add Barriers, alongside free Guard. Different particles would not fix that overlap. Keep useful redundancy, but compare these changes **separately against the unchanged baseline**, rather than replacing every support action at once.

| Candidate | Exact proposed departure | Decision it should improve |
|---|---|---|
| **Escorting Screen**, replacing Glassgrazer's Shed Screen | Physical, Reach; 2 Focus / cooldown 2. Exchange rows with one standing ally, then give that ally 10 Barrier through this round. Requires opposite rows and neither participant Rooted; no self-target. Consumes Glassgrazer's activation; the exchanged ally retains its acted/unacted state and gains no extra action. | Rescue includes committing the large body to a different position. Creature-targeted Reach still follows the ally; movement is no blanket dodge. |
| **Banked Coal**, replacing Embermole's Radiant Cover | Accord, Attack, Reach; 1 Focus / cooldown 3. Apply Scorch 6 to one standing enemy in either row through **end of next round**, with no immediate damage. It ticks at each round end while present, reduced by Ward and Barrier. | Threaten sustained pressure instead of carrying another identical ally screen. Cleanse can prevent both ticks if used before the first. Existing nonstacking Scorch rules prevent Coal Cast adding a second tick. |

Retain Cupguard for this comparison: its self-only 12 competes with free Guard's 8, so spending Focus should visibly prevent otherwise meaningful damage. Retain Doorling's cheap Physical screen as insurance. If those choices remain automatic or pointless, revise them after seeing the alternative pairs; do not declare balance from written differentiation.

Combat-essential information must be on the action preview: legal targets, lost or retained activation, exact removal, mitigation, cost and expiry. Lore helps the player anticipate behavior; it is never a prerequisite for knowing a rule. Gestures remain recognizable with reduced motion, captions and skipped animation. Review whether players describe five different decisions and memorable companions, including teams missing any one species. These pages establish a design comparison, not proof of enjoyable play or accepted art.
