# Encounter design

## Selected direction: deliberate turn-based encounters

On 2026-09-26 the user selected strategic, technical turn-based combat with up to five friendly creatures active together, positioning, and coordinated abilities. Dota 2 is a major source of inspiration for mastery and counterplay. A modest field shows relative positions and a few meaningful environmental features. Each round offers readable intentions, creature techniques, protection, or repositioning. De-escalation is available where the encounter supports it.

The intended pleasure is understanding a creature and assembling a response. [Tactical Combat](Tactical%20Combat.md) now owns the proposed turn order, formation, resource and damage rules; [Status and Counterplay](Status%20and%20Counterplay.md) owns effects and removal. [Worked Battle](Worked%20Battle.md) follows a complete small challenge with explicit HP, Focus and cooldown accounting. These detailed mechanics remain draft even though the broad combat direction is selected.

## Encounter decisions beyond dealing damage

- **Coordinate:** expose a target, remove its protection, then commit an attacker; the opponent can interrupt that sequence.
- **Protect:** shelter a threatened partner, cleanse a restriction, or guard an objective. These spend an activation rather than adding free reactions.
- **Inspect:** read visible intentions and known rules freely. An explicitly authored investigation action can reveal something beyond that baseline; basic tactical information is never a mandatory observation tax.
- **Reposition:** exchange formation rows, occupy a sheltered position, or move a threatened bystander where the encounter defines that interaction.
- **Offer / withdraw:** a context-specific invitation or withdrawal replaces one creature's activation. No separate free protagonist turn is assumed. Ordinary supervised challenges allow immediate surrender from their menu; dangerous encounters state the exit condition before it matters.

These are encounter purposes, not five additional commands on every creature. The common command panel remains Strike, Guard, Move and four equipped techniques. An encounter-specific interaction should clearly replace one activation and identify who can perform it.

## Roles grow from bodies and behavior

A creature that bends reeds into shelters may defend, reshape lanes, or protect a frightened ally. A territorial singer may interrupt a charged action but attract unwanted attention. A carrion eater may be a clever opportunist without being a horror monster. A dazzling creature can have an awkward limitation that creates personality and tactical choices.

Species pages own ecological facts. This page owns common encounter principles. Individual technique lists should link to the species and avoid rewriting its lore.

## Example encounter: a crossing under dispute

This encounter is a generic mechanical sketch, not a named canon quest. A large nesting animal occupies a narrow causeway. It signals before sweeping the approach with a tail; smaller scavengers exploit the disturbance. The player can battle from safe patches, withdraw and find a longer route, return with a calming resource, or identify a blocked nesting channel and clear it.

Controlled victory grants immediate passage during an urgent crossing and can establish the animal's respect for a later challenge. Protecting the nest during the fight gives a concrete tactical objective alongside winning. Reckless damage can worsen nesting conditions, but ordinary competent combat is not automatically destructive. Clearing the channel takes longer and requires a resource or helper, but keeps the crossing open for a later caravan. The alternate route costs travel and offers its own discovery. These approaches have different benefits; peaceful resolution does not automatically grant every reward.

## Battles people and creatures enjoy

Supervised challenge grounds, expedition practice, rival keepers, and species-specific contests provide joyful tests of a growing team. Winning can earn techniques, unusual equipment, distinctive invitations, and recognition. Some wild encounters are dangerous enough that skilled defense is necessary and worthwhile. The design should allow the player to enjoy being good at battle without repeatedly implying that fighting was the morally inferior answer.

See the Bellwether challenge in [Companionship and Catching](Companionship%20and%20Catching.md) and the optional games in [Creature Activities](Creature%20Activities.md).

## Injury, defeat, and stakes

**Latest user answer, 2026-09-27:** compare lasting consequences with recovery or rescue (D-049). [Tension, Stakes and Recovery](Tension%20Stakes%20and%20Recovery.md) develops immediate retry, partial retreat, separation/rescue and lasting-change alternatives, plus a bridge-defense scene. Permanent companion death remains unselected. [Travel, Mapping and Pursuit](Travel%20Mapping%20and%20Pursuit.md) compares a bounded chase with a turn-based escape; neither silently replaces ordinary turn-based encounters. The user also requests fewer filler battles; each encounter should offer a purpose beyond routine XP repetition.

The initial proposal uses exhaustion, disrupted accords, damaged equipment, and changed local circumstances as ordinary defeat costs. Permanent companion death is not assumed. A dark narrative does not require an unforgiving default combat penalty.

Special story stakes must be explained before the player commits, even when the fictional explanation is incomplete. Uncertainty about an entity's motive can coexist with clarity that entering its chamber is a consequential choice.

## What the next paper encounters should challenge

The old action-based alternative is superseded by the user's turn-based selection. The open questions concern the best turn-based structure, not a parallel action game.

- A five-creature opponent breaks a predictable setup and rewards changing activation order.
- A positional encounter makes moving worthwhile without requiring a compulsory mount or rare species.
- A protected enemy can be beaten by both a Strip composition and a composition that survives its protection window.
- An encounter offers a meaningful way to retreat or recover after a companion is exhausted; failure does not imply an hour of replacement grinding.
- A late uncanny opponent obeys an investigable rule, with reliable command and save behavior preserved.

## What would prove the concept later

- Five active creatures produce meaningful coordination without excessive repeated command work.
- A player can infer an enemy behavior without reading a long glossary.
- De-escalation and retreat are practical options.
- The same encounter is readable on a phone and a PC display.
- A familiar rule remains dependable during a horror encounter; any exception has evidence and a recoverable interpretation.
