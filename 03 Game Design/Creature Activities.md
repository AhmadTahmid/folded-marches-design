# Creature activities

These activities are allowed to be delightful without a hidden moral lesson or horror payoff. They give the player reasons to learn creatures' habits, develop a team, explore, and return to familiar places.

Exact scoring, duration, rewards, and control schemes remain untested. Species capabilities follow the [Bestiary](../01%20World/Bestiary.md).

The September note welcomes minigames if they are enjoyable and reward exploration, without selecting crafting or base-building progression. [Everyday Joy, Trade and Appearance](Everyday%20Joy%20Trade%20and%20Appearance.md) compares additional activities, rewards and non-chore rest. The activities below remain optional; perfect performance never supplies the only viable main-story route.

## The Ribbon Run

**Where:** a grassy practice terrace outside Rocca Selva, with visiting competitions in Istravel's wind gardens.

**Invitation:** children and adult keepers are arguing about whether a Kitefin that crossed the finish ribbon backward has won twice. The protagonist is offered a borrowed light harness and a place on the course.

**What happens:** the player guides a Kitefin through a sequence of hanging cloth hoops, using ground positions and familiar signals. The creature carries only a small pennant, never a person. Wind shifts make one ambitious shortcut unreliable; a slower route offers stable currents. Another competitor is extremely fast but cannot resist a fluttering audience hat.

**Player choices:** practice a precise route, use a companion's temperament to attempt a flourish, or try a cooperative relay. A player without a Kitefin can partner with a resident individual after a short introduction. Access to an ordinary game should not require a rare catch.

**Reward:** colored pennants, a camp decoration, a creature portrait moment, and a light harness design where appropriate. The first completed course gives a useful reward regardless of rank. Victory adds recognition and optional variants, not essential story access.

**Reason to return:** changed wind and hoop arrangements, a familiar rival's new trick, seasonal team relays. The joking argument over the backward finish can recur with increasingly elaborate evidence.

**No hidden turn:** the course remains a course. Later political or supernatural changes elsewhere do not need to corrupt it.

## The Thimbleback Treasure Exchange

**Where:** the Staired Wood's gathering camp.

**Invitation:** a keeper has laid out a collection of harmless trinkets for local Thimblebacks to inspect. One repeatedly exchanges the grand prize for a particularly unconvincing stone.

**What happens:** several animals select, hide, and trade objects according to readable individual preferences. The player watches a short round, then predicts where an object will end up or joins the exchange with a companion. A bold animal takes a shortcut over a basket; a fussy one rejects anything wet; the stone enthusiast has priorities nobody shares.

**Player choices:** observe more closely, trade strategically, follow a burrow-side route, or let a bonded Thimbleback participate on its own terms. An overconfident guess produces a comic reveal, not item loss. The animals exchange provided play objects; player quest items are excluded.

**Reward:** small cosmetic finds, gathering hints, individual portraits, and a friendly short route to a picnic ledge. The rarest reward can simply be an animal trusting the protagonist enough to show its favorite useless possession.

**Reason to return:** familiar individuals change their preferences slightly with the season; the camp acquires new handmade puzzles. Knowledge matters more than repeated grinding.

**No hidden turn:** none of the trinkets must be a cursed artifact. An optional strange object belongs in a separate clearly authored story, if ever chosen.

## A Concert for the Wrong Cave

**Where:** a harmless limestone cavity near the Old Switchback, used by local musicians.

**Invitation:** a traveling ensemble booked the wrong cavern. Its echoes turn a solemn performance into an accidental dance tune. Their audience likes the mistake.

**What happens:** arrange simple resonant objects and coordinate Bellwether calls with percussion. The puzzle is about sequence, spacing, and listening rather than fast rhythm input. Visual pulses and a textual pattern make the same relationships readable without sound.

**Player choices:** restore the planned melody, compose a local variation from discovered motifs, or deliberately lean into the comic version. A resident animal and musician make the activity available without a required party composition.

**Reward:** a camp song, a small performance scene, an optional motif the player's companion recognizes, and an invitation to return with more sounds from later travels.

**Reason to return:** new instruments and regional musicians change the arrangement. A late visit can be an ordinary warm reunion even after the main story becomes frightening.

**No hidden turn:** the echo is acoustics plus a small established accord, not a voice announcing the cosmic plot.

## Activity design rules

Reward participation, offer optional mastery, avoid exhausting repetition, and let creature personality matter. Challenges can be funny without making the creatures incompetent props. Cosmetic and expressive rewards can carry value when the player already cares about the team.

These are authored activities, not a commitment to a generalized minigame framework. Pick the strongest after the core interaction style is settled.
