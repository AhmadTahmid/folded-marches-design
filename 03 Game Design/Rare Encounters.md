# Rare encounters

The user wants curiosity rewarded through exceptional creatures appearing by chance or under conditions such as rain and night, with their existence made apparent to players. The rules below are proposals for delivering that pleasure without asking players to grind a spawn table. Species behavior belongs in the [Bestiary](../01%20World/Bestiary.md).

## The possibility should be visible before the creature is

An intriguing empty place needs evidence of what could happen there: large pads above a washed gorge, shed vanes caught on a thermal marker, old reed crossings interrupted by fresh crescent bites. A traveler can recognize these immediately as something worth investigating, even before knowing the species. Local conversations, field-journal sketches, and overheard route reports provide equivalent leads. No crucial clue depends solely on a tiny color difference or an inaudible call.

The journal distinguishes **observed**, **reported**, and **untested**. “Seen feeding here after gentle rain” is better than an unexplained rarity percentage. Once the player confirms the rule, the note identifies the relevant conditions plainly. Discovery preserves surprise by withholding the creature's exact appearance and behavior, not by hiding the rule forever.

## Two ways to earn the same meeting

**Ambient surprise:** eligible habitats use weighted opportunities when game-world conditions fit. An exceptional individual might appear during an ordinary journey. Exact probabilities remain unchosen. Rare does not have to mean one chance in thousands; a limited habitat and distinctive event can create rarity without punitive odds.

**Authored investigation:** the same individual has a reachable tracking sequence. Find two independent current signs, infer its destination, and follow it to a resting or feeding site. This sequence guarantees the meeting once its stated conditions are satisfied; no second invisible capture lottery follows. Players can begin it immediately from sufficient evidence, without first accumulating failed rolls. A rare recruit follows [Companionship and Catching](Companionship%20and%20Catching.md); seeing an animal guarantees neither willingness nor automatic ownership, but the interaction and any retry remain intelligible.

Proposed opportunity rules:

- Draw ambient opportunities once for a meaningful habitat visit, preserve them through a save/reload, and avoid rerolling on every step or screen transition. The authored route makes knowing this policy unnecessary for normal play.
- Keep discovered leads until resolved. A missed departure leaves tracks and a next opportunity; a startled individual settles at a discoverable refuge. It does not disappear forever because the player opened a menu.
- Ordinary exploration through a new habitat always offers information or another point of interest, even when the exceptional animal does not appear. Do not substitute endless consolation materials for the actual lead.
- Unique story encounters that cannot be repeated must not hold an exclusive collectible. Important habitat changes relocate a continuing opportunity, with a field note explaining where to resume.
- Partners have sidegrades, habits, and visual distinctions. Rare individuals do not monopolize essential traversal or universally superior combat statistics. Observation-only species still earn completed records and substantial discoveries.

## Time and weather are tools the player can plan around

Rain, dusk, dawn, humidity, and clear intervals use **simulated game time**, never the player's real clock, date, paid access, or online login schedule. Forecasts describe a bounded local horizon. At a safe shelter, the player can choose an announced interval directly—“wait for tomorrow's clearing”—with a clear preview of any relevant active quest consequences. Menu time does not silently consume the encounter window.

For a weather-dependent investigation, the lead supplies a reliable forecast or an alternate sheltered observation site. The player must not repeatedly sleep to roll for rain. A completed investigation can provide a journal travel appointment at the next suitable interval, replayable if declined. Seasonal stories can exist, but ownership opportunities do not depend on waiting real months or missing a one-time calendar date.

## Three encounters with different rewards

| Lead | What the player learns and does | Result beyond a catch |
|---|---|---|
| A copper mark above the Old Switchback | Compare Cragmantle pads, recent runoff, and a mason's rain account; follow the washed wall at dusk | A spring shelf, a safe survey path, and evidence of an older public water route |
| Pollen falling upward | Ask a keeper or observe rising seed clusters after a clear afternoon; follow Sailrook vanes to a resting ledge | A view showing that two apparently separate paths meet, plus the thermal route's landing logic |
| A gap in the reflected lanterns | Watch wild Morrow Moths move to the sheltered side of Low Lantern Fen before a wind change | A weather insight, field-record illustration, and a safer crossing; the moths remain wild |

The knowledge remains useful after the first encounter. A forecast that once led to a rare animal can later guide an ordinary journey. Changed tracks can reveal altered water flow or a new predator before an NPC explains it. Later horror may contradict a well-learned rule, but should leave independent evidence; random unreliability cannot be the default explanation for rare encounters.

## Expansion and review

Add species when they contribute a distinct silhouette, ecological relationship, meeting, or field/combat decision. Image generation can explore appearance; it does not establish animation, readable behavior, balancing, or asset readiness. First prove on paper that several encounter sequences remain interesting when the surprise is known. Later prototypes should test whether players infer an opportunity, find the deterministic alternative, and enjoy the actual meeting without consulting an external guide.
