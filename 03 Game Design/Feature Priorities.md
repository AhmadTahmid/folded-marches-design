# Feature priorities

## The current next step

**D-047: comprehensive wiki development now; implementation later.** The personal opening, combat examples and [First Region](../02%20Narrative/First%20Region.md) are drafted, and the separate browser outing has been delivered and reviewed. [Wiki Development Plan](../04%20Production/Wiki%20v0.1%20Checklist.md) sets the current writing priorities; September Design Expansion (working-archive reference) assesses the new intensity, attachment, horror and everyday-life ideas. D-035/D-036 still govern the preserved browser foundation, but do not instruct another build during this phase. The independent wiki governs content; useful reference techniques and temporary assets do not choose the game's fiction or mechanics.

Current writing deepens companion adventures and conversations, compares observer/sanctuary horror, makes stakes recoverable and clear, develops atmospheric places and discovery-led travel, and compares recruitment/fusion/trump-card alternatives. Ordinary fun and a legible opening remain necessary; the new adrenaline goal is not permission to frontload five creatures, clutter or punitive systems.

The user selected combat and exploration as current priorities. Cooking, crafting, and base building remain possibilities. They are not prerequisites quietly assumed by another system.

## Cooking, crafting, and a home: why each might belong

| Candidate | Strong reason to include it | Version worth comparing later | Risk to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cooking | Expresses companionship, place, hospitality, and recovery | A short collection of regional meals and camp scenes; small optional preparation choices | Optimal food becoming a compulsory pre-battle tax; ingredient farming replacing adventure |
| Light crafting | Lets discoveries produce useful tools and personal equipment | Repair, a few field tools, cosmetic customization, and understandable recipes | Hundreds of interchangeable materials and menu work with little decision value |
| Personal home or sanctuary | Gives companions a lived place and makes return emotionally meaningful | Furnished village rooms or a shared sanctuary with visible creature preferences | A freeform construction game competing with the RPG for camera, controls, assets, and attention |
| Full base building | Could make a lasting community the subject of play | A separate comparison only if the user later wants this to be central | Passive income timers, raids during absence, or maintenance obligations that punish exploration |

These are neither rejected forever nor selected for the current design. Camps and ordinary meals may exist as setting and scenes without a cooking progression system. A companion can have a place to rest without a construction mechanic.

## A large roster, developed in purposeful groups

The user welcomes many creatures and image generation can help explore visual ideas. The roster should grow through groups that add something players can notice: a new silhouette, habitat, travel style, team role, interaction, or unusual relationship. Additional designs need not all be rare or combat partners.

Concept art is one step. A usable creature also needs consistent anatomy, animation, characteristic behavior, encounter design, sound, readability, and an appropriate production route. Those are creative opportunities to document as well as future production costs. The existing Character and Creature Workflow (working-archive reference) records that distinction.

The current expansion adds a pair of different approaches to each travel domain rather than selecting a final roster size. Subsequent groups can cover habitat ecologies, specialists, unusual social behavior, and opposing tactical roles. Do not fill a numerical quota with nearly identical designs or assume concept-generation capacity proves implementation throughput.

## Design work supporting the next playable stages

Use these exercises to resolve specific gaps in the connected outing. They do not require finishing every draft or enlarging the roster before implementation can continue.

1. Review the new [Family at Home](../02%20Narrative/Family%20at%20Home.md) and [First Paid Journey](../02%20Narrative/First%20Paid%20Journey.md) scenes. Refine his mother and siblings, the work opportunity, and how he expresses existing duty alongside desire.
2. Walk through the five-creature battle, deliberately trying a poor sequence and a better sequence. Revise timing or abilities where understanding does not improve the outcome.
3. Follow a journey using weather signs, a rare encounter, and more than one travel approach. Check that curiosity produces reliable progress even when chance produces no immediate sighting.
4. Review a few creature talent forks and field roles in those same situations. Add only the concepts that produce useful decisions.
5. Choose a visual direction for a small reference set when the user wants concept images. Then ask which additional roster group would enrich the world.

The authorized expansion is drafted: [First Region](../02%20Narrative/First%20Region.md), [First Companion](../02%20Narrative/First%20Companion.md), four travelers, two quests, [five creature kits](First%20Region%20Creature%20Kits.md), and a [counterplay example](Counterplay%20Battle.md). A useful subsequent pass is one shared evening, one reunion after disagreement, and an opposing roster with different species. Select which relationships and combat roles deserve more depth before adding another large group.

The assistant should use the user's preferred question pop-ups for a few consequential choices at a time, with space for a custom answer. Routine linking, continuity corrections, and transcription of already-made decisions do not need repeated approval.
