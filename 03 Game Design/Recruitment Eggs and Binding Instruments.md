# Recruitment, eggs and binding instruments

**v0.1 reading:** [Adventure Rules v0.1](Adventure%20Rules%20v0.1.md) selects one working interpretation for this edition. The comparisons below remain useful alternatives; they are not all simultaneous requirements. User-selected turn-based combat and the five-active ceiling still govern.

The user does not want catching to centre on repeated hidden Poké Ball-like probability rolls. They suggest eggs, quest/condition recruitment and a few powerful instruments associated with advanced or forbidden use. These are separate possibilities, not permission to implement every route. [Companionship and Catching](Companionship%20and%20Catching.md) retains the proposed voluntary field accord and owns the basic relationship model.

## The catch should resolve an intelligible action

An invitation succeeds because the player met a visible condition, completed a challenge or received an individual's response. The interface can say **ready to invite**, **needs more space**, **already bonded elsewhere**, or **will return under these conditions**. Do not repeatedly consume items while secretly rerolling the same situation. Rare encounters may still include chance sightings; reliable investigation offers another path to meeting them.

This is not a requirement for a lengthy consent quest for every common animal. Preserve fast delightful recruitment, skillful challenges, substantial personal quests and beings that never become party members. Knowing a creature is still a discovery when it remains wild.

| Route | Core player pleasure | Example and confirmation | Failure or decline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quick invitation | A memorable small interaction resolves into a new friend | The button collector offers its prized object beside the token | It stays reachable; the player keeps the useful observation |
| Earned challenge | Mastery impresses an independent creature | A supervised Bellwether repeats the successful opening signal | Advice and normal retry, not more consumable rolls |
| Shared work | A quest creates a reason to travel together | A rescued local individual joins a defined journey | Help remains meaningful even if it chooses another home |
| Ecological conditions | Discovery rewards reading place and time | Rain signs lead to a known ledge and a partner opportunity | Absence leaves signs; the journal states what to try next |
| Egg stewardship | Anticipation becomes an individual relationship | A keeper entrusts an abandoned or voluntarily shared clutch member | No invisible hatch failure for missing a daily visit |
| Rare containment | A dangerous exception forces a consequential tool choice | An instrument holds one compatible entity briefly for evacuation | A readable partial result or escape, not universal capture |

## Eggs without a breeding factory

Egg acquisition must fit a species. Compare a keeper-entrusted egg, recovery after a damaged nesting site, a legitimately traded egg with known provenance, and a found egg whose parentage needs investigation. Taking from an active wild nest is a different act with different consequences; do not silently make it the fastest normal collection strategy.

**Worked candidate:** a storm damages a Cloud Loom shelter. Keepers identify one recoverable egg needing a stable chamber while the colony relocates. The protagonist can carry it on a short sheltered route, ask a courier to take it, or help repair local care. A species-appropriate adult will eventually be authored; this page does not invent Kitefin breeding rules or select which species provides the egg.

During two stated story/rest milestones, the shell shows a readable change and a brief movement. The player can inspect or let the keeper handle it. No real-time incubation countdown, compulsory walking in circles, daily heating chores or surprise mortality while the user is away. Returning to the nest habitat remains a valid option.

Hatching should introduce personality immediately: a sound, a choice of cover, a stubborn attempt to carry something too large. It need not create a forced obligation to fight with a newborn. Field acclimation and catch-up growth can make a later combat recruit usable without trivial battle farming. Exact age/biology and training scale remain open.

For a collector, eggs reveal silhouette hints or an attributed species identification. **Compare** known-species eggs with a small number of mystery eggs whose source limits the possibilities. A random premium hatch machine or repeatedly discarded offspring would reintroduce the gimmick the user dislikes. Breeding is not selected by proposing eggs.

## A very limited instrument: working name, the clasp

This is a draft instrument, not a new glossary fact. Its proposed operation arrests a compatible accord's **return boundary** and holds one localized manifestation for a short stated period. It does not establish friendship, erase an apex creature's will or shrink every cosmic being into a pocket.

Compare two uses before selecting one:

- **Emergency containment:** a publicly regulated device prevents an injured or panicked being from destroying a crowded route while specialists arrange release. It can save lives. Its size, compatibility and finite duration are shown before use.
- **Coercive appropriation:** a modified device refuses the creature's exit and suppresses a visible response, producing forced availability. A prohibited network or particular contractor might use it. This is proposed wrongdoing by identifiable actors, not proof that all Istravel residents or all advanced technology are evil.

Orthe may permit one narrow emergency use while publicly condemning broader dependence; Istravel may contain both advocates and opponents of coercive versions. Those are potential local arguments, not replacements for the city pages. “Forbidden players” is treated as a prompt for forbidden practitioners unless the user clarifies another meaning; it does not introduce multiplayer.

## What makes rarity interesting

Offer perhaps a small, authored supply rather than a grindable consumable, but **no count is selected**. Disclose what it can hold, what it costs, how release works and what happens to a captive. Scarcity should create an interesting decision among known uses, not force hoarding forever because a hidden future boss might require it.

A main story objective has another viable path. A player can lend or sell the device only with a clear understanding of who gains access. Buying one should not replace exploring a habitat or forming a relationship. If a legitimate containment later supports partnership, the creature's independent invitation remains a separate step.

**Horror connection:** the instrument's seal or release behavior can recur in the sanctuary enclosure. A laboratory record and two observable devices establish the correspondence. The threatening inference is that a human technique helps a larger being learn how to keep a person—not that the player was evil for accepting an ordinary companion.

## Rare entities and extraction expeditions

The quoted twenty-to-forty-five-minute containment ritual is best treated as an occasional expedition, not the usual catch. Most of its time should contain decisions and discoveries, not a progress bar or repeated weakening. Retreat preserves route information, useful materials and rescued lives. A later attempt skips solved preparatory work where reasonable.

The climax can be turn-based boundary stabilization: maintain two disclosed anchor conditions through several rounds, with enemy intentions that threaten them. A separate real-time version is a comparison only. A failed attempt may release the target, alter a route or separate a partner under the recovery design; it does not automatically kill an existing team.

Some ancient beings grant a pact, route or knowledge and remain uncaught. Their scale is not undermined simply to satisfy a roster checklist.

## Adoption questions and continuity

Choose which species use eggs, whether hatch identity is known, how ordinary invitation is confirmed, what a rare device actually contains and where coercive exceptions originate. Keep recruitment, storage and fusion distinct. No innate collection gift, deterministic catch model, device count or economy value becomes adopted merely because it is described here.
