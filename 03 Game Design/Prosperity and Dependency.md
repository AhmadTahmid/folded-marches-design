# Prosperity and dependency

## A prosperous world needs visible beneficiaries

Show what changed: fewer children dying of winter fever, hillside crops that survive late frost, public baths with warm water, safer childbirth, easier travel, inexpensive light, time for scholarship and festivals. The Largesse has real value before its dangers become apparent.

The village still remembers scarcity through habits: saving string, drying chestnuts, communal repairs, teasing someone for wasting warmth. In prosperous districts these habits can appear quaint. This contrast can communicate history without a lecture.

## Four layers of a miracle

Each major civic use should have an entry or a section that identifies:

1. **Benefit:** whose life improves and how.
2. **Maintenance:** who cares for the creatures, land, and infrastructure that sustain it.
3. **Dependency:** what happens if it stops, and what alternatives remain.
4. **Authority:** who can expand, ration, change, or refuse it.

Example proposal: a district's warm-water network depends on creatures whose breeding pools must cool each winter. Continuous service saves lives, but preventing the seasonal cooling harms the population sustaining it. Engineers propose cultivated substitute pools; keepers ask for a planned shutdown; residents with medical needs cannot simply do without heat. This conflict has material stakes without a single obvious villain.

## Everyday economy

[Everyday Joy, Trade and Appearance](Everyday%20Joy%20Trade%20and%20Appearance.md) extends purchases into guides, durable tools, preset clothes, performances and known-provenance eggs without establishing prices or a maintenance grind. The [old-quay candidate](../01%20World/Atmospheric%20Frontiers.md#the-old-quay) explores a locally bypassed economy; it does not overturn the broader world's real prosperity or treat poverty as moral purity.

Money can handle routine trade, while access to rare accord services depends on training, seasonal resources, community agreements, or trust. Avoid making every social relationship purchasable. Equally, do not require personal favors for every basic item; ordinary commerce should stay convenient.

Markets can contain inexpensive magical conveniences and costly ordinary goods: a self-warming cup beside imported salt, a floating delivery basket whose woven handle needs repair, or an exquisite handmade chair prized because no accord shaped it. Abundance changes value unevenly.

## Political consequences as visible services

Support for a project might change a clinic's hours, a migration route, market prices, a public garden, or a community's obligations. Use authored changes the player can recognize. Simulating a complete regional economy is not required to tell this story.

The city-states should trade and rely on one another despite rivalry. Restraint can rely on imports produced by expansion elsewhere; expansion can rely on independent seed stores, skilled repair, or refuges maintained by its critics.

## A proposed measurement problem

Istravel might measure success through preventable suffering and service coverage. Orthe might prioritize reversibility and independent capacity. Confluence communities might value continuity of relationship across changes of body. None of these measurements captures everything.

The Largesse's disturbing tendency could first appear where an institution measures satisfaction and quietly loses the capacity to record dissatisfaction. This is a story observation, not proof of a single cosmic intelligence.

## Player role

The protagonist can witness, help, connect evidence, and influence a few consequential projects. They should not single-handedly invent ethics for civilizations that have debated these questions for generations. Local people supply expertise and disagree with the player's interpretation.
