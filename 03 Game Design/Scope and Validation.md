## Three kinds of confidence

- **Creative coherence:** the idea fits the world, theme, and story. Reading and review can assess this now.
- **Experiential value:** the interaction is enjoyable, understandable, or frightening in the intended way. A future playable or performative test is needed.
- **Production feasibility:** the asset, system, and platform targets can be delivered at acceptable cost. Actual pipeline and device evidence is needed.

Do not promote one kind into another. A beautiful concept image does not prove a usable rig. A good desktop demo does not prove mobile performance.

## Representative studies and current limits

The current browser slice brief (working-archive reference) combines a household purpose, village route and detour, companionship, conditional discovery, useful field action, five-active tactical combat, a modest improvement, one inspectable discrepancy, return and save/resume. Pilot to Whole Game (working-archive reference) defines the bounded exit and later expansion. Review actual behavior before adapting earlier rules; neither an older prototype nor a written plan proves the combined experience. Platform targets include Steam, Google Play and Apple App Store; packaged builds and physical-device results must be reported separately from browser tests.

This does not reduce the intended game's world to those contents. Review visual fidelity separately from gameplay correctness; creating a workflow skill does not establish either in an engine.

## Main unresolved risks

| Question | Why it matters | Evidence needed later |
|---|---|---|
| Which camera and asset methods meet the visual goal? | Controls composition, animation work, atmosphere and readability | Same bounded scene with justified camera/method comparisons, motion/contact and feedback against the reference benchmark |
| Does the five-active turn-based proposal reward coordination? | Controls ability design, enemy behavior, UI, and pacing | Same encounter with understandable mistakes, recovery, and contrasting builds |
| Do rare encounters reward learning without waiting chores? | Controls exploration satisfaction and accessibility | Discovery through both chance and a dependable tracking route |
| Does growth preserve mastery and welcome new partners? | Controls level curves, talent value, and unwanted grinding | Equal-level build comparisons, modest level gaps, and new-recruit catch-up |
| Are field companions convenient and distinct? | Controls mount appeal and team freedom | Several routes and travel species without compulsory combat roster changes |
| How individual are companions? | Controls attachment and content cost | A few contrasting behaviors that players remember |
| How open is the narrative? | Controls clue order and consequence complexity | Walkthroughs through several valid discovery orders |
| How does an altered journal remain fair? | Controls trust and accessibility | Observation and recall study with stable recovery |
| How much geography can change? | Controls navigation and production | Small authored transformation with clear anchors |
| Can the repo workflow adapt? | Original runtime and assumptions may differ | New-game rig/atlas/environment proof in selected engine |

## Scope inventory

Each major concept can be tagged **core promise**, **strong candidate**, or **expansion possibility** after review. Do not silently discard costly concepts; preserve what makes them valuable, then compare versions that express the same intent.

[Feature Priorities](Feature%20Priorities.md) records the current sequence: personal story, combat, exploration, and progression. Cooking, crafting, and base building remain optional. A large proposed roster can grow in the wiki while each future production group still needs distinct ecological, narrative, travel, or combat value.

## Documentation checks now

Links resolve; names and dates are consistent; user decisions are distinguishable from invention; species have nonhuman purposes; factions have credible benefits and failures; quests handle re-entry and prior knowledge; mysteries have evidence; references have provenance; untested workflows are labeled; unanswered questions stay visible.
