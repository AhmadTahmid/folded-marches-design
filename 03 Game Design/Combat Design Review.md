# Combat design review

The user requested candid assessment of two combat conversations, then authorized the proposed follow-through: stronger creature identities, an encounter learning sequence, and a paper comparison of individual activations with shared actions. This authorizes development, not adoption of either conversation's rules. Strategic turn-based play and the five-active-companion ceiling remain selected. [Tactical Combat](Tactical%20Combat.md) remains the numerical baseline.

## Source provenance and judgment

Both shared conversations were read through their rendered browser pages on 2026-09-28: [DeepSeek](https://chat.deepseek.com/share/gleae99nj3l1rrrb2f) and [Gemini](https://share.gemini.google/2ur7PV1DA8c1). They discuss the appeal of turn-based combat, the possibilities of creature collectors, and making five combatants approachable. They are brainstorming sources, not verified documentation of the commercial games they name, scientific evidence of adrenaline, or instructions to implement their suggestions. This page paraphrases their proposals; there is no complete local source archive.

| Suggestion | Assessment for this game |
|---|---|
| DeepSeek: gradual team growth, visible intentions, forgiving early failure | Develop. Much already exists in [Engagement Reset](../04%20Production/Engagement%20Reset.md); the missing work is convincing situations, not another general statement of the principle. Teach through demonstrated competence and opportunity, not fixed playtime. |
| DeepSeek: practical bestiary and NPC clues | Develop strongly. Observing a creature should help anticipate it. The same essential tell must remain available during combat; reading is a source of anticipation and pleasure, not an entry fee. |
| Gemini: partner interactions and small setup/payoff combinations | Develop selectively through creature identities and encounters. Show consequences before commitment; avoid introducing universal chemistry, a combo meter and extra-turn rewards together. |
| Gemini: shared action points across five creatures | Compare fairly. It may let the player concentrate effort where needed. It may also increase allocation choices and leave favorite companions uncommanded. Five present creatures do not, by themselves, establish satisfying five-creature play. |
| Gemini: two attackers with three passive supports | Do not adopt as the default. It risks reducing companions to equipment and hiding complexity in triggers. Shared actions can be considered without this separate proposal. |
| Fixed tank/healer/damage roles; auto-battle for routine encounters | Do not make these foundations. Roles should overlap, and ordinary battles should deserve participation. The game should not require one species or build boring encounters merely to automate them. |
| Cheap early defeat followed by progressively severe lost progress | Keep recovery legible, but reject severity as a substitute for depth. Use meaningful objectives and comeback opportunities; [Tension, Stakes and Recovery](Tension%20Stakes%20and%20Recovery.md) owns the consequence alternatives. |

## Criticism of our own draft

Four equipped techniques plus three common commands, Focus, cooldowns, rows, tags and brief status windows already create a demanding system. Introducing them gradually does not prove that the mature battle is comfortable. Four equipped techniques remains a draft quantity, not a requirement to fill every early menu.

Several benchmark creatures offer nearby variations of Barrier. That makes arithmetic comparison easy, but risks weakening their individual appeal. A useful refinement gives each creature an action a player can describe and remember, with a genuine cost or limitation. A flamboyant effect alone will not distinguish identical decisions.

Our current activation structure already emphasizes an interesting question: **who should act now, and what response are we preserving for later?** Develop that pleasure before attaching more economies. The shared-action alternative asks a different worthwhile question: **which companions need our limited commands this round?** The paper comparison exposes the difference; it does not settle player preference.

## Recommendation after this pass

Keep individual activations as the working baseline while reviewing the identity and encounter proposals. Retain shared actions as a concrete alternative, with no declared winner. The worked comparison uses fifteen friendly commands in each version: it isolates allocation, and cannot be cited as evidence of fewer clicks. A later three-action version would be a separate pacing and balance experiment.

Future human review should distinguish understanding, companion attachment, and desire for another encounter. Correct arithmetic and complete prose establish none of those experiences. This paper pass produced no game code, assets or browser assignment. Public sharing was subsequently authorized; publishing these proposals does not adopt their alternatives.
