# The Folded Marches — game design v0.1

**A complete campaign direction to read and challenge, not a claim that the game is finished.** This edition makes working choices where earlier pages only compared possibilities. They are the author's recommended draft, not newly accepted user decisions. Existing selected constraints still win. The father's absence remains an actual unresolved author question; this edition does not turn it into a hidden plot.

## The game someone wants to play

You are eighteen or nineteen, good at difficult mountain work, and tired of being told you are dependable. Your mother and three younger siblings already rely on you. You love them. You also want clothes that were bought for you, a room in a strange city, extraordinary creatures at your side, and stories that happened to you instead of somebody passing through.

A paid road job gets you out of Rocca Selva. Beyond the mountain lie suspended rivers, forests with three inhabited roofs, a city cut through a gorge, migrations moving like weather and creatures whose absurd habits gradually become familiar. Travel makes you more capable and less certain about where to belong. Some people become friends. A skilled rival offers genuinely attractive work. Your companions surprise you without becoming unreliable tools.

Then a helpful sanctuary starts returning things that should have stayed lost. An animal. A street. A version of home that knows exactly why you miss it. Something has learned that people leave when they want, fear or lack something. It believes it can fix all three.

The late adventure is to get people and creatures out of that kindness—and keep enough of the world's actual miracles to live afterwards. It includes dangerous expeditions, tactical reversals, a pursuit, rescue, comedy and an ending with a future. The entire world is not exposed as fake. The early joys remain worth having.

## A representative session

Leave a friendly place with one attractive intention: see the white orchard, find an animal someone described, earn passage, or meet a friend. Notice a creature doing something specific. Try a small action. Choose a route that asks something of your team or your understanding. Meet resistance worth engaging with. Return with a relationship, technique, shortcut, discovery or consequence that changes the next outing.

One example: you plan to reach a high fair. A Kitefin steals the bright tie from your pack, leading to a nesting shelf. Returning its displaced roost marker opens an invitation; climbing another way still reaches the fair. A rival blocks the easier crossing with a team designed to punish careless front-row attacks. Beat the objective, bargain for a shared crossing or take the longer route. At the fair your companion imitates the wrong whistle, your friend gets a useful lead and you can buy an extravagant sash with money you probably meant to save. Later, the stolen tie becomes the detail that an impossibly attentive host remembers incorrectly.

This is an illustrative session, not an extra mandatory quest or a declaration that every keepsake will become sinister. At least some evenings, jokes, habitats and friendships remain entirely ordinary throughout the campaign.

## The distinct pleasures

- **Creature affection:** a memorable silhouette followed by a memorable behavior; a partner with a small preference you can recognize without a tooltip.
- **Travel:** a destination you want to reach, a physical route worth moving through, an optional temptation and a shorter return after you understand it.
- **Tactics:** see an approaching disaster, keep one option in reserve, and make the right partnership matter. Strong late play is coordinated, not rapid clicking.
- **People:** return expecting one familiar routine and discover somebody else's life has advanced. Not every conversation concerns the main plot or the protagonist.
- **Horror:** test a contradiction, choose to investigate and eventually confront a mind that has learned something about how you play. Its fictional attentiveness never requires personal-device access.
- **Release:** successful escapes and battles get space to breathe—food, a changed skyline, a ridiculous companion gesture, an honest conversation.

## Working choices that make the draft buildable

The campaign uses four movements and a connected open region, with the two cities visitable in either order. Late pressure follows committed story events, not hours spent exploring. The specific sanctuary Keeper and its observer channel have a concrete local mechanism in the campaign draft; the ultimate origin of the Great Opening remains beyond this campaign's required answer.

Free exploration is real-time. Creature combat is strategic and turn-based. Five companions are the eventual active ceiling, reached through staged learning; the opening begins with one. Clear recruitment conditions replace repeated capture rolls. Defeat preserves growth and can lead to recovery or a bounded rescue. Progressively discovered mapping supplies orientation without revealing the entire world. Borrowed tools, guides or ordinary routes prevent compulsory collection of one particular species.

For this edition, the protagonist has a fixed family situation, speaking voice and personal desires, with player-chosen name and preset appearance/clothing as the recommended presentation. This develops D-011's open comparison; it does not claim the user has selected it. Dialogue choices shape attitude, commitments and relationships rather than erase his established history.

The recommended ending tone is bittersweet and habitable. Some arrangements end; useful care, ordinary joy and the possibility of another journey survive. More enclosing and more destructive endings remain fully authored outcomes, not locked moral grades. D-010 remains open for the user's eventual selection.

## What v0.1 completion means

This edition must contain a legible central promise, a connected regional map, an independently designed home, a campaign through its endings, an executable-on-paper opening, a discoverable bestiary, recruitment examples, tactical rules, growth/recovery rules and a handoff route. No core plot beat should depend on an unnamed future answer to "what happens next?"

It is not the final dialogue for every side quest, a balanced combat database, a surveyed 3D terrain model, finished creature art or an implementation estimate. Those are later kinds of completion. The [edition checklist](../04%20Production/Wiki%20v0.1%20Checklist.md) makes the distinction visible rather than attaching a misleading percentage to the whole game.

The original visual ambition remains open and fantastical. Bellwater, Lanternfall and earlier studies supply possible production methods and evidence; their streets, species, quests, palette and limits do not dictate this game's design.
