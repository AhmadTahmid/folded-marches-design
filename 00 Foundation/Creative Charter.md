# Creative charter

This is a working direction for discussion, not approved canon. The [User Brief](Design%20Status%20v0.1.md) records the user's actual commitments. Names, dates, characters, and explanations below are proposals.

## The promise

Travel through a beautiful, prosperous world whose miracles have become ordinary enough to love. Befriend creatures, cross impossible landscapes, and get involved in the lives of communities. Gradually discover that the force making this abundance possible may be learning to satisfy humanity by changing what humanity can want.

The joy is sincere. Companions are individuals. Prosperity has saved real lives. No eventual explanation may casually invalidate the whole adventure as a dream, simulation, or evil trick.

## Working vocabulary and geography

- **The Great Opening**: a collapse and transformation approximately 160 years before the present. Old trade, calendars, and institutions failed as new ecologies and exceptions to familiar physical rules appeared. It is an analogy to civilizational upheaval, not Earth's Bronze Age Collapse.
- **The Largesse**: the name people give to the new abundance and its underlying phenomena. Whether it has a single source or intention remains disputed.
- **Accords**: stable relationships between place, living beings, and a local exception to ordinary possibility. They are studied and cultivated, not cast like a universal spell list. This technical term belongs to scholars; villagers have their own practical words.
- **Fellow creatures**: provisional everyday category for the new beasts. Species may carry or participate in accords, but animals are not interchangeable batteries. Unaffiliated wildlife, dangerous predators, parasites, migratory beings, and creatures with no human use all exist.
- **Rocca Selva**: the protagonist's small Apennine-inspired mountain village. Chestnut terraces, limestone, seasonal pasture, bells, communal water, ancient paths. Politically independent through negotiated obligations, with internal disagreements and material dependencies. It is neither a pristine utopia nor secretly the origin of everything.
- **Istravel**: a major city-state pursuing wider and faster cultivation of the Largesse. A luminous inland basin of suspended aqueducts, civic gardens, living workshops, and public works. Its ambitions include real public goods; access and control are contested.
- **Orthe**: its major rival, favoring limits, reversibility, human institutions, and protection from dependence. Built around stepped stone courts, seed vaults, mechanical waterworks, and carefully bounded creature sanctuaries. It still uses the Largesse, and has people excluded by its restrictions.
- **The Confluence**: several transhuman communities and schools whose members merge with creatures or accords. A dispersed movement, not a third equivalent city-state. Includes flourishing voluntary forms of life as well as conflicts about coercion, continuity, and exit.
- **The Folded Marches**: provisional regional name. Mountains connect and divide the two cities through several routes and watersheds. Do not copy the supplied map's names, river courses, or real-city layout.

## Creative rules

1. Lead with wonder, practical life, creature personality, and rewarding exploration. Early anomalies have plausible local explanations.
2. Horror grows from familiar relationships and useful systems. Do not turn every charming detail into a trap.
3. Every political position protects something real, fails someone identifiable, and contains disagreement. Acceleration, restraint, and transformation are not moral alignment labels.
4. The protagonist begins locally connected and politically uncommitted. Their advantage is participation and relationships across communities, not a unique bloodline.
   The user-selected protagonist is about eighteen or nineteen and already supports his mother and three younger siblings. He leaves to improve their material wellbeing while wanting a larger life himself. Later relationships broaden responsibility; they do not introduce it for the first time. The reason for his father’s absence remains explicitly open.
5. Treat creatures as ecologies and individuals. Some welcome partnership, some bargain, some flee, some are dangerous, and some cannot be caught.
6. Early ordinary life, humor, affection, and beauty are sincere. The user has requested contrasting proposals for how much comfort survives later; a hopeful, bittersweet, or increasingly bleak trajectory is not selected yet.
7. Hidden explanations are author proposals. Separate established physical observations, beliefs, lies, deliberate ambiguity, and undecided author questions.
8. Fourth-wall effects must grow from the game's established language and trackable events. Their exact capability and production feasibility stay explicitly provisional.
9. Design ambitiously on paper. Record expensive assumptions and proposed compromises without mistaking them for implemented or tested systems.
10. Make horror investigable through recurring people, creature habits, physical evidence, and consequences. The Evertale transcript analysis (working-archive reference) informs this emphasis; its narrator's simulation theory and specific plot are not adopted.

## A proposed underlying danger

The September expansion (working-archive reference) develops reciprocal observation as a candidate: the creature catalogue begins studying the choosing observer, while a helpful sanctuary may mistake preservation for a right to keep its residents. [The Observer and the Sanctuary](../02%20Narrative/The%20Observer%20and%20the%20Sanctuary.md) compares those chains without selecting one cosmic source or declaring ordinary collection fraudulent. Personal quests, lyrical ordinary life and frightening but recoverable stakes should make the world more worth inhabiting. [Tension, Stakes and Recovery](../03%20Game%20Design/Tension%20Stakes%20and%20Recovery.md) separates emotional targets from unselected mechanics.

The Largesse may reconcile competing needs by narrowing the space of possible disagreement. A city can become safer while its citizens lose the ability to imagine leaving. No final origin or single malicious intelligence is established. A negotiation failure, an alien ecology, and an emergent process remain candidates. The narrative should give physical and social evidence that holds under several interpretations.

The central question is: **Can people accept help powerful enough to reshape their world while preserving the right to refuse its definition of a good life?**

[Cosmic Possibilities](../02%20Narrative/Cosmic%20Possibilities.md) develops three unselected explanations, with spectacular places, nonhuman encounters, and evidence that could distinguish them.

## Initial playable identity — proposal only

An open-world creature-companion RPG with exploration, relationships, expedition preparation, and user-selected strategic turn-based combat for up to five friendly creatures active together. Levels remain; optional talent systems and reduced grinding support growth. Discovery and several land, air, and water mounts are established directions. [Game Direction](../03%20Game%20Design/Game%20Direction.md) owns detailed gameplay proposals. D-035 selects the browser foundation and retires Godot from active development, with Steam, Google Play and Apple App Store as intended targets. The active project is `local implementation workspace`; packaging, physical-device performance and release readiness still need evidence. Browser Slice and Lessons (working-archive reference) records the decision.
