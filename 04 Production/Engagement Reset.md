# Engagement reset

**Current scope, later on 2026-09-27:** D-054 authorizes one isolated 1v1 integration using the original combat engine and the returned standalone presentation. The user enjoyed the simple demo, called the battle “super” and better than before, and praised its animations (D-055). Existing monsters were reused, so this is no new creature-art judgment; UI polish is deferred. The wider opening below remains a design proposal alongside the comprehensive expansion (working-archive reference). Preserve the main game/default 5v3 encounter; see coordination scope (working-archive reference). The browser slice has supplied useful implementation evidence and direct feedback. It has not established an engaging opening or an accepted creature style.

The user rejected the 0.3 monsters' appearance, felt overwhelmed by the opening combatants, pressed buttons without reading, found the interface cluttered and the writing obscure, and questioned the visible “Narration” label. They still want strategic combat, distinctive creatures, stronger individual move animation and a game worth exploring. This page proposes a response; its sequence, dialogue and smaller encounter sizes are not adopted canon or implemented changes.

## What might be failing

| Observed feedback | Working hypothesis | Smallest useful check |
|---|---|---|
| Many unfamiliar combatants prompted indiscriminate clicking | Too many decisions arrive before the player cares about one partner or recognizes one enemy tell | One partner, one opponent, two meaningful choices |
| The UI feels cluttered | Several panels compete with the action and repeat information | Keep actor, target, consequence and commands visible; inspect the rest on demand |
| Dialogue feels obscure | Author explanations and thematic language are occupying moments that need a concrete want or action | Read the exchange aloud, then ask what the player now wants to do |
| The monsters are unappealing | The current treatment lacks the user's desired visual character; beginner status does not excuse dull design | Review a small candidate set together with scenery, expression and characteristic motion |

These are hypotheses, not evidence that technical combat, the species concepts or the entire premise must be discarded. Do not infer a precise palette fix from a broad visual rejection.

## A proposed first eight to ten minutes

Timing is a pacing target for a direct route, not a countdown. Optional conversations and exploration may extend it. Keep the scenic village reveal brief; historical cards remain skippable. No expanded cosmology lecture precedes the first action.

| Beat | Player action and curiosity | Immediate payoff |
|---|---|---|
| **0–1: Leave with a reason** | Pick up the packed bag after a short exchange with Ada. A shirt and lunch make the departure personal. Pell's paid survey is the clear destination. | Understand “earn better money, see more of the world.” Control arrives before a household exposition tour. |
| **1–3: Meet a creature doing something** | Find Clatter refusing a noisy, crowded plank. Make space or take the wider route. Its response follows the visible cause. | It crosses, then interrupts congratulations to scratch its neck. Offer the existing optional invitation; show following and personality immediately. |
| **3–5: Win one understandable exchange** | Accept a supervised **1v1** with one clear enemy wind-up. Strike makes progress; a horn-based interruption stops the announced heavy hit. A keeper can lend a partner if the player declined recruitment. | See the consequence of a deliberate choice and one move belonging to this creature. Brief recovery and a characteristic creature celebration; no permanent injury or compulsory victory gate. |
| **5–7: Follow something interesting** | A nearby Thimbleback covets a loose button. Follow, exchange or leave it. Its cache reveals a small useful find and an optional partnership. | Curiosity produces a funny behavior, a discovery and potentially a second partner—not another paragraph about exploration. |
| **7–10: Try a plan with two partners** | An optional **2v2** crossing exercise gives one partner a protective job and the other a retrieval or attack job. Explain the single objective before entry. Borrowing remains available. | Coordination achieves something ordinary attacks accomplish less efficiently. Finish with the survey wage or a clear next destination, plus a reaction from someone met earlier. |

This compresses material from [First Companion](../02%20Narrative/First%20Companion.md), [First Paid Journey](../02%20Narrative/First%20Paid%20Journey.md) and the [button collector](../03%20Game%20Design/Companionship%20and%20Catching.md#a-quick-playful-recruit--the-collector-of-bad-buttons). It is a reviewable alternative to the compressed full-team trial, not a silent rewrite of those quests. If the 2v2 crowds this opening, move it later rather than accelerate every preceding scene.

Later encounters grow through three, four and up to five active partners as the player acquires relationships and tactical needs. The existing five-creature trial remains useful as an optional advanced benchmark. **Five active is the maximum, not the onboarding requirement.**

One quiet discrepancy may follow an established ordinary observation. It should not compete with first controls, first companionship and first combat; later horror requires familiarity, not ominous dialogue from everyone.

## Reveal information when it becomes useful

- **First duel:** creature scene, two HP bars, active outline, one short enemy intention and clearly labelled commands. A deliberately limited starter/practice kit offers Strike and one signature interruption; explain any practice restriction and its cost. Do not silently hide a learned legal ability to force the lesson. A simpler Strike/Guard version is a fallback comparison if the interruption still demands too much explanation, not an additional mandatory tutorial.
- **Learning a technique:** introduce one distinctive move when it solves a visible problem. Its button leads with the result, such as “Stop its next action”; exact cost and eligibility remain visible before confirmation.
- **Two partners:** show whom the player commands and who has already acted. Preview legal targets directly on the scene. Introduce the one formation interaction the encounter uses; avoid an unrelated status lesson.
- **Growing teams:** keep the compact default. Inspection exposes all statuses, expiry, costs, targeting and calculations; the optional log explains prior events. Urgent enemy intent never disappears into a glossary.

Selection previews; confirmation commits. Back is always clear. Touch must have the same inspection route without requiring hover. Result animation then gives the player back control promptly. One move should visibly connect its anticipation, source, target reaction and outcome. A distinctive horn action and a distinct thrown-object action are a better next animation test than dressing every move in the same larger burst. More effects cannot establish engagement by themselves.

## Speech samples, not lore delivery

Each quoted line is its own short named-speaker beat. Stage directions below belong in the script and animation notes, **never a “Narration” character box**. These examples replace exposition at this moment, not the detailed worldbuilding kept in the wiki.

*Author cue: Ada puts lunch beside the better shirt in his bag.*

**Ada:** “Good shirt. Going surveying or getting married?”

**Protagonist:** “Depends what the survey pays.”

**Ada:** “Keep enough for a bed. Your sisters don't need you sleeping in a ditch.”

*Player takes the bag. Optional follow-up, rather than another compulsory speech.*

**Protagonist:** “I want to see what it's like down there.”

**Ada:** “Then bring back a story. Tobin will want the creature's feet drawn properly.”

*Author cue: Pell turns a road sheet toward him and points at the crossing.*

**Pell:** “Check the markers. Tell me if the crossing's safe. Bring this back and you get paid.”

**Protagonist:** “Even if it's closed?”

**Pell:** “Especially then. I'd rather hear it from you than lose a cart.”

*Author cue: Clatter plants its feet as the crowd claps. After the player clears space, it crosses and stops at the rough post.*

**Protagonist:** “Right. You needed quiet.”

*Clatter scratches its neck against the post.*

**Protagonist:** “And that.”

Clatter communicates through behavior; no invented translated speech is required. Necessary non-dialogue information uses a brief contextual caption or object inspection. Preserve skip/advance, named portraits and accessible descriptions of meaningful actions.

## The next review should answer

Watch an unfamiliar player without explaining the intended solution. Note where they hesitate, skip, inspect, experiment or simply repeat a button. Then ask:

1. Why is he leaving, and where were you trying to go?
2. What did your partner do that you remember?
3. What did the enemy intend, and why did you choose that response?
4. What made you take a detour—or made you unwilling to?
5. Was there a moment you wanted another battle or another discovery?

Success means observable comprehension, at least one deliberate tactical decision and some voluntary curiosity, followed by the player's account of what interested them. Record contrary evidence and revise the failed beat. A completed route, faster clicking or passing automated checks is insufficient. One small session diagnoses this opening; it does not prove broad audience engagement.

**Open for selection:** whether this ordering feels right; which appealing creature candidate leads it; how much optional practice belongs before the first paid journey. Combat's exact numbers, final art, names and fiction remain draft. No new feature, asset generation or implementation is performed by this plan.
