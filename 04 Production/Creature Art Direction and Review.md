## Working style bible

For each creature, state six things in ordinary language before producing art:

- **Silhouette:** one recognisable body shape and a secondary feature that survives a thumbnail.
- **Personality:** a visible attitude, not just a list of ecological uses.
- **Palette:** a dominant family, contrasting secondary area and restrained focal accent. Each species need not share the same brown coat.
- **Gesture:** one characteristic request, refusal or playful habit; readable without a label.
- **Emotional hook:** why someone would stop, approach or remember it before knowing its statistics.
- **Identity invariants:** limb count, proportions, material landmarks and markings that remain stable through views and actions.

Keep lighting, edge treatment, contact and detail density coherent with human sprites and scenery. Coherence does not mean identical saturation or palettes. Preserve broad forms before microtexture. Ordinary creatures may be funny, elegant, stubborn, startling or beautiful; rarity must not monopolise visual invention. Future horror can change a familiar relationship or gesture without making every initial design ominous.

## Three concepts, one controlled brief

Use **Bellwether/Clatter as a provisional comparison subject**, not a chosen compulsory starter. Hold its compact mountain-grazer body, four broad gripping feet, soft ruff, two hollow crescent horns and pale crescent under one eye. Its existing draft gesture is two shoulder presses and a backward step. First compare the same three-quarter standing view and comparable thumbnail scale. Describe the gesture in each concept's notes; a selected design then receives gesture frames and a scene-scale composition. The attached village view is context for that later fit check, not a request to repaint the world now.

| Candidate | Proposed emphasis | Question |
|---|---|---|
| A — confident little nuisance | Compact, expressive head/ruff relationship; emphatic broad feet; clear warm/cool colour blocking; a cheeky sideways stance | Do we want to approach it and learn its habits? |
| B — mountain wonder | More poised neck and horn rhythm within the same compact anatomy; luminous mineral accents and elegant large colour planes; quiet, self-possessed gaze | Can an accessible early companion feel beautiful and extraordinary? |
| C — proud but ridiculous | B's striking horn/ruff composition with A's stubborn stance; one strong contrasting accent; a dignified pose interrupted by its request for a scratch | Does combining appeal and presence create a stronger identity, or dilute both? |

These are alternatives to review, not three species, evolutions or locked colour assignments. No candidate may add anatomy simply to look more elaborate.

## External artist or Gemini handoff

Send the species excerpt, individual notes, this comparison brief, annotated reference PNGs, actual human/environment crop, rejected 0.3 example and required viewing size. Include stable ID and revision, source/provenance requirements, allowed changes and open questions. Do not rely on access to the whole chat or private repository.

Request the **three concepts first**. Review silhouette, immediate appeal, personality, scene fit and identity continuity together. Record specific keep/change notes. After a direction is selected, establish one master image and its invariant sheet; then commission controlled views, idle/travel and one signature ability sequence. Request editable layers where available, alpha assets, frame timing, anchors, source prompts and an honest list of missing outputs. An attractive action illustration is not a finished animation.

Validate animation and effects together at game scale: anticipation, source, travel/contact, target response and recovery must explain what happened. Reusable effects may share implementation while individual abilities retain distinct gestures, shapes and timing. Only expand to a small contrasting second anatomy after the first combined creature, human and environment review succeeds.
