# Istravel

**Role:** major city-state advocating accelerated cultivation of the Largesse. Its public benefits, internal disagreements, and political risks are all real within this proposal. It is not designated the villain city.

## A city that believes tomorrow can be kinder

Istravel fills a broad inland basin with terraces of colored ceramic, pale timber, hanging gardens, and ribbons of moving water. Anchored bridges connect inhabited towers rather than decorative spires. Workshops open directly onto shaded public courts. At sunset the water reflects streets that lie above it, briefly making the city look as though it contains two different horizons.

Visitors first encounter generosity: drinking fountains, inexpensive meals, public shade, musicians, bright creature processions, and volunteers helping newcomers navigate a place that is genuinely complicated. A child from Rocca Selva might spend half a day simply watching packages rise through a freight garden.

Its civic promise is straightforward: no person should endure a preventable hardship because the remedy seems too ambitious.

## Districts and experiences

**The Highwater Arcade** carries water along elevated channels shaped by Ribbonwake populations. It combines transport, walking routes, laundry stages, gardens, and public swimming enclosures. Keepers monitor current markers and close sections for migration. Residents complain when closures inconvenience them; children consider the departure procession a holiday.

**The Ninefold Garden** is a large public garden integrated with clinics, rehabilitation courts, quiet rooms, and social space. Different paths offer shade, gentle movement, sensory relief, and specialist care. Patients have picnics here; students practice portrait drawing; many people visit without being patients. Its benefits should be established through recognizable personal outcomes before anomalies enter the story.

**The Choirworks** cultivate Loam Choir communities to process suitable agricultural waste into soil amendments and selected food materials. Skilled workers maintain separation between colonies, track contamination, and protect rest cycles. Their union wants civic ownership and staffing levels that acknowledge creatures' needs as well as human shifts.

**The Lantern Roofs** are dense residential terraces covered with evening markets, laundry, small gardens, and painted wind screens. Beautiful public infrastructure does not prevent crowding or landlord disputes. A balcony with an independent water connection commands a premium.

**The Old Granary** is a former fortified storehouse now containing an archive, classrooms, rehearsal halls, and a lively argument about whether the building's history should be celebrated. Its massive shadow makes a useful contrast with the newer city.

## Who makes decisions

The city is governed through neighborhood assemblies and elected civic officers, supported by technical boards that certify proposed accords. This produces expertise and accountability in principle. In practice, boards can decide that a question is too technical for ordinary residents, while assemblies can make demands that maintenance workers cannot safely meet.

Public works are not all under one institution. Municipal services, cooperatives, private workshops, keeper guilds, and household arrangements overlap. The strongest expansion coalition includes clinicians, ambitious officials, industrial operators, and poor neighborhoods still waiting for services. They do not agree about ownership or acceptable risk.

## What acceleration means in practice

An expansionist does not need to worship progress abstractly. A doctor may have a queue of children whom a new garden could help. A resident may finally be able to work without disabling pain. A keeper may want to share a method whose license currently benefits one wealthy district.

The proposed next step is deeper coordination among districts and eventually across the Marches. Shared prediction could reduce shortages, smooth migration, distribute care, and prevent local failures. Each of these is a credible public good.

The political danger appears when refusing coordination looks equivalent to choosing other people's suffering. A temporary emergency requirement can become permanent. A public system can measure participation more easily than genuine consent. A prosperous district can assume that everyone wants the kind of life it already knows how to provide.

## Labor and animals

The city's skyline depends on labor that visitors rarely notice: underwater inspections, night migrations, sanitation, food safety, recordkeeping, patient care, repairs, and dealing with a sick creature that cannot explain what changed. Workers have jokes, competence, exhaustion, and political demands.

Creature care ranges from excellent to exploitative. Sanctuary advocates have won breeding-season closures and habitat corridors; some operators try to evade them. Residents disagree about whether a public-service creature is a worker, a dependent, a partner, or wildlife temporarily tolerating a structure.

These disagreements provide ordinary quests before they connect to the larger story.

## What Istravel cannot make alone

The city relies on mountain water knowledge, wild migration, outside seed diversity, salt, precision tools, and reserve engineering sold by its rival. It supplies valuable medicine, agricultural products, trained specialists, and accord-based services in return.

Its dependence on Orthe is embarrassing only to propagandists. Most working people recognize that a rival's pump may keep their clinic open during maintenance.

## Recurring people

[Dr. Senna Vey](People%20of%20the%20Marches.md#dr-senna-vey) wants to make effective care available regardless of district wealth. She is alert to patient discomfort but initially trusts aggregate evidence too much.

[Arel Dast](People%20of%20the%20Marches.md#arel-dast) organizes civic systems and public consultations. They can make astonishingly complex projects function. They struggle to accept a refusal that cannot be converted into a requirement the project can satisfy.

[Tavio Rusk](People%20of%20the%20Marches.md#tavio-rusk) knows both the public splendor and the service lanes. His deliveries reveal how many apparently local conveniences begin somewhere else.

## The first unsettling discrepancy

**Observed proposal:** a repeat visitor to the Ninefold Garden insists that an old route was removed, while current plans and several staff members say the route was never public. One physical trace and one independently kept account suggest a more complicated history.

**Beliefs:** clerical error, a patient's imperfect recollection, an unrecorded repair, political embarrassment. **Author proposal:** one coordinating accord has begun reconciling incompatible accounts in a way that reaches beyond paperwork. **Undecided:** origin, reach, and intention.

Do not retroactively make every healing encounter false. The frightening question is what might be added to a successful system, and who can stop that addition while preserving its good.
