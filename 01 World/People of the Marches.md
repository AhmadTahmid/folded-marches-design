# People of the Marches

Eight proposed recurring characters. Their names, pronouns, histories, appearances, and roles are working choices, not accepted canon. They are people with tasks and attachments beyond the protagonist. No romance availability, voice casting, companion-party system, death, betrayal, or mandatory ending is established here.

The protagonist's mother and three younger siblings are established direction; [Family at Home](../02%20Narrative/Family%20at%20Home.md) develops their four proposed character entries separately from the eight regional people below. Defined/customizable presentation remains open. Mara is a separate village contact in this draft; the reason for his father's absence remains unchosen.

Four additional people in [Traveling Cast](Traveling%20Cast.md) bring peer rivalry, freight work, performance, and commercial retrieval into recurring adventures. [Cast Arcs and Relationships](../02%20Narrative/Cast%20Arcs%20and%20Relationships.md) connects their later returns to these eight regional people and the family. This page remains the authority for the original eight biographies.

## Mara Vezzi

**She/her. Bellkeeper, pass-work coordinator, neighbor or other long-standing connection to the protagonist.**

Mara wears practical layered wool, a belt of differently shaped keys, and cuffs repaired in conspicuously mismatched thread. She has the posture of someone who has spent years leaning out of windows to call after people. Her pockets contain chalk, folded schedules, a sweet she forgot about, and a small ceramic bird with no obvious purpose.

She keeps the bells, teaches route signals, organizes emergency stores, and remembers who helped when the village needed a roof repaired. Her authority comes from competence and accumulated obligation rather than a formal title. She is funny about local vanity and considerably less funny when someone misses a work party.

**Want and contradiction.** Mara wants Rocca Selva to remain a place where no one has to plead with a distant office for basic care. Yet the system she defends depends on people—often the same few people—giving more time than they can comfortably spare. She can call this solidarity while overlooking exhaustion.

**Relationships.** She trusts Tavio's affection more than his timetable. Iona has helped repair mountain equipment and earns her respect by admitting what a mechanism cannot do. Pell once shared several difficult survey seasons with her. The protagonist has reason to care what she thinks, but need not always agree.

**Private fact, proposed.** Mara has quietly used lowland credit to maintain the pass house after a poor harvest. She fears disclosure will let city advocates portray the commons as a failure. The debt is finite and negotiable; it is not evidence that independence was a sham.

**Discovery and possible change.** Early interactions establish her generosity and controlling habits through ordinary work. Later the player can help her make obligations visible and share authority, or leave her more entrenched. Neither arc requires her death or betrayal. The ceramic bird may simply be a gift from someone she loves; every object need not be a clue.

**Voice cue.** “If the plan requires everyone to be generous forever, write down who counts as everyone.” She should occasionally have to hear her own advice returned to her.

## Tavio Rusk

**He/him. Traveling creature keeper, small merchant, and connector between communities.**

Tavio's wagon is a tidy argument with gravity: narrow shelves, rotating baskets, repair pieces tied where he can reach them, and painted route signs altered so often that the older lettering shows through. His clothing is cheerful, patched, and less expensive than his manner suggests. A familiar Doorling sometimes rearranges his stock labels; whether it is a permanent companion can remain open.

He carries medicine, correspondence, tools, seeds, small luxuries, and news whose reliability he rates with a theatrical change of expression. He knows that a village needs frivolous things as well as necessary ones. A festival ribbon can make a miserable winter feel survivable.

**Want and contradiction.** Tavio wants small settlements to stay on the map of things worth serving. He cross-subsidizes weak routes with profitable ones, extends informal credit, and overpromises. His network is generous partly because its accounting is fragile. Keeping every promise may require telling someone a truth that ruins their plans.

**Relationships.** Mara complains about him while keeping a place for his wagon. Senna relies on him for feedback from patients beyond the city. He buys Orthe tools from Iona's cooperative and transports equipment for Corda. He knows which political slogans disappear when an urgent delivery arrives.

**Private fact, proposed.** An Istravel distributor has offered to absorb his debts in exchange for exclusive carriage rights on several highland routes. The contract would improve reliability but narrow the communities' supplier choices. He has not signed it. He has already accepted a limited advance and is embarrassed about how this weakens his bargaining position.

**Discovery and possible change.** The player first encounters him as a source of invitations and small adventures. Bills, delayed stock, and conversations gradually reveal the strain. Outcomes might include a route cooperative, a constrained but transparent contract, or a reduced network whose limits he finally admits. No outcome should reward him for promising an impossible service indefinitely.

**Voice cue.** He answers “Is it good?” with “For whom, at what price, and will you still think so in the rain?” Then he tries to sell it anyway.

**Optional later story.** [A Room for the Keeper](../02%20Narrative/Quests/A%20Room%20for%20the%20Keeper.md) follows Tavio and the familiar Doorling through ordinary market visits and a rest service that begins restricting his departure. It develops his need for rest without making his generosity fraudulent, inventing a criminal past, or deciding whether the Doorling is his permanent companion.

## Dr. Senna Vey

**She/her. Physician associated with the Ninefold Garden and public access to care.**

Senna moves through the garden in a short work coat with replaceable sleeve covers, a waist pouch of instruments, and shoes chosen for long days rather than ceremony. She ties written reminders to a plant stand because an elegant filing system failed her once and she is still angry about it.

She treats recognizable problems: pain, limited mobility, difficult recovery, fatigue, infection, and distress. Her competence is demonstrated through attention, explanation, and follow-up. The garden helps; so do nursing, patient knowledge, ordinary medicine, and time. She should never be written as a mystical healer who merely waves at everyone.

**Want and contradiction.** Senna wants care to reach people who currently spend their lives waiting for it. She can show actual improvements from expanding the garden. That success makes her initially too willing to group complaints under “adjustment” when they do not fit existing measures. She listens to individual patients, but a system built from aggregate outcomes has trained her to discount what it cannot yet count.

**Relationships.** Arel makes projects possible and frustrates her by discussing people as demand curves. Tavio brings testimony from patients who cannot return regularly. Neris can obstruct a treatment Senna believes overdue, yet has also defended patients against institutional pressure. Corda challenges her assumptions about which changes count as a cure.

**Private fact, proposed.** Senna has begun maintaining a separate, explicitly consented case record for a small set of discrepancies at the garden. She does not yet know whether the cause is administrative, clinical, environmental, or stranger. This is inquiry, not proof that she always knew the secret.

**Discovery and possible change.** The player sees people genuinely helped before joining an investigation. Senna's strongest arc is learning to protect uncertain testimony without abandoning effective care. A darker possibility is that urgency makes her rationalize increasing intervention; it remains a branch, not a predetermined betrayal.

**Voice cue.** “Tell me what improved. Then tell me what it cost you. Those are separate questions.”

## Arel Dast

**They/them. Civic systems organizer and advocate for wider coordination.**

Arel carries rolled neighborhood plans held with bright cloth bands, wears a practical formal jacket whose pockets have been enlarged by a tailor, and recognizes maintenance staff by name. They enjoy public ceremonies more than they admit. A beautifully functioning fountain can make them briefly lose the thread of an argument.

They grew up in an intermittently served district where simple work took much longer than it did elsewhere. Their politics come from seeing infrastructure change ordinary life. They are skilled at bringing together engineers, keepers, residents, and funders who think they are discussing different projects.

**Want and contradiction.** Arel wants reliable regional provision: fewer failed shipments, better care, predictable migration windows, and less labor wasted on incompatible rules. Their weakness is a profound faith that every legitimate objection can eventually be translated into a design requirement. Someone who says “I understand, and I still refuse” leaves them searching for the missing variable.

**Relationships.** Senna supplies clinical reality and sometimes resents being used as the moral justification for expansion. Iona earns Arel's admiration through clear failure analysis. Neris is a formidable negotiating partner whom they privately find funny. Tavio's irregular network looks to them like both a lifeline and an avoidable inefficiency.

**Private fact, proposed.** Arel's own office has approved a pilot whose public feedback categories omit one form of refusal. They did not invent the omission to silence people; they initially considered it a duplication of another category. When challenged, the real character test is whether they will delay a project they believe can save lives.

**Discovery and possible change.** Early meetings show a person who can fix things. Later, an audit reveals that the records are cleaner than the consultation was. Arel can become a vital ally by accepting that a useful system must preserve decisions it cannot reconcile. Alternatively they may pursue ever more comprehensive consultation while missing the need for an enforceable boundary.

**Voice cue.** “If we can make the disagreement precise, we can work with it.” Another character should eventually ask who benefits when disagreement cannot be made precise.

## Magistrate Neris Vale

**She/her. Orthe magistrate responsible for appeals and civic continuity disputes.**

Neris wears dark blue fabric with broad unadorned sleeves and keeps a small folding stool for hearings held outside formal chambers. Her writing is severe; her doodles in the margins are unexpectedly good caricatures. She prefers a public argument with a record to a private agreement everyone remembers differently.

Her career began with forcing disclosure of reserve failures that earlier officials had hidden behind reassurance. People trust her because she has embarrassed powerful institutions, including her own. She knows how a right becomes useless when no one can afford to invoke it.

**Want and contradiction.** Neris wants decisions to remain reviewable under pressure. She also believes the city must keep people alive while an appeal proceeds. She therefore signs temporary continuity orders restricting changes to essential systems. Some applicants spend too much of their life waiting inside her definition of temporary.

**Relationships.** Iona supplies technical evidence Neris respects, along with public criticism she finds infuriating. Senna challenges her caution with patients who cannot wait. Corda presses for legal categories that preserve difference instead of forcing every person into a preexisting form. Arel is useful precisely because they understand implementation costs.

**Private fact, proposed.** Neris knows one reserve plan is technically compliant but fails to serve a particular district adequately. She has been seeking a repair through internal channels. Public disclosure could accelerate action or undermine trust during a crisis. She must eventually choose what residents have a right to know regardless of that calculation.

**Discovery and possible change.** The player first sees her uphold someone's unpopular appeal. Later, they encounter someone harmed by an order she signed. Her arc can preserve lawful accountability while admitting the harm and changing the procedure. She need not become an enemy of law to recognize that she has misused it.

**Voice cue.** “Write the exception down. Include the date when it ends. If you cannot name that date, stop calling it an exception.”

## Iona Serr

**She/her. Restoration mechanic, cooperative member, and Orthe dissenter.**

Iona wears a sleeveless outer work coat over rolled sleeves, carries gauges in a padded case, and paints repaired public mechanisms in bright colors so residents can see what changed. She dislikes decorative gears that do nothing. She will spend an afternoon making a child's absurd toy mechanism work because the absurdity delights her.

Her cooperative repairs pumps, lifts, shutters, and reserve systems. She teaches residents how to recognize faults before a breakdown. The classes are noisy, hands-on, and a little competitive. She regards practical confidence as a civic right.

**Want and contradiction.** Iona wants alternatives that function for actual people, including those who cannot climb stairs, carry water, or spend hours maintaining a household system. She defends Orthe's ideals while using carefully chosen accords when they improve access. Purists accuse her of inconsistency. She can answer that accusation so forcefully that she stops hearing legitimate concerns about a favorite solution.

**Relationships.** Mara appreciates her plain explanations. Tavio carries spare parts and occasionally tries to substitute something “nearly equivalent.” Arel sees her as a potential collaborator; Iona wants commitments about ownership before giving them a design. Neris needs her testimony and dislikes the public meetings where she delivers it without invitation.

**Private fact, proposed.** Iona has accepted support from a cross-city technical exchange that her cooperative has not fully discussed. The work is sound, but the funding could create obligations she has been too impatient to examine. Her blind spot mirrors the institutions she criticizes on a smaller scale.

**Discovery and possible change.** The player meets her during a repair that visibly improves daily life. Subsequent quests expose reserve gaps and the political choices behind them. She can help design a shared system with independent fallbacks, provided the story gives those fallbacks land, labor, and funding rather than declaring them free.

**Voice cue.** “Show me who uses it when it breaks. That is part of the design.”

## Corda-of-Reeds

**They/them. Confluence emissary, former river courier, and advocate for changed persons.**

Corda retains a recognizable mobile body, with translucent reedlike structures along their arms and neck and a resting posture adapted to how they now sense flow. Their clothing includes detachable moist layers and ordinary pockets full of correspondence. Travel away from their reed-bank accord requires equipment, pauses, and occasionally help; these are access needs, not signs of moral corruption.

Their perception extends through a limited area of water and rooted life at Reedhouse. The experience can be rich, distracting, intimate, or boring. They still dislike waiting for officials. They remember delivery routes by meals and people rather than coordinates.

In this draft, Corda sometimes describes their experience as singular and sometimes as plural. Different threads of preference can become easier to express under different sharing conditions. [How Many Hands Are Mine](../02%20Narrative/Quests/How%20Many%20Hands%20Are%20Mine.md) develops one specific conflict about that expression. This does not establish a universal account of all merged people or make internal disagreement itself a symptom of danger.

**Want and contradiction.** Corda wants changed people to have housing, work, ordinary citizenship, and relationships without having to prove that their lives are cautionary tales or miracles. They can become defensive when a member of the Confluence describes a painful experience, fearing that opponents will exploit it. Protecting a movement can make them slow to protect a particular person.

**Relationships.** Tavio understands the material complications of travel and teases them without reducing them to their body. Senna is a useful ally when she asks what care should achieve instead of assuming. Neris offers genuine legal tools within frustrating categories. Pell records Corda's testimony in forms that preserve pauses and uncertainty rather than smoothing them away.

**Private fact, proposed.** Corda recognizes a memory of a riverside room but does not recognize the affection attached to it. Several explanations remain open: sensory association through the accord, a forgotten human visit, another participant's contribution, or a wider disturbance. They do not secretly know the final answer.

**Discovery and possible change.** Initial scenes establish humor, skill, friendship, and good reasons for their chosen life. A later continuity dispute asks them to defend someone's refusal even if it challenges the community. Their strongest arc separates defending the right to change from insisting that every change was good.

**Voice cue.** “I am asking for a room I can sleep in. We can discuss the future of humanity after that.”

## Pell Arven

**They/them. Independent route surveyor and disputed historical witness.**

Pell carries a weathered plane table, measuring cord, spare gloves, and several notebooks made by different binders. Their outer clothing is plain and meticulously repaired. They draw tiny jokes in otherwise precise maps: an exceptionally rude goat, a tea cup beside a difficult climb, a duck that only appears after a sheet is folded correctly.

They map usable routes rather than claiming a godlike view of the world. A reliable record includes weather, uncertainty, who provided a local name, and where observation stopped. They are willing to be corrected, which gives their later distress more weight than a reputation for infallibility would.

**Want and contradiction.** Pell wants a shared record that people can test and dispute. They are increasingly afraid that admitting uncertainty will let institutions dismiss a real anomaly. This can tempt them to sound more certain than their evidence warrants—the opposite of the practice they teach.

**Relationships.** Mara has trusted them on difficult passes and knows some of their mistakes. Corda appreciates their careful handling of testimony. Arel wants usable route data; Pell insists on preserving contradictory observations. Tavio has carried copies of their logs to different places, creating mundane independent records.

**The disputed season.** Pell remembers a season of ordinary work, but several records place them on mutually incompatible itineraries. “The missing season” is other people's shorthand; Pell is not simply an amnesiac who can deliver whatever plot information becomes convenient. Some evidence may result from duplication, mistaken dates, or fraud. At least one carefully developed discrepancy should eventually resist those explanations if this hook is retained.

**Discovery and possible change.** Early scenes demonstrate competent fieldwork and humor. Later the player compares physical markers, independently held copies, and witnesses. Pell can learn to make uncertainty public without surrendering the claim that something is wrong. They should not become the sole source through which every revelation arrives.

**Voice cue.** “This line means I measured it. This one means someone told me. Leave them different.”

## Relationship pressures that can generate scenes

- **Mara and Tavio:** she asks him to promise supplies he cannot guarantee; he asks her to disclose the village's actual reserves.
- **Senna and Neris:** a useful treatment is delayed while its safeguards are debated. Both must speak with the affected person, not only about them.
- **Arel and Iona:** they can build something excellent together if they agree who owns it, who repairs it, and how a district can leave it.
- **Corda and Senna:** care may mean supporting a chosen difference rather than restoring a previous form.
- **Pell and Mara:** mutual trust makes a disputed recollection painful, but neither must accuse the other of madness or betrayal.
- **Tavio and Corda:** a practical delivery problem reveals how infrastructure assumes a standard body and standard traveler.
- **Neris and Pell:** public testimony needs usable claims; preserving uncertainty can make it harder to obtain a remedy.

These relationships should develop through meals, work, favors, disappointment, humor, and ordinary disagreement before becoming a panel of people debating the central theme.

## Author guardrails and next decisions

Not every recurring character is hiding a catastrophic secret. The private facts above are vulnerabilities and pressures from which scenes can grow; they are not a mandatory chain of betrayals. At least some disagreements can be resolved without a tragic price.

The user has requested both protagonist approaches and contrasting tonal futures before choosing. Do not lock Mara into a parent role, require a romance, kill a cast member to establish stakes, or decide that all successful relationships must dissolve. Such commitments belong in the discussion process.
