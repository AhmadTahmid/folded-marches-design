# Atlas of the Folded Marches

This is a proposed network of places and journeys, not a literal adaptation of the supplied reference map. Relative connections matter more than final compass directions, distances, or terrain scale. The fixed creative anchors are a mountain home and two ideologically opposed major cities.

**Current design v0.1:** [Regional Map v0.1](Regional%20Map%20v0.1.md) develops this atlas into ten connected zones, explicit return routes, watershed/climate relationships and optional frontier placements. Its topology is the active working draft for campaign coordination; this page retains the broader place descriptions. Neither map adopts the Bellwater prototype's geometry or establishes implemented scale.

## Regional shape

Rocca Selva occupies a limestone shoulder above two watersheds. One descends toward the open basin of Istravel, the other toward the stone gorges containing Orthe. The rivers meet far below in a braided wetland before entering a salt basin. Consequently, a traveler can cross the mountains, follow either watershed, or make a longer lowland circuit through the wetlands.

This produces several ways between important places. Neither city serves as the sole gate to the rest of the region. High routes expose travelers to weather and ecology; low routes expose them to tolls, civic rules, markets, and people who need things carried.

## Four major journeys

| Route | Everyday purpose | What makes travel memorable | Pressure or complication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bell Road** — Rocca Selva to Istravel | Medicine, orchard trade, students, keeper apprentices | White ridges, wind gardens, Kitefin flocks, the first view of elevated water | Seasonal animal movements and city-sponsored improvements that could turn a common route into a regulated corridor |
| **Old Switchback** — Rocca Selva to Orthe | Metal tools, wool, seed exchange, stonework | Mossy retaining walls, stair markets, refuge cisterns, Bellwether paths | Rockfall and maintenance debts; debate over who receives a safe travel permit during emergencies |
| **Braided Road** — lowland circuit between the cities | Heavy freight, salt, ceramics, food, mixed caravans | Ferries, mobile river gardens, red reeds, night markets reflected overhead in water | Upstream decisions alter downstream habitats; toll collection crosses several overlapping jurisdictions |
| **Skywash Traverse** — high-country bypass | Herding, research, sanctuary access, occasional unofficial trade | Grass islands in cloud, ice-colored pools, distant Knuckle Saints | Weather, few reliable services, nesting grounds, and trails that shift slowly over seasons |

Walking journeys should feel substantial, but no travel time or world-map kilometer count is fixed. Those choices depend on the eventual exploration model and platform constraints.

## Home and civic centers

### Rocca Selva

A compact mountain settlement of chestnut terraces, rough limestone, shallow roofs, and communal water. It is small enough for social consequences to persist and connected enough for several futures to be plausible. It provides an emotional reference point, useful people, changing local needs, and a working example of negotiated independence. [Village page](Rocca%20Selva.md).

### Istravel

An inland city whose public water, gardens, workshops, and residential terraces are interwoven with large accords. Its skyline includes anchored bridges and suspended channels that look delicate but are maintained with very unromantic schedules. A first visit should feel liberating: more color, health, music, food, and possibility than the protagonist knew a city could contain. [City page](Istravel.md).

### Orthe

A city built through a stone gorge, with tall counterweights, broad stair courts, reservoirs, open workshops, bounded sanctuaries, and enormous seed stores. Its beauty comes from visible effort: stone joints, moving water, polished handrails, and people demonstrating a repair in public. Its civic festivals are loud, generous, and competitive. Restraint does not mean austerity in every part of life. [City page](Orthe.md).

### Reedhouse

A Confluence settlement in the braided wetlands. Some buildings float; others remain on piles above the water. Walkways include dry, shallow-water, and submerged approaches for different bodies. The market exchanges river plants, instruments, correspondence, and testimony from travelers whose senses no longer fit a single viewpoint. [Confluence page](The%20Confluence.md).

## Optional places worth finding

### The White Orchard

A broad hollow where Glassgrazers deposit pale branching mineral growths around old root systems. From a distance it resembles a winter orchard in midsummer. Close up, the branches carry tiny pools, insects, and mineral-eating lichens. Children collect fallen pieces; keepers discourage breaking living formations.

Exploration promise: bright paths, herd behavior, careful climbing, mineral craft, and a trade dispute over extraction. The place needs no horror reveal to be valuable. Its runoff connects directly to downstream water quality.

### The Staired Wood

An old managed chestnut forest now interspersed with new organisms. Branches grow along the horizontal lines of abandoned terraces, making a canopy that can be crossed in rising stages. Thimblebacks cache seeds in wall cracks. Vergecats hunt along interfaces between crowded and empty space.

Exploration promise: layered routes, forest work, missing livestock, a cheerful gathering camp, and the choice to observe a predator before trying to approach it. Not every dangerous place is malicious.

### Low Lantern Fen

A wetland where Morrow Moths roost on reed heads and reflect dusk into the water. Local boaters navigate by the moths because they shelter on the lee side before a wind change. The luminous ribbons are living populations, not streetlights owned by a city.

Exploration promise: a night festival, small boats, pollination observation, and conflict over a proposed permanent lighting installation. The spectacle should be allowed to remain beautiful.

### The Unfinished Causeway

A monumental pre-Opening road ends midway across a shallow basin. Successive builders completed extensions that now stand slightly out of alignment with one another. Some failures are obvious survey errors. Others are harder to account for.

Exploration promise: picnic shelters, ruined toll offices, survey puzzles, and evidence that can support several historical explanations. The site becomes stranger through comparison across visits, not an immediate haunted-dungeon announcement.

### The Cloud Loom

A wind-carved high saddle where airborne pollen and Kitefin breeding flights produce long colored veils. Shepherds mark the safest approach with loose cloth that can be heard in poor visibility. Temporary keeper camps supply warm food, weather advice, and arguments about who may collect eggs.

Exploration promise: dramatic horizons, migration, climbing, weather preparation, and the possibility of earning a wild individual's trust over several encounters. No compulsory capture collection should exhaust the breeding grounds.

### The Cairn of Many Hands

A Confluence community built into a disused mountain quarry. Its stone ramps and thick communal walls accommodate members whose bodies have become too broad, slow, or temperature-sensitive for ordinary city housing. Residents shape stone through long shared contact, producing beautiful public rooms over years.

Exploration promise: hospitality, unusual sculpture, bodily difference treated as ordinary design, and a serious disagreement over the responsibilities of long-lived residents to younger ones. See [The Confluence](The%20Confluence.md).

### The Quiet Salt

A shallow terminal basin whose edges alternate between working salt pans and closed wildlife grounds. The mineral flats preserve footprints unusually well. Some migrations can be read as overlapping routes from several seasons.

Exploration promise: vast light, salt workers, long-distance views, Knuckle Saint passage, and an archive of movement. A late question can emerge when one route seems to have been used before its bridge was built; it should have an ordinary geological or recordkeeping interpretation until further evidence exists.

## Material geography

Istravel buys mountain survey knowledge, certain seeds, salt, precision metalwork, and access to high nesting grounds. Orthe buys medicines, warm-season produce, and specialist accord care, while selling tools, reserve engineering, and durable storage. Rocca Selva exports chestnuts, wool, trained route labor, and modest specialized ecological knowledge; it imports salt, iron, medicines, paper, and some winter grain. Reedhouse connects transport, wetland care, and translation between incompatible bodily needs.

No community has every essential resource. These relationships explain why rivalry produces tariffs, treaties, bargaining, and proxy disputes more often than an uncomplicated permanent war.

## Design boundaries

[Atmospheric Frontiers](Atmospheric%20Frontiers.md) develops mist terraces, a three-layer forest, a snow/thaw route and an old trading quay. [Regional Map v0.1](Regional%20Map%20v0.1.md) now gives these **working draft placements**: lower western terraces, high Skywash, a bypassed freshwater branch, and a separate warm/wet downstream tributary valley. These are reviewable geographic choices, not adopted canon; the Staired Wood remains managed chestnut forest. [Travel, Mapping and Pursuit](../03%20Game%20Design/Travel%20Mapping%20and%20Pursuit.md) develops progressively recorded player maps separately from the author's full geographic knowledge.

This atlas proposes locations and route relationships, not a promise of fully seamless traversal, rideable species, flight, swimming, or simulation at world scale. Every optional place can support a contained encounter space if required later. Political and ecological connections should survive production compromises even if literal map dimensions change.

Keep plot unlock conditions in the narrative and game-design pages. Discovering a beautiful landscape should not always require accepting a faction's ideology.
