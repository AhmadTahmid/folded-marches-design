# World overview

All setting material on this page is proposed. The user's fixed intentions live in the [User Brief](../00%20Foundation/Design%20Status%20v0.1.md); names, histories, institutions, and explanations here can change together.

## The world you first fall in love with

At dawn a girl on a municipal balcony shakes crumbs from a tablecloth. A school of little rays peels away from the washing lines and sails into the pink air. Beyond them, an aqueduct bends uphill around a garden hospital. Its water is carried by a procession of river creatures, each resting in the next creature's current. Under the balcony a baker argues with a heat-fed mole that prefers sleeping in the expensive oven.

Far above this city, the bells of Rocca Selva call people to open the summer paths. The bells also call a herd of antlered animals, which may or may not attend. The protagonist knows which shop puts honey in its chestnut bread, which neighbor needs help on steep steps, and which pass should never be crossed with a frightened pack animal. They have never seen an aqueduct travel uphill.

This is a world worth leaving home to explore, and a home worth returning to. Its people have accomplished extraordinary things. The eventual danger matters because the things it threatens are already good.

## The Folded Marches

The proposed starting region is a mountain hinge between two watersheds and two powerful cities. It contains limestone ridges, orchard terraces, copper-red wetlands, warm inland gardens, foggy salt basins, and land altered by the [Great Opening](History%20and%20the%20Opening.md). Several valleys have greater interiors than their entrances suggest. The name *Folded Marches* began as a surveyor's joke and is now printed on expensive stationery.

The wider world exists beyond the regional map. Merchants bring unfamiliar languages, metalwork, and conflicting accounts of the Opening. This first wiki does not establish a world-spanning empire, a complete globe, or the claim that these two cities represent every possible politics.

See the [Atlas](Atlas.md) for actual connections and proposed exploration regions.

## What changed

About 160 years ago, the old lowland order disintegrated during a period now called the Great Opening. New creatures, ecologies, and local exceptions to familiar physical rules appeared. Food could grow through a winter. Certain waters ceased carrying disease. A creature could draw yesterday's heat from stone. Some roads acquired destinations their builders had never intended.

The first decades were destructive. Harvest schedules failed; capitals lost their roads; obligations written against the old seasons became impossible to pay. The prosperity of the present was built afterward through observation, public works, creature keeping, treaties, and immense amounts of repair. It was not bestowed fully organized.

People call the resulting abundance **the Largesse**. Scholars call a repeatable relationship among place, life, and an unusual effect an **accord**. A rural keeper is more likely to say that a spring “likes shade and a crowded bank.” Neither vocabulary is automatically wiser. [Largesse and Accords](Largesse%20and%20Accords.md) records the working rules and uncertainties.

## The prosperity is real

- A winter child is no longer routinely a hungry child in either major city. Public broth halls and creature-assisted granaries have reduced seasonal insecurity dramatically, though remote settlements still face access problems.
- Certain infections and crop blights have become treatable. People who would previously have died young can now expect decades of ordinary, complicated life. The scale of improvement is a setting proposal, not a measured demographic statistic.
- Domestic tasks take less punishing labor. Shared heat houses, water lifts, and public laundry courtyards free time for education, craft, and leisure.
- Markets sell small luxuries to ordinary workers: glowing paper fish, chilled fruit in summer, boots grown to a foot's shape, miniature gardens that flower on train-like caravan schedules.
- Children learn several practical relationships with creatures before they learn civic history. Keeping a companion is neither universally affordable nor an exotic privilege.
- Prosperity creates new kinds of work: migration steward, accord translator, municipal keeper, sanctuary advocate, route surveyor, household calibrator, and someone who mediates complaints about an animal that has adopted a street.

Abundance has not abolished scarcity. Land in a stable accord district, reliable medicine outside the cities, specialist attention, safe passages, and the right to redirect public infrastructure remain unequally distributed. A plentiful loaf can still sit behind a locked door.

## Three arguments about living well

**[Istravel](Istravel.md)** wants to extend successful accords until preventable suffering becomes politically indefensible. Its best citizens ask why anyone should die while a remedy exists. Its danger is allowing the people who operate the remedy to decide what counts as a problem, a patient, or an acceptable refusal.

**[Orthe](Orthe.md)** insists that a society must remain able to sustain itself when a miracle is withdrawn. Its best citizens defend exit, memory, and institutions that survive their rulers. Its danger is making caution a privilege: the people already safe can demand that everyone else wait.

**[The Confluence](The%20Confluence.md)** contains communities seeking changed forms of personhood through merging with creatures and accords. Some have found richer lives. Others encounter troubling questions about whether a person who has changed can still revoke an earlier agreement. They disagree with one another as fiercely as either city disagrees with itself.

**[Rocca Selva](Rocca%20Selva.md)** does not possess the correct answer in miniature. Its commons preserve useful independence, but its residents rely on lowland medicine, metal, credit, and buyers. Some want more connection; others fear that a contract can become a leash. The protagonist begins inside these relationships, without inheriting a party line.

## Creatures belong to themselves

The new beasts are commonly called *fellow creatures*, though no universally accepted biological category unites them. Some accompany humans. Some congregate around human infrastructure without being tame. Some have never tolerated a settlement. Some are ordinary predators in extraordinary bodies.

The [Bestiary](Bestiary.md) includes animals that are funny, inconvenient, affectionate, dangerous, unhelpful, and impossible to catch. A species description is not an individual personality. A farm-raised Glassgrazer and a wild Glassgrazer share ecological needs; they do not share an opinion about strangers.

Existing plants and animals survived the Opening unevenly. Chestnut trees, sheep, foxes, insects, and familiar birds remain part of daily life. The new ecology should look interwoven with a recognizable landscape, rather than having replaced every organism with a mascot.

## Faith, custom, and private meaning

There is no single Church of the Largesse. Old rites continue, acquire new interpretations, or split into local practices. Some people thank the phenomena as a gift; others direct gratitude toward keepers and ancestors who made survival possible. Mountain households leave a chair for a late traveler. In Istravel, people place the first blossom from a balcony garden in the public water. In Orthe, a repaired tool is traditionally returned with its broken part attached until the owner chooses to discard it.

These customs provide warmth and disagreement before they supply any clues. None is automatically evidence of an ancient conspiracy.

## The concealed pressure

**Author proposal:** widely connected accords may begin resolving human conflict by reducing the range of desires and interpretations that reach their shared process. The symptoms could resemble successful administration for a long time: fewer complaints, easier coordination, people who report being content.

This is a proposed threat mechanism, not the established origin of the world. It does not prove that the Largesse is a person, that all creatures are its agents, or that every use carries a secret price. The source may be alien, ecological, emergent, or something for which these categories are insufficient. The [narrative wiki](../02%20Narrative/Story%20Architecture.md) owns revelation order and alternative tonal futures.

The first anomalies are small enough to be lived around. A garden attendant recalls a familiar visitor choosing a different bench. A record contains two equally plausible names. A companion refuses a passage it used happily last week. Early incidents must retain ordinary explanations until evidence earns a stronger reading.

## What remains open

- How much ordinary comfort survives the deepest revelations; the user requested contrasting possibilities before choosing.
- Whether the protagonist is a defined person or customizable; neither approach is canon yet.
- Whether *the Largesse* denotes one underlying process or a human category imposed on many unrelated events.
- How far the contemporary setting extends beyond the Marches.
- Whether some phenomena predate the Opening but were misunderstood.
