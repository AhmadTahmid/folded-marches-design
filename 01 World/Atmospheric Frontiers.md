# Atmospheric frontiers

Four location briefs develop moods the user requested: Apennine mist, dense layered forest, snow/ice travel and a worn settlement with ennui and the ache of passing time. These are original candidates, not selected regions, fixed biomes or reproduced reference maps. [Atlas](Atlas.md) continues to own the draft geography. The names below are working labels; no new world-scale or asset-production guarantee follows from this page.

**Design v0.1 placement pass:** [Regional Map v0.1](Regional%20Map%20v0.1.md) supplies active working locations and connections: mist terraces beside the lower Old Switchback, Thaw Stair on Skywash, old quay on a freshwater Braided Road branch, and Three-Roof Forest in a warm/wet tributary valley reached from Reedhouse. This develops the alternatives below without approving their anomalies, species or quests. The tropical canopy proposal remains geographically separate from Rocca's chestnuts.

## The mist terraces

**Possible placement:** a side valley on the lower Old Switchback, where maintained chestnut terraces and abandoned service steps overlap. Apennine fog is the user's landscape prompt. The Silent Hill/GTA San Andreas references are atmosphere suggestions, not a copied weather system or audited scene.

**Ordinary identity:** wet limestone smells, roof-water dripping into a courtyard, chestnut trunks entering and leaving view, mule bells briefly clarifying a route. A baker opens the same shutter each morning. Children know which stair has a broken handrail. Residents do not call all fog a supernatural event.

**Travel:** the high wall, flowing water and three distinct bell rhythms give stable orientation. The player learns one short loop in clear weather before the first dense fog crossing. A route note retains discovered turns; captions and visible markers substitute for audio. A longer staffed path is slower but safer, without an arbitrary toll that makes accessibility an inferior choice.

**Creatures:** existing forest animals forage around walls and courtyards. A predator can exploit poor sightlines for ecological reasons. A familiar companion's hesitation distinguishes a crowded turn from a quiet one, but is not a universal detector of ghosts or lies.

**Ordinary adventure:** retrieve a windblown performance banner or find the animals that moved a delivery basket. The detour reveals a working rain shelter, a funny local disagreement and a useful return path. One mist visit should contain no horror at all.

**Possible anomaly:** in one bounded service lane, calls arrive before the caller makes them, or three independently marked exits lead to the same courtyard. Use one mechanism per case. Old charcoal marks, an outside witness and route measurements allow comparison. Fog obscures the test; it is not proof of supernatural intent.

**Failure and re-entry:** a wrong turn returns to a known landing or exposes an alternate clue. Dangerous penetration requires visible signs and a chance to retreat. The weather does not erase the player's discovered route history. A later investigation can restore an independent exit while leaving ordinary fog beautiful.

**Author layers:** the drafted terraces and their ordinary weather are candidate facts. Residents' “white caller” story is folklore. Repeatable echoes or route returns are proposed player evidence. The source, intention and even use of the anomaly remain open.

## The three roofs of the forest

**Possible placement:** an exceptional warm basin beyond the established mountain routes, or a separately reached later woodland. Do not turn the managed chestnut Staired Wood into a Congo rainforest without climatic and geographic revision. “Triple canopy” here is an experiential proposal for three navigable layers, not a researched claim about a uniform real biome.

**Ground layer:** wet roots, dark pools, fungi, shed mineral plates and shafts of light. Small clearings contain routes maintained by residents and animals. The player notices footprints and dragged vegetation before any predator appears. Dense planting must leave legible silhouettes and movement paths.

**Middle layer:** broad branches, suspended leaf pockets, rope repairs and older platforms from a small keeper station. This offers the best route knowledge and one inhabited rest. The station is not empty merely to maintain a scary mood. Someone complains that every visiting surveyor draws the wrong branch.

**Crown layer:** brilliant sun, flowering masses, airborne creatures, distant water and wind exposure. The forest's impressive depth should reward ascent with a new interpretation of the path below. Canopy travel requires supports, guides or appropriate partners; collecting one flying species is not the only way to reach a key site.

**Expedition:** recover an observation beacon stranded between layers. Ground access is direct but noisy, middle access requires repairing one safe line, and crown access requires favorable wind and a descent. Tools can be borrowed. Each path shows different evidence of a changed feeding corridor, so replay or revisit is about discovery rather than duplicate combat.

**Predation:** a territorial apex animal claims one interface between layers. It does not instantly pursue every exploration detour. Fresh scoring on supports, sudden absence of prey and a known boundary warn the player. Detouring, observing, using cover or a purposeful defensive battle are valid approaches. Its hunger does not make all forest life a coordinated evil.

**Optional eldritch extension:** one vertical shaft has more internal depth than its outer crown suggests. A marked dropped line and measured descent provide evidence. Preserve an independent route and a bounded escape rule; the entire forest is not suddenly unchartable. [Travel, Mapping and Pursuit](../03%20Game%20Design/Travel%20Mapping%20and%20Pursuit.md) owns the chase candidate rather than selecting a species here.

**Reward:** a contrasting habitat, a conditional creature invitation, new route information and a return conversation. The beacon's recording can foreshadow a later observer without revealing its full origin. A bright crown rest follows the hardest crossing.

## The thaw stair

**Possible placement:** a seasonal branch of the Skywash Traverse. Permanent ice versus seasonal snow remains undecided. This does not make the entire high route accessible from the first region.

**Ordinary identity:** snow packed against dark stone, wind polishing exposed ridges, cloth markers becoming stiff with frost, meltwater under a bridge. A sheltered keeper hut offers drying space, hot food and an argument about which boots are useful. Warmth can be a sincere reward.

**Travel:** compare a short exposed shelf, a longer marked stair and a guided intermediate traverse. Movement speed reflects surface and shelter, but ice does not constantly steal control or require prolonged stamina recovery. A traction tool, path preparation, guide or compatible mount offers different answers. Freezing is not an invisible timer while the player reads the journal.

**Expedition:** carry a repaired signal to a shelter before an announced weather change. Advance the change through stated journey stages, not real-world minutes or time spent paused. Missing the best window changes the route and lodging rather than deleting a household. Optional prints in thawing snow reveal a creature opportunity, not a compulsory storm capture.

**Creature life:** identify a species that already fits or write a new ecology brief before assigning thermal immunity and riding. A beautiful ice creature need not be an elemental weapon with fur. Any new anatomy remains outside the eighteen-species bestiary until separately developed and reviewed.

**Possible horror:** prints begin inside an occupied room and lead outward although nobody saw entry. A wax seal, an independent witness and the sequence of fresh snow test ordinary access explanations. Do not infer a ghost solely from unusual tracks. The reveal can be an animal, a human act or a spatial phenomenon depending on the chosen quest.

**Aftermath:** socks drying, a companion choosing the warm wall and music barely audible from the hut create relief. Snow should offer pleasure and wonder, not only reduced speed and attrition.

## The old quay

**Possible placement:** a bypassed freight settlement on a Braided Road tributary. It has lost work to a newer route, not become uniformly poor because its people refused progress. The broad world's prosperity remains real and unevenly distributed; this local economic history needs continuity review if selected.

**Desired feeling:** afternoon light across shuttered workshops, water marks above an old loading stair, a public clock that still works, a tune people used to dance to. Ennui and mono no aware are the user's requested emotional register: boredom, tenderness and an ache for things passing. This is a mood brief, not a claim to reproduce a whole cultural tradition.

**People:** a repairer with a second job, a young person choosing departure, an older boat worker tired of being interviewed as “the last,” and a cheerful seller who knows the trade is slow. They can be witty, annoyed, ambitious and generous. Material poverty is neither moral purity nor an exotic aesthetic. Give residents present work and opinions beyond remembering better days.

**Personal adventure:** a musician wants to hear the old boat-house chorus once before the roof is dismantled for safe reuse. Find participants, choose an accessible place, retrieve a damaged song page and decide whether to keep the old arrangement or let a young performer change it. Optional help from Miret or Jori deepens the scene; neither is mandatory.

**Choices and consequence:** the building can be repaired modestly, repurposed, or dismantled with a surviving gathering place elsewhere. Money and labor must come from an identified source; one purchase cannot restore an entire regional economy. The meaningful success is an event and a practicable future, not erasing decline.

**Possible supernatural branch:** a retired service repeats the quay's best trading evening exactly, including a person who wanted to leave. The appeal must be sincere before its inability to accommodate tomorrow becomes frightening. This is an optional local containment case, not proof that all nostalgia is captivity. A version with no supernatural secret is equally valid and may be more moving.

**Reward and return:** a song, clothing repair, route service, friendship or habitat lead. A later visit shows someone's new job, an empty room with a letter, or music at a different landing. Change need not be failure.

## Spirits: appearances before ontology

The user asks whether battling spirits could fit. Compare **actual dead persons**, **accord echoes of repeated actions**, and **living beings mistaken for spirits**. These explanations are not automatically interchangeable. Selecting actual spirits would add major world and afterlife questions; it cannot happen only because a combat target needs a label.

A provisional encounter can be written from shared evidence: a repeated crossing gesture, an attack pattern interrupted by restoring a physical boundary, and a voice that changes when approached from another route. Folk belief may call it a ferryman's ghost. Evidence should eventually rule something out. Resolution can be survival, release, negotiation or combat; not every apparent spirit becomes collectible loot.

## Design review before adoption

Choose placement, material/climatic explanation, inhabitants, routes, sightline grammar, ordinary reward and one bounded complication. Ensure the same area is worthwhile without its horror secret. Evaluate silence as the absence of a known sound, fog as a navigable condition and slow travel as a choice with payoff. These briefs do not authorize map construction, new creatures, music or asset generation.
