# History and the Opening

This chronology is a working scaffold. Relative dates are approximate; no present calendar year or precise historical count is approved.

## Before the Opening

The lowlands were connected by a network of granary cities, river tolls, kinship obligations, and seasonal fairs. Their wealth depended on predictable floodwater, long grain roads, and the ability to make distant settlements honor debts. They could build impressive monuments while still exposing most people to disease, failed harvests, and exhausting labor.

The mountains were inhabited, traded through, and politically contested. Rocca Selva's predecessors supplied chestnuts, charcoal, wool, stone, and seasonal workers. Their relative autonomy was a bargain with lowland powers, not proof that the mountains had never participated in exploitation.

The old order should not be idealized as a lost golden age. Nor should it be reduced to misery merely to make the Largesse desirable. Its songs, irrigation works, friendships, and buildings were real achievements; its descendants still live among them.

## A catastrophe that did not happen everywhere at once

The “Great Opening” compresses perhaps a dozen very different years into one convenient phrase. In some districts it began with a spring that overflowed without rain. In others a migrating herd appeared beyond a road whose milestone still named a village that could no longer be reached. Several cities reported perfect harvests just as their trading partners faced ecological collapse.

Contradictory first-event stories survive. None is designated the true first moment in this draft. This allows future discoveries without making every historical witness foolish or dishonest.

## Three events still argued about

### The Winter of Full Tables

During a bitter winter, an early network of heated terraces and Loam Choir kitchens kept several valleys fed. The kitchens accepted refugees when hereditary storehouses would not. Modern Istravel invokes this episode whenever critics ask it to slow an expansion.

Surviving account books also record forced deliveries of timber and the disappearance of some villages' seed reserves into emergency redistribution. Modern Orthe cites those passages. Both things happened in the proposed history: the feeding was lifesaving, and the emergency created obligations that later governments abused.

Neither city's simplified story includes the keepers who argued that the kitchens should eventually belong to their users.

### The Twelve-Day Stillness

A lowland district once lost the coordinating effect that kept its water lifts, granaries, and public heating aligned. Each individual creature remained alive. The systems simply ceased cooperating on the expected schedule. Some quarters had enough independent stores; others did not. Rescue involved both mechanical engineers and experienced keepers.

Orthe's present reserve laws grow partly from this event. Istravel argues that the failure resulted from a brittle, poorly maintained arrangement whose lesson is better coordination. There is no author-confirmed evidence that the Stillness was punishment or deliberate attack.

The precise death toll, causes, and political manipulation afterward remain suitable subjects for future entries. They should not be filled with invented numbers merely to make the history look finished.

### The First Return Petition

A group of early merged people petitioned to resume separate legal identities. Some could separate physically; some could not; several no longer agreed about whether separation was desirable. City courts initially tried to solve every case through one ruling.

The resulting injustices helped create the Confluence's current practice of recording consent over time and appointing outside witnesses. They also explain why many merged people distrust civic promises of protection. A future plot can dispute a particular return without implying that changed lives are inherently tragedies.

## What the landscape remembers

- Toll towers stand beside dry channels while new water travels overhead.
- A Rocca Selva terrace wall incorporates stones from several abandoned road shrines. Residents know this and argue about which facing stones should be restored.
- The oldest district of Istravel occupies the shade beneath a former granary fort. The fort now houses a music school and a quarrelsome archive.
- Orthe's great cisterns include a maintained refuge quarter, with working kitchens and sleeping platforms. They are used for festivals between emergencies.
- A salt basin contains the foundations of a village on both shores, with matching house marks. A geological explanation remains possible; the duplication should not immediately announce a cosmic secret.

## Whose evidence counts

The wiki distinguishes several historical sources: public records, family objects, oral accounts, creature migration traces, and sites that can be revisited. No source class is perfectly reliable. An archive can preserve a convenient omission; an oral tradition can remember something its tellers misinterpret.

**Author-side constraint:** the history should supply independent evidence for major revelations. Do not make all uncertainty depend on documents changing offscreen. Contradictions become meaningful when the player can compare people, places, objects, and consequences.
