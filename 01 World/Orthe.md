# Orthe

**Role:** Istravel's major civic rival, advocating bounded use, continuity, and workable alternatives to dependence. This is one proposed interpretation of the user's opposing city-state; a more radically anti-urban movement remains an open direction.

## A city that shows how it stands

Orthe rises through a broad stone gorge in courts, bridges, counterweights, cisterns, and inhabited retaining walls. Water runs in visible channels beside stairs. Workshops display their lifting mechanisms behind open grilles. A public clock is maintained by an association whose members argue passionately about an error of a few seconds.

The city is beautiful in motion. Sunlight crosses rotating shade screens; fountains change with reservoir pressure; copper flags show which public lifts are operating. Market courts are noisy and colorful. The annual repair festival culminates in a procession of absurd machines that function, more or less, until they reach the upper square.

Orthe does use creatures and accords. Its argument is that no essential system should become impossible for its users to understand, refuse, or replace.

## Districts and institutions

**The Counterweight District** contains waterworks, mills, public lifts, schools of repair, and crowded worker housing. Glassgrazers assist with mineral filtration in bounded channels, while mechanical stages provide independent capacity. Neither system is adequate everywhere; operators constantly choose where to spend limited resources.

**The Hall of Measures** is a public complex for technical review, civic records, appeals, and permit hearings. Citizens can inspect the stated purpose, conditions, and exit procedure of major accord projects. This transparency matters. So do long waits, inaccessible language, and the advantage held by people who can afford representation.

**Threshold Court** receives travelers and people transitioning between bodily forms, care systems, or jurisdictions. Its advocates help newcomers obtain housing and legal standing. Its administrators also impose restrictions that can trap people in temporary arrangements.

**The Deep Reserve** is a network of granaries, seed libraries, cisterns, kitchens, and shelter rooms maintained for failures. Between emergencies, some halls host concerts and community meals so residents know the spaces before they need them.

**The Bounded Sanctuaries** are inhabited creature landscapes, not cages in every case. Access and expansion are regulated. Some populations thrive because the city preserved habitat; others might flourish more freely if officials allowed routes beyond surveyed boundaries.

## The promise and its uneven price

Orthe's guiding commitment is that a citizen should be able to leave a system without losing the means to live. Its reserve laws, public instruction, inspection rights, and repair culture have prevented genuine disasters. A household may reject a local accord connection and still receive basic services.

But alternatives cost money and labor. A wealthy neighborhood can fund excellent independent capacity; a poorer one may receive a technically adequate but exhausting substitute. A treatment approved in Istravel can spend years in Orthe's review process. Officials describe this as prudence; a person awaiting relief may experience it as abandonment.

The right of exit can also conflict with collective dependence. If several workers or creatures leave a water system at once, the city must keep other residents supplied. An emergency continuity order may protect the public and still violate the spirit of its own ideals. This tension supports a concrete dispute at the Counterweight District rather than a generic debate about freedom.

## Politics inside the walls

Orthe's civic chambers draw representation from districts, trades, and registered commons. These institutions make organized labor and local knowledge visible. They also disadvantage residents whose lives fit none of the established categories.

One coalition wants stricter limits and more independent reserves. Another argues that meaningful consent requires access to new possibilities, including merging. A third focuses on municipal inequality: the most urgent problem is who must carry buckets while the chambers debate.

Many people admire Istravel's doctors while distrusting its ambitions. Many Istravel residents admire Orthe's public records while laughing at its committees. Cross-city marriages, apprenticeships, and contracts keep the rivalry from becoming a simple division between populations.

## Dependencies

Orthe exports precision metalwork, durable containers, repair education, seed storage, and reserve engineering. It imports medicines, produce beyond its season, specialist keeper labor, salt, and selected accord services. Its bounded-use identity therefore relies partly on others taking risks and doing work beyond its walls.

An honest defender acknowledges this and pays for shared responsibility. A propagandist calls every import an exceptional necessity and every export proof of independence.

## Recurring people

[Magistrate Neris Vale](People%20of%20the%20Marches.md#magistrate-neris-vale) protects appeal rights and expects reasons to be written down. She also signs temporary restrictions that can outlast the situations that justified them.

[Iona Serr](People%20of%20the%20Marches.md#iona-serr) restores pumps and public lifts. She believes the city has confused a working fallback with the appearance of one. Her criticism comes from love and practical knowledge, not a desire to see everything fail.

[Corda-of-Reeds](People%20of%20the%20Marches.md#corda-of-reeds) works with Threshold Court on recognition of changed persons. Their visits reveal both the real protection offered by formal rights and the cruelty of forms with no truthful box to tick.

## A slow irregularity

**Observed proposal:** several separate appeal statements describe different events using exactly the same unusual phrase. A trained advocate notices because one writer does not normally use that dialect.

**Ordinary explanations:** a helpful clerk supplied wording; campaigners shared a template; an unofficial interpreter translated them. **Possible later explanation:** coordination is narrowing how a grievance becomes expressible before a hearing ever evaluates it.

The files have not all vanished. Some clerks remain attentive. The horror emerges through specific failures of a meaningful protection system, rather than proving that rights were always meaningless.
