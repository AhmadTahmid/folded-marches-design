# Latchrunner

**Draft nonrecruitable late encounter.** This develops the candidate in [Travel, Mapping and Pursuit](../../03%20Game%20Design/Travel%20Mapping%20and%20Pursuit.md): an animal used by a local sanctuary's retrieval accord, neither the Keeper's body nor the Largesse's source.

## A body that suddenly unfolds

A narrow seal-brown torso hangs between immensely long folding forelimbs and compact hindlegs. Pale jaw plates enclose blunt gripping ridges. A chalk chest seam appears when the shoulders lift. It seems too tired to carry its own reach—until its forelimbs unfold beyond the expected distance.

Its identifying action is **plant, gather, release**. One forefoot fixes the turn; shoulders rise and jaw closes before acceleration. Claw clicks, visible posture and encounter text share the warning. Straight bursts end in a sliding plant and vulnerable recovery, with no instant pivot or teleport.

## Life outside the chase

Wild Latchrunners hunt small marsh-edge grazers along root corridors and rest on raised dry ground. Young learn through object chases; adults carry food toward sheltered hollows. They need meat, water and recovery between bursts.

The Keeper's network has conditioned particular individuals to follow marked harnesses and recover moving cargo. During the confinement crisis it extends retrieval to people attempting departure. The animal responds to a bounded signal and reward; the species has no moral opinion about leaving home.

**Removing the mark works.** The runner targets the marked object, not an invisible identity attached to its wearer. Dropping a marked pack diverts its next committed burst into retrieving that pack. Removing a simple harness takes one escape-stage action; there is no surprise mark underneath it. Some evacuees depend on marked medical carrying frames: transfer the person onto the prepared unmarked stretcher at a sheltered stop, or take the marked frame through the service boundary. Both are valid rescue plans. If every remaining target has been detached or crossed, pursuit ends. Abandoned belongings can be recovered from the indicated pen later; original journal observations and the possibility of a good ending never disappear with a pack. This rewards a clever countermeasure rather than secretly defeating it.

## Learn it before escaping it

Three earlier observations establish the encounter: a trainer closes a gate after a failed burst; fresh scoring shows where a runner planted to turn; a retrieved pack stops at a clearly marked dry-service boundary. That boundary interrupts this retrieval accord. It is not proof that the species cannot tolerate dry ground.

The late escape reuses an explored service route. The player sees the marked target and intended lane before committing. Move behind a sound support, draw a burst into an empty lane, interrupt an eligible commitment or use the recovery to open a manual gate. Tools and allies provide alternatives to a particular recruited species. Ordinary combat eligibility and visible protections apply; “boss” grants no unexplained immunity.

Turn-based decision stages can present these bursts at frightening speed without a hidden input timer. A short real-time version remains the separate comparison in the pursuit page. This species brief does not migrate strategic combat to real time.

## Capture, rescue and relief

A successful latch seizes the marked harness or pack and pulls its bearer toward a visible holding pen. A readable release point lets an ally cut the tether or open a service gate. If separation completes, the next playable objective is physical rescue through the known circuit, with a usable companion or human helper. No unavoidable permanent death, save damage or lost roster follows one missed counter.

In the finale, opening the independent exit and breaking the retrieval circuit ends the pursuit. The runner can retreat alive; surviving the scene need not require adopting or killing it. Beyond the boundary it still breathes, rests and watches. Give the party room to recover before any further threat.

**Author truth:** the local conditioning and physical limits above belong to this draft. **Player evidence:** harness marks, turning scars, the trainer's gate and repeatable boundary tests. **In-world belief:** workers say it “brings back what the house is owed.” That belief does not establish ownership of people. Why this animal responds to this accord, and how its training is undone, can be investigated without explaining the origin of all Largesse.
