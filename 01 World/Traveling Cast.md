# People who keep crossing your road

These **four proposed recurring characters** join the eight [regional people](People%20of%20the%20Marches.md) and the protagonist's [family](../02%20Narrative/Family%20at%20Home.md). Their names, biographies, scenes, and futures remain drafts. The user's *Berserk* and *Attack on Titan* references inform sustained attachment, distinct ambitions, friction, and competence; no referenced character or plot is reproduced.

Vela wants credit and a good evening out. Jori wants an audience that paid to be there. Rovan wants a company that cannot discard him. Miret wants another opening night. Their lives continue between meetings with the protagonist.

## Vela Cotrin

**She/her. Twenty. Junior field naturalist and aspiring expedition keeper from the lowlands.**

Vela's family owns a modest but successful dye business supplying outdoor cloth. Her boots fit, her lenses arrived in a padded case, and she has somewhere to return if a posting fails. She works hard; those advantages still matter. She wants to publish a useful field guide under her own name and win expedition work on the strength of it. A family-sponsored printing arrangement makes that ambition possible while inviting justified questions about whose contribution will be remembered.

She wears a good coat badly: sleeves rolled unevenly, a bright sample ribbon marking a repaired pocket, lens cloth tucked into her collar. She likes public dancing, improbable hats, and arguing about creature names. Her pages contain excellent drawings beside furious crossed-out captions. She can be funny without meaning to be: she announces that she will now be quiet, then explains why silence is useful.

**First footprint.** At a Bell Road shelter she identifies hooked seeds as evidence of a Brackenstride feeding route. The protagonist can question the hoof marks heading the other way, or simply watch. Vela defends her diagram, follows the marks, and returns carrying a seed-covered pack strap: a traveler brought the seeds here. “That is a remarkably persuasive animal, considering it is luggage.” She corrects the page. Her substantial introduction follows in the White Orchard, mapping clean access around feeding Glassgrazers. She contributes sound observations, and they can work together or take separate routes.

**The relationship.** She assumes he can extend an interesting trip. Learning about his household changes her offer: she states pay and duration instead of calling unpaid work an opportunity. He may envy her equipment and enjoy her company. She envies his apparent ease with ordinary route work. Sharing a cramped shelter and comparing observations reveal how much effort each has concealed.

**A real failure, with continuity.** Her early attribution dispute occurs in [Under the White Branches](../02%20Narrative/Quests/Under%20the%20White%20Branches.md). If she agrees to corrected credit there, the published sheet honors it. A later sponsor asks her to simplify that credit; refusing gives her a harder test of growth. If an actual shared contribution remains uncredited, publication can expose the unresolved failure. With separate work or no contribution owed to the protagonist, a later dispute concerns Pell's independently supplied analysis instead. A deliberate omission is ordinary misconduct, not supernatural editing. Repair requires correcting the distributed claim; a private apology is insufficient. Do not manufacture an injury to the player for an identical scene.

**Other people.** Pell respects her observation and demands a reproducible record. Jori helps stage a field demonstration and expects a credit and fee. Rovan offers sponsor money with exclusive access conditions she finds attractive. Her family can dislike her business judgment without secretly owning every regional institution.

**Voice:** “I had a theory. It was excellent. Unfortunately, the creature has declined to use it.” Under pressure her sentences shorten; jokes stop covering an answer she owes.

**Possible growth:** shared authorship without diminished ambition, or a capable professional he meets cordially but no longer trusts with joint work. Romance and faction allegiance remain unselected.

## Jori Tern

**He/him. Twenty-seven. Freight handler, route substitute, and amateur comic performer.**

Jori earns his living moving difficult loads and covering other people's inconvenient shifts. He wants to assemble a small traveling show with two colleagues and pay everyone properly. His private notebook contains stage dimensions, ticket estimates, and jokes marked “never again.” He is not secretly a prince, a legendary fighter, or a man concealing tragedy behind every smile. He likes making a room laugh and wants a career that includes doing it.

He wears broad suspenders and ordinary work clothes, with an embroidered cuff he keeps protecting from the dirty part of each job. He can balance an awkward basket while arguing, then forget where he put the basket. His humor comes from precise imitation, physical timing, and narrating a failed plan as if it were an ambitious public ceremony. He stops when somebody asks him to; cruelty is not required to prove he is funny.

**First scene.** At the exchange, he rehearses an entrance speech to stacked baskets. A small creature knocks over his intended audience. “Difficult house. Very little interest in language.” Then a customer arrives and he becomes briskly professional: weight first, route second, price before lifting. If the protagonist takes Pell's extension in [First Paid Journey](../02%20Narrative/First%20Paid%20Journey.md), Jori can be the local help Tavio hires. If the protagonist helps Tavio, Jori is already booked elsewhere and later evaluates their packing. He is not a spare worker who could secretly make both appointments possible.

**Strength and mistake.** He sees the practical difficulty people are embarrassed to admit. He also volunteers before checking what he has promised elsewhere, then uses charm to soften the consequences. During his show's preparations, he misses a paid unloading appointment after agreeing to help a frightened performer. Tavio has to pay a replacement. Jori pays the difference over an agreed schedule and relinquishes the next desirable job; he cannot solve this by making another moving speech about friendship.

**The relationship.** He teaches the protagonist to quote a job without apologizing; the younger man returns that lesson when Jori asks for unpaid rehearsal help. They have evenings trying on a ridiculous costume, disagreeing about music, or failing to teach a companion a harmless stage cue. Jori sometimes wants to discuss himself.

**Other people.** Tavio is a valued employer whose overpromising Jori recognizes too late as familiar. Vela lends a painted backdrop to the show and initially gives too many notes. Miret coaches his voice and refuses to let their affection replace a rehearsal schedule. Ada can receive a fairly priced commission for weatherproof stage coverings; this is ordinary work, not charity arranged behind her back.

**Voice:** “I can carry it, or I can agree that it was sensibly packed. Those are separate prices.”

**Possible growth:** a smaller show that actually pays its workers, an honest return to freight while rebuilding it, or a strained partnership after another broken commitment. Warmth does not make him endlessly available, and neither failure nor humor requires his death.

## Rovan Cale

**He/him. Thirty-three. Commercial retrieval captain and prospective employer.**

Rovan takes contracts to retrieve stranded freight and expensive equipment from routes ordinary carriers cannot safely use. He has built a capable crew but rents much of its equipment and depends on short commissions. He wants permanent ownership and wages that continue between jobs. He likes competitive keeper matches, buying unusually sweet preserves, and teaching a nervous beginner one thing well. His crew's affection is credible; it does not excuse what he later chooses.

His recognizable object is a narrow board displaying crew assignments, altered in chalk before every departure. Work clothes are well maintained, with replacement pieces that do not match. He takes the time to move a strap off a tired creature's irritated shoulder while continuing a difficult negotiation. His physical presence comes from certainty and attention, not an unexplained reputation that everyone must recite.

**First scene.** In a public exercise he lets an opponent spend a protection too early, changes his creature formation, and wins the objective. He explains the mistake without humiliating the learner. His subsequent offer of a paid trial includes usable travel terms; declining does not insult him. His team follows the same combat rules as the player's.

**The conflict he creates.** Later, a client offers enough money for him to buy equipment if he obtains exclusive retrieval access to a newly usable ledge. Pell's survey establishes that the approach is a maintained public route. Rovan knows the claim is sound. He nevertheless removes portable grips his crew installed, places guards at the shared approach, and falsely tells other workers that Pell agreed to a temporary closure. His crew retains its own safe access; other carriers lose work and must take a longer route. Nobody needs to fall to make this an abuse.

He is responsible for the lie. Debt supplies motive, not exoneration. The confrontation can involve evidence, a rival safe approach, a withdrawal agreement, or a clearly announced creature contest over the disputed equipment under local mediation. A battle cannot magically confer lawful ownership. The player need not accept a duel or work for him to challenge the obstruction. His capacity to organize, anticipate routes, and persuade workers makes sustained opposition credible.

**Other people.** Tavio previously hired him for a recovery and knows exactly how useful he is. Pell refuses to let old professional respect become consent. Vela is offered access to rare observations in exchange for postponing publication of the shared approach. Jori refuses a lucrative handling shift once he learns what is being excluded; Rovan remembers the refusal and can still honestly recommend Jori's work.

**Voice:** “Hear the whole offer. Then hate it.” When caught lying, he initially argues about consequences instead of answering the question.

**Possible change:** restitution requires restored access, payment for demonstrable losses, and relinquishing the exclusive claim. He might accept those costs and remain difficult, or double down and become a recurring human adversary. Neither a rescue nor a tragic backstory purchases automatic forgiveness. No secret connection to the protagonist's father or cosmic source is proposed.

## Miret Sool

**She/her. Forty-six. Traveling vocal coach; formerly a caller on difficult retrieval expeditions.**

Miret once coordinated crews where water, echo, and moving terrain made shouted directions unreliable. She now trains performers and public speakers, repairing her income between small productions. She wants to revive a neglected comic opera because she loves its music and thinks the ending is funnier than anyone performs it. She is carrying the revised final scene herself and will argue about it over breakfast.

**Light first footprint.** At the mountain fair she gets three embarrassed apprentices to imitate a gale. They succeed just as a real gust steals her lesson sheet. She applauds the weather for its timing. A player who stops can help retrieve the pages or hear the exercise; someone who misses it can later meet her at Jori's rehearsal. The scene provides a person, not an ominous prophecy or a compulsory collectible.

Miret wears a loose neck wrap, pockets rehearsal pencils, and moves cheap chairs with the determination of someone rearranging an entire theatre. She is attentive to breath and fatigue without being a physician. Senna once helped her recover from an ordinary work-related injury; Miret owes her a promised performance, not secret knowledge of the garden's danger.

**The later importance.** When an expedition needs signals that do not rely on spoken reassurance, Pell or Jori can recommend her. She designs, demonstrates, and tests rope and touch signals with the group. During the proposed later ordeal, hearing a calm voice is insufficient evidence that its owner can stop or consent. Miret notices a missed agreed signal because the group rehearsed it. Other trained participants can do the same if she is absent; the plot must not require an optional acquaintance to unlock the only escape.

**Her failure and stake.** She rewrites a returning student's interview for a printed program, turning an uncertain answer into confident praise without asking. The student objects. Miret must retain the original written answer and obtain agreement about what may be published. Her instinct to rescue a performance can become overbearing care. Later she may be tempted to speak for an exhausted colleague when the honest answer is to wait.

**Voice:** “From the back. If you can hear me, stop shouting yes.” Her tenderness is practical: a chair placed near the exit, a rehearsal ended before someone has to beg.

Her eventual performance, expedition involvement, and closer friendships remain open. She has a career to return to after helping others; surviving the horror should leave room for an opening night.

## Author truth, evidence, and open decisions

The biographies and failures above are proposed author facts for a coherent draft. Characters' claims about their motives can be incomplete; the wiki must not quietly replace an authored action with a supernatural explanation. Show correction notices, missed work, the physical route obstruction, and rehearsed signals as player evidence. Let other witnesses disagree about interpretation.

The exact cast size, names, optional participation, long-term loyalties, romance possibilities, and outcomes remain open. The protagonist's family circumstances are established; these people neither explain his father's absence nor make him secretly exceptional. Their narrative presence does not create human combat turns or require a party larger than five creatures.
