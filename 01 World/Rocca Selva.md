# Rocca Selva

**Role:** mountain home; emotional anchor; politically independent community with real dependencies. **Status:** proposed place, consistent with the user's Apennine village requirement. The protagonist is about eighteen or nineteen, with a mother and three younger siblings he has already supported; presentation and individual family details remain open.

**Design v0.1 working draft:** the spatial brief below now supplies the town's intended form and ordinary play. [Regional Map v0.1](Regional%20Map%20v0.1.md) owns its current route relationships. This is wiki design, unconstrained by Bellwater's square, assets or runtime; no layout, character art or new implementation is approved by this page.

## First arrival, or first departure

Rocca Selva climbs around a limestone outcrop above an old chestnut forest. Terraces form a soft staircase below its houses. Laundry moves against dark cypress-like trees; slate gutters carry water into a common cistern; bells sound through the ridges in patterns older than the present bell tower.

In summer the village smells of stone dust, cut herbs, frying dough, and animal bedding. A Thimbleback is probably blocking a drain with an exceptionally valuable collection of nuts. Someone has put a chair in the road because repairing it is easier when every passerby offers advice.

The village is not untouched by the Largesse. It has a warm bathhouse, helpful creatures, and a few expanded storerooms. It has chosen smaller and more individually understood arrangements, partly through principle and partly because it cannot afford the lowlands' grander systems.

## Places people use

**Bell Court** is the shared open space beside the tower, with a market awning, a schoolroom, and the broad steps where public arguments become entertainment. Bells communicate weather, work parties, emergencies, and the opening of seasonal paths. They are civic tools before they are symbols.

**The Chestnut Steps** are cultivated terraces extending into the Staired Wood. Several families own plots, while paths and drainage channels remain common responsibilities. Harvest involves ladders, baskets, children allowed to help badly, and Thimblebacks negotiating access to fallen nuts by persistent behavior.

**The common cistern** supplies households through stone runnels. Its purity depends partly on ordinary maintenance and partly on a modest local ecology upstream. A proposed lowland upgrade offers greater capacity but requires more coordinated oversight. The village's existing system has real weaknesses; rejecting an upgrade should not solve them by magic.

**The pass house** stores stretchers, grain, spare bells, bedding, and weather gear for travelers. No one owns its emergency beds. Keeping them stocked is a recurring source of labor and resentment.

**The warm rooms** combine a bathhouse, laundry, and winter gathering hall. Embermoles help heat them. Their keeper still cuts emergency fuel and grumbles when visitors assume the place runs itself.

## Spatial brief v0.1: a town wrapped around its work

Three uneven contours wrap the limestone shoulder. Houses share walls but face different landings; a roof becomes a neighbour's garden edge. Bell Court offers orientation, while hanging sheets, glimpsed sky and flowing water reveal other routes in pieces. There is no single square from which everything is equally accessible.

A carrying incline winds from the eastern trade gate to the pass house, crossing shorter stairs. Good conversations and views belong on this accessible route too. Chestnuts descend west into the Staired Wood; the saddle opens north toward Cloud Loom, the eastern road toward White Orchard. Protected springs feed covered drinking channels; wash water reaches separate settling beds below the warm rooms. No river conveniently crosses the hilltop.

The lower loop is **home → Gutter Lane → Bell Court → Carrier Yard → warm rooms → home**. The upper loop is **Bell Court → Cistern Crown → Rope Walk → pass house → Bell Court**. Threshold Groves adds a wooded return. Shortcuts change what the player sees, not whether essential services can be reached.

### Moving because the place invites it

The user's Lanternfall reference informs **smooth walking, jumping and rolling as a design aspiration**, not system adoption or implementation evidence. Roots and terrace lips offer forgiving jumps; a missed hop lands on a lower path. Rolling belongs on roomy descents, without scattering townspeople. Stairs need convincing contact and uninterrupted steering. The carrying incline reaches every important destination without mandatory jumps, mounts or stamina chores. Animation and framing should make an ordinary turn under an arch satisfying.

### Morning, evening, and a small hidden room

At dawn: a shutter lifted, grain poured, a dragging bench. Home lies in blue shade while the pass-house porch catches sun. Bread scent leads past the warm rooms; bright cloth unrolls in Carrier Yard. Evening brings repairs indoors, bathing voices through high windows and warm light on upper rock. The same loop offers different company. Closed workbenches leave notes rather than frustrating service deadlines.

**Discoverable secret:** behind Gutter Lane's drying screen, a charcoal loft opens onto a roof garden Ada and friends used as teenagers. Initials, a terrible landscape sketch and a surviving bench invite a household conversation. There is no father's clue or compulsory supernatural explanation.

### Two short town quests

**The longest table.** Ada wants covered space for a large harness repair; the schoolroom's folding table is available, but residents assumed it belonged to the fair committee all week. The player hears both users, checks the actual schedule with Lina, and helps choose a route for the folded sections. The stairs are awkward; Carrier Yard's incline offers room and a conversation with a returning worker. A reversible arrangement shares the space instead of declaring one group selfish. Reward: Ada can finish a paid job, Lina receives credit for the schedule, and the workroom looks changed on return. This is a brief spatial/social problem, not recurring furniture labour.

**The borrowed shade.** Miret's apprentices have moved festival cloth from Rope Walk to a sunny ledge; a Kitefin keeps dragging it beneath the rail. Follow three small signs—warm updraught, damp sheltered stone, dropped nesting fibres—before deciding the animal is stealing. Retie the practice cloth on existing pegs, leaving one sheltered strip unoccupied. A companion may help indicate the wind, but ribbons and dust provide the same evidence. Reward: a short comic rehearsal, a keeper's introduction and a reliable place to revisit the individual. No automatic capture or compulsory egg removal follows.

### The first walk

Begin with the household packing exchange. Notice one changed chalk rule outside; follow Gutter Lane past the fair preparations to Tavio and Pell in Carrier Yard. The route board presents both cities. Take the warm-room loop home, meet a possible partner or inspect the road before accepting a placement. [Family at Home](../02%20Narrative/Family%20at%20Home.md) retains the support agreement without a hidden household countdown.

## How independence works

Rocca Selva belongs to a small highland commons charter recognized by both major cities. The village maintains pass shelters, keeps certain routes open, and protects breeding and watershed sites that lowland infrastructure depends on. In return it retains local arbitration, limited taxation powers, and the right to refuse permanent civic installations on common land.

Recognition is practical, historical, and continually negotiated. Neither city wants the other to control the central high routes. Neither can cheaply replace mountain maintenance knowledge. This makes independence plausible without making the village militarily invincible or magically self-sufficient.

The charter does not make every resident equal. Families with land, strong lowland relationships, or time to attend meetings exert more influence. Seasonal workers and people who return after years away can find that “we all decided” did not include them.

## What comes in, what goes out

Chestnuts, wool, dried herbs, stonework, creature care, and route labor earn income. A few people take lowland seasonal work. Iron tools, salt, paper, many medicines, and some grain must be imported. During a bad season the village needs credit; during a blocked winter it needs stores.

The community therefore cannot simply sever trade when politics become uncomfortable. Its freedom is the ability to negotiate among relationships, maintain alternatives, and make refusal possible at a known cost.

## A village with several futures

- **Connectors** want reliable lowland medicine, training, and safe travel. They do not necessarily want annexation. Some remember a death that better access could have prevented.
- **Commons defenders** want decisions to remain local and infrastructure understandable. They can overlook how much unpaid care supports their preferred way of life.
- **Departing and returning residents** want room to become different people without losing their home. Their dispute may concern housing and family expectations rather than the fate of humanity.
- **Young keepers** want access to creatures and knowledge beyond inherited practice. Some traditional precautions are wise; some are local habits hardened into authority.

These are tendencies, not permanent factions with matching uniforms.

## People and relationships

[Mara Vezzi](People%20of%20the%20Marches.md#mara-vezzi) tends the bells, coordinates some pass work, and has known the protagonist for years. She is a proposed former teacher, employer, or trusted neighbor, separate from his mother in the current household draft.

[Tavio Rusk](People%20of%20the%20Marches.md#tavio-rusk) arrives with goods, invitations, and a suspiciously flexible understanding of when he promised to arrive. His trade connects the village to both cities; he cannot protect everyone from prices changing elsewhere.

[Pell Arven](People%20of%20the%20Marches.md#pell-arven) has surveyed the upper paths. Their disagreement with Mara concerns recent changes in a route log, initially explainable by a damaged notebook and a difficult season.

Ordinary residents should also retain their own concerns: a shared oven dispute, an apprenticeship, a wedding between people from rival city districts, an unreliable mule, a new roof. These are future hooks, not unnamed people waiting only to be frightened.

## The first adventure belongs to daily life

The proposed opening brings preparations for the seasonal reopening of the roads: carry supplies, help a creature, learn a familiar landscape, attend a fair, and earn a first paid departure. The protagonist wants better earnings for his mother and three younger siblings, and a life beyond the role the village already knows him in. Paid road stewardship is a proposed first step. [Family at Home](../02%20Narrative/Family%20at%20Home.md) gives departure a practical agreement; travel broadens the responsibilities he already knows. [Personal Story](../02%20Narrative/Personal%20Story.md) develops the arc without requiring the village to burn or a loved one to be abducted.

No creature species is designated a compulsory starter here. Thimbleback, Kitefin, and Bellwether offer different relationships to the landscape; final selection belongs to game and narrative design.

## Subtle irregularities

**Observable proposal:** one pass marker appears in two handwritten inventories under slightly different descriptions. **Local explanations:** a stone was replaced, someone copied an old list, or memory has become less reliable. **Author question:** whether this connects to wider accord coordination or remains a useful ordinary discrepancy.

Most village rituals should retain their ordinary meaning after this mystery develops. The bells can remain bells. A shared meal can remain a shared meal. The amount of comfort that survives later horror is explicitly undecided at the user's request.
